Shot in Black and White

17 films, ranked on my own star ratings by Arwin Edward Bagaslao. Updated 2026-09-04.

Watched films whose entire runtime is black and white. No colour anywhere, not a prologue, not an epilogue, not one insert. Films that go partly monochrome are deliberately left out. Ordered by my rating. ...plus 7 more. View the full list

  1. 1
    Seven Samurai (1954)
    ★★★★★

    There are silent stretches in this film where the performances really shine.

  2. 2
    12 Angry Men (1957)
    ★★★★★

    What immediately pulled me in were the long takes.

  3. 3
    Roman Holiday (1953)
    ★★★★½

    Wow! Audrey Hepburn is so gorgeous! Love at first sight agad.

  4. 4
    Dr. Strangelove or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bomb (1964)
    ★★★★½

    What unsettles me most is how calmly it presents something so catastrophic, as if total destruction can be triggered by routine, ego, and blind protocol.

  5. 5
    Polytechnique (2009)
    ★★★★½

    A Denis Villeneuve film where I can really feel the tension through the characters’ motivations and the musical score.

  6. 6
    Following (1998)
    ★★★★

    This is a rewatch for me because I did not fully get it the first time.

  7. 7
    Paths of Glory (1957)
    ★★★½

    The scale of no man’s land is great, but the sound feels blown out and harsh at times.

  8. 8
    Harakiri (1962)
    ★★★½

    It feels like something pulled straight from an old album.

  9. 9
    Citizen Kane (1941)
    ★★★

    It feels like a reinventing the wheel kind of entrepreneur story, someone building power by acquiring and outmaneuvering competitors.

  10. 10
    The Killing (1956)
    ★★★

    Citizen Kane’s fragmented, perspective driven storytelling meets Ocean’s Eleven’s meticulously planned ensemble heist.

  11. 11
    To Kill a Mockingbird (1962)
    ★★★

    What I admire most here is the film’s moral clarity.

  12. 12
    Pi (1998)
    ★★★
  13. 13
    The Lighthouse (2019)
    ★★★

    The film immediately stands out through its immersive sound and production design, where the environment feels alive and oppressive rather than just background.

  14. 14
    Casablanca (1942)
    ★★

    The lighting really reminded me of 12 Angry Men.

  15. 15
    Ivan's Childhood (1962)
    ★★

    Ivan’s childhood is beautifully shot. The cinematography really stands out.

  16. 16
    Guy and Madeline on a Park Bench (2009)
    ★★

    The trumpet and tap dance scene immediately got my attention.

  17. 17
    La Haine (1995)
    ★½

    Bunch of youths with nothing to do but disrupt peace.
