No Country for Old Men 2007

★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-11-11
The film starts off like a painting: a vast desert drenched in silence and gold. Roger Deakins’ cinematography is so crisp it almost feels still, each frame heavy with quiet tension. The brown tinted daylight and deep blue nights give it that Western dusk tone. There are a lot of silhouette shots that I like, and dun pa lang, alam ko na agad why this is considered a cinematic masterpiece. I can really feel the DOP’s DNA, dahil siya rin yung sa Sicario. May action na agad sa simula pa lang. When Llewelyn Moss (Josh Brolin) runs from the dog across the river, it feels like The Revenant; every movement is survival. The sound design here is pure brilliance. The hissing of Anton Chigurh’s oxygen tank, the suitcase sliding inside an air duct, each sound replaces the score. The Coens remove music entirely letting dread become the soundtrack. The silence builds up to an inevitable standoff. Javier Bardem is a quiet menace. Anton isn’t just a killer, he’s inevitability personified. The calm voice, the still eyes, the random philosophy of chance. The coin toss scene is pure dread. You can’t predict him because he doesn’t feel, he’s almost robotic. Ang ganda ng light na galing sa natumbang lamp. Yung direction ng ilaw, papunta from below up to his face, looks almost a horror film shot. This could have been an easy five star film, but the second half felt anticlimactic. The rising tension and “action” suddenly stop, leaving Sheriff Bell (Tommy Lee Jones) alone with the aftermath. Moss dying off screen got me thinking there would be a flashback or at least some sort of explanation, but there’s none. It’s just a callback to the driver who told him that hitchhiking like that is very dangerous. Meaning, him finding the money out of nowhere shows he was always an outsider to the chaos. A man who “hitchhiked” his way into this whole mess. It feels like the entire story shifts to the POV of Bell, making it clearer how the world has become too chaotic for men like him. He retires while the villain remains on the loose, and suddenly, the line “You can’t stop what’s coming” really makes sense.
