The Texas Chain Saw Massacre (1974) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-01
654 reviews · 1,580 films logged · 175,559 words
Barely any blood. Somehow still terrifying as fuck. The presentation is simple. Nothing too flashy with the camera. It is just cramped and intimate with them inside the minivan. Like a calm before the storm. I love the wide shots and low angle shots too! I think I like it when the filmmaking is straightforward. The camera does not constantly call attention to itself. It just puts me inside the situation. Even cinematographer Daniel Pearl described their approach as generally simple compositions and basic lighting, which helped create that almost documentary look. There is a contrast between inside and outside the house. Outside, it is bright and sunny, like it is safe. But inside the house, it is dark, and the framing feels tighter, giving this sense of no escape. The farmhouse is really where the movie changes from an uneasy road movie into a full nightmare. There are no over the top techniques to create dread, horror, suspense, mystery, or thrill. Even the gore! There is not much blood spilled compared with modern slasher films. But it is still incredibly effective up until this day. I think a huge part of why it still works is control. The film does not need to keep showing me graphic wounds because my imagination is already doing the work. The violence also comes suddenly and unpredictably, so I never feel prepared for what happens next. Everything feels dirty, physical, and believable instead of designed around elaborate kills. It is so controlled and textured. I love it. I am legitimately scared for the characters. And that texture is real. The film was shot on 16mm, which contributes to that rough, documentary like image. That is probably why it visually reminds me of those episodes of Are You Afraid of the Dark? (1990–2000). That grainy, imperfect image, practical looking locations, darkness, and simple lighting have that same kind of visual horror texture that still brings back childhood trauma for me. The sound design is expertly crafted to create this sense of impending death breathing down my neck. There is no traditional score needed to tell me when to be scared. Just the chainsaw is enough to make my heart pound. The sound itself becomes the threat. The masterful editing also helps the pacing. Not too many cuts, just enough to create tension. And it lets scenes stay uncut when necessary for the emotions. The acting was incredible too. Marilyn Burns is easily the best thing here. Her screaming, panic, physical exhaustion, and desperation make the terror believable. By the time she is running for her life, she looks completely drained, like I am watching someone genuinely trying to survive rather than an actress performing a horror scene. The crazy family is believable too. That is another reason the film scares me. They do not feel like movie villains. They feel like people I may accidentally stumbled across and immediately wish you had never met. The whole escape sequence was so fucking intense. Actually, the whole thing is intense. Haha! Verdict: Absolute Cinema
