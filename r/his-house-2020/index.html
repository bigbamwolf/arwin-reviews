His House (2020) review

★½
Rated 1.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-01
648 reviews · 1,574 films logged · 172,352 words
War trauma wearing a haunted house costume. I read this more as a movie about war PTSD, racism, isolation, and adjusting to a new country. The whole film feels like war trauma following them into what is supposed to be a fresh start. They escaped the physical danger, but they clearly did not escape everything they carried with them. The isolation works both ways. They are isolated as refugees in a country where they barely know anyone, and they are also physically isolated inside this house. That combination makes the house feel less like a new beginning and more like another place they are trapped in. The racism also comes through in the way the neighbors treat them. It constantly reminds them that even after escaping one place, they are still outsiders in another. I think the film portrays these struggles convincingly, but I never really felt emotionally connected to the characters. I understood what they were going through, but I wasn't particularly invested in their story. Neither the supernatural scenes frightened me, nor was I as interested in them as I was in the refugee story itself. The horror felt pretty generic to me. I appreciate the ideas behind the film, but nothing about the actual viewing experience stood out enough to become memorable. Nothing in the filmmaking or horror itself really stuck with me afterward. Verdict: Hollow Watched on Netflix.
