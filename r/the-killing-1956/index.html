The Killing 1956

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-09-04
Citizen Kane’s fragmented, perspective driven storytelling meets Ocean’s Eleven’s meticulously planned ensemble heist. The old school black and white visual language: dramatic lighting, deep shadows, carefully staged blocking, and compositions where the characters are positioned very deliberately inside the frame. It has that look where even simple conversations feel visually constructed. The actual structure of the story: assembling different people with specific roles, meticulously planning the robbery, watching the operation unfold from multiple angles, and seeing whether all those moving pieces actually work together. It has that familiar pleasure of watching a heist being planned step by step. But I think Kubrick is still a hit or miss for me I was not fully locked in until the actual heist finally started and the movie became much more engaging. The ending genuinely shocked me. After all that planning and precision, the way it ended is absurdly funny. It is one of those endings where you just sit there thinking, “You’ve got to be kidding me.” Lesson learned: research about airline policies.
