Eddington 2025

★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-10-17
At first, di ko magets kung saan papunta. Emma Watson was weird, the mom was weirder, and Joaquin was just stubborn about that mask issue. Pero habang tumatagal, narealize ko na sila mismo ay produkto ng propaganda, parang mga taong nabrainwash ng belief system na ayaw nilang bitawan kahit halatang mali na. Nahook lang ako nung inassassinate ni Joaquin si Pedro. Ang galing kasi ginamit pa niya yung current events to twist the narrative and pin it on someone else. Ang sugoi moment talaga was that exact scene tahimik lang, calm before the storm, tapos boom. Too late for your brain to process. Nakakatakot. Joaquin here feels like Joker living inside Jeff Bridges’ body, controlled pero may halong insanity sa loob. Visually, it felt like One Battle After Another meets Breaking Bad dry desert tones, flat grading, grounded lensing. Walang standout shot except the chaotic gunfight scene, where his asthma-breaths and the echoing gunshots sold that “no survival” feeling. Sound design and score were forgettable, but that silence during the assassination sequence worked well that tension before the blast hits. Production design looked real, though. The pandemic paranoia, isolation, and collapsing sanity of people all believable. Thematically, malinaw: people convinced they’re right even when they’re destroying others. Liberal, conservative, activist, extremist lahat ganun. In their mind, they’re the hero. Kaya kahit walang malakas na emotional pull, nagtagumpay siya in showing how both sides use ideology as armor. Emma Watson felt underused stoic, random outburst, walang resonance. Joaquin carried the whole film. He’s unsettling, manipulative, unhinged. That’s both the strength and weakness of the film. Masyadong dependent sa kanya. Cinematography is serviceable, not spectacular. It’s not like The Creator or Dune. More like TV level realism with one big IMAX-worthy moment. Kung sa sound, wala rin masyadong maalala, but that one “calm before chaos” scene earns its place. Ultimately, Eddington works more as a psychological allegory than a visual spectacle. It’s a film about delusion, moral confusion, and how people twist truth to fit belief. Pero emotionally? Meh. It irritated me more than it moved me which, maybe, was the point.
