Godzilla Minus Zero (2026) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-05
Action, Adventure, Drama · Directed by Takashi Yamazaki · 119 min
649 reviews · 1,575 films logged · 173,102 words
House of the Dragon meets Top Gun: Maverick, but make it kaiju. The opening feels like Hope (2026), with its ground level cinematography and this looming threat towering over everyone. It really makes the humans feel tiny while also giving that feeling of civilians being trapped in chaos they cannot control. I love this kind of scale because I am experiencing the monster from the human perspective. The IMAX cinema was literally shaking. I could feel the vibrations and even the seats swaying during the roars, impacts, and Godzilla’s footsteps. I honestly did not know if someone was moving their chair and causing it or if it was just placebo, but it added so much to the immersion. There were moments when I could almost feel the weight of the monsters through the theater. The score!!! OMG. Great ominous themes. The music makes the monsters feel like approaching disasters. The CGI of the planes is not that polished, though. They look obviously digital, especially compared with the stronger live action photography and production design. This is still centered on human emotion, with Godzilla and Ghidorah on the side. Fear, guilt, desperation, responsibility, anger, hope, and unwavering spirit. The kaiju are massive, but the story still keeps returning to people dealing with what war and destruction left behind. I still prefer black and white and believe it would make this feel even more visceral, especially with all the destruction, smoke, fire, and monster imagery. I already preferred Godzilla Minus One (2023) in black and white because the lack of color made Godzilla’s silhouette and scale feel more imposing. I think the same treatment would work beautifully here. The trailer did not entice me to watch this, but I was with my accountant near the cinema, so we decided to give it a try. And I can say that watching the trailer on a TV or laptop is very different from actually experiencing the film inside a cinema. I am glad I gave it a try, especially as someone who is not even a Godzilla fan. This is one of those movies where the theatrical experience becomes part of the film itself. The production design is great, especially given that this is a period piece. It feels like watching House of the Dragon (2022) crossed with Top Gun: Maverick (2022). Ghidorah gives me the dragon side, especially whenever those huge wings and multiple heads fill the frame. But this handles the aerial mission side more like Top Gun, with pilots, planning, sacrifice, and people trying to solve an impossible problem from the cockpit. The pacing is slow, but the mission came much faster than in Godzilla Minus One (2023), so I stayed more engaged. Minus One took more time patiently building each character before the operation. Here, the movie gets to the mission planning earlier even while keeping the human drama. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The slow-mo scenes hit so hard, especially the dream sequence of Godzilla attacking the USA. The scale, destruction, and slowed down imagery made that sequence feel almost apocalyptic. I was surprised by Noriko’s mutation. I did not know that her exposure to Godzilla had caused her to develop Godzilla like scales and affect her eye. I swear there were a couple of times when I thought Ghidorah was going to pop out of the screen. IMAX really sells his scale. But the fight would have been easier if they had just hired Tom Cruise. Kidding!!! I love the idea of using rods and weather to defeat Ghidorah. I like that they are actually using science and planning instead of just relying on Godzilla to punch harder. The human characters still feel useful even when they are standing beside creatures that completely dwarf them. But I have seen this conflict before in Godzilla: King of the Monsters (2019), so it feels kind of repetitive. That movie already gave me the big Godzilla versus Ghidorah showdown. This one has a very different period setting and emotional treatment, but the basic monster conflict still feels familiar. It also feels like another, “Oh, we need Godzilla. Oh, now we don’t.” He is a villain, then suddenly we need him as the hero again. I do not necessarily hate it. I have just seen this cycle enough times that the shift no longer surprises me. I love the character development of the Hiroshima survivor finally giving up his revenge against America. His anger makes sense considering what happened to his family, but I like that the film does not let that revenge become his final destination. There is a lot of CGI, though. I just wish there were more practical effects, especially during the fire and destruction sequences. Whenever buildings are collapsing and entire environments are burning, I miss seeing more real fire, smoke, debris, and physical destruction mixed into the digital work. I’m glad I experienced this in IMAX because the cinematography, score, and sheer scale were incredible, but the uneven pacing, limited emotional impact, and familiar Godzilla versus Ghidorah conflict made the film itself less memorable than the theatrical experience. Once was enough for me. Verdict: Watchable Watched at SM Megamall IMAX
