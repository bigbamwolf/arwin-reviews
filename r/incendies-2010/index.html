Incendies 2010

★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-10-30
Slowburn talaga yung pacing. The story jumps between POVs and locations, intercutting past and present: a daughter tracing her mother’s life to fulfill her dying wish and find her brother. The structure alone already feels like a puzzle you’re not meant to solve right away. Color grading is almost washed, and lit mostly with light. The silhouette shots during sunset are beautiful, similar to those in Sicario, with that Villeneuve signature contrast between beauty and brutality. The bus scene was crazy! Brutal, senseless, and painful to watch. You start asking the same question the film does: why are they fighting in the name of religion? The tragedy hits harder when you see how children are always the ones who lose the most in war. WTF. The film hints at deep religious critique: showing how faith, when weaponized, becomes justification for hate. You can feel the roughness of war through every frame: cracked walls, and dusty streets. The environment looks lived in, not staged. Every time the camera tracks a person or an object, alam mong may significance katulad ng briefcase and yung foot steps nung rap*st. Yung scene noong tatakas si Marwal nung dalaga pa sya para hanapin yung anak nya, the narrow path she walks through feels symbolic, showing how difficult and unlikely her journey is. Ang ganda rin ng transition after childbirth, from the darkness to the tunnel’s light, signifying Simon’s birth and emergence from darkness into a harsh world. Then the reveal hits: they weren’t looking for a missing brother… they were twins born out of r*pe. Para ka talagang mabubuhusan ng malamig na tubig with that kind of revelation. The transition to the pool scene, diving underwater, captures that wave of realization and anger. You feel submerged in their pain. That discovery explains why their mother was so broken. She carried that pain all along, in silence. Villeneuve uses visual metaphor like poetry. The twist is tragic. Parang Oldboy twist, horrifying yet strangely poetic. I won’t spoil it further, hoping you’ll experience your own level of shock. Maybe Villeneuve could’ve trimmed some scenes to tighten the pacing.
