Status: Rejected (2026) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-18
668 reviews · 1,593 films logged · 181,283 words
A late life love story where being seen becomes both the dream and the danger. Been eyeing this since Cinemalaya, but either there were not enough showtimes, I had schedule conflicts, or there were not enough tickets available. I am glad I finally got to watch it. I wanted to see this because I felt like I would enjoy a story about experiencing life again at an old age. Rediscovering romance, confidence, and simply feeling alive and wanted again. And I really like that this Filipino film gives an older woman that space. Virginia is not treated simply as someone’s mother or grandmother. She still wants romance, attention, adventure, and a life that feels like her own. It is fun seeing Virginia feel the butterflies again. I like the static TV like graphics with shapes representing the fantasy and physical distance between them. It feels like a digital replacement for something she cannot physically experience. I love Tessie and Virginia’s friendship too. The loyalty, honesty, humor, and companionship between them give the film warmth outside the romance. The comedy and sadness are nicely balanced. It can make Virginia’s excitement funny without turning her loneliness into the joke. At its core, this is about the desperation to be loved and appreciated. When somebody has gone so long without feeling wanted, even obvious red flags can start looking negotiable if the alternative is going back to feeling invisible. The film treats that desperation compassionately while still showing how easily loneliness can be exploited. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I predicted the scam pretty early on, but another part of me was still invested in watching how far Virginia would allow herself to believe it. That is what makes it sad. She is not stupid. She is vulnerable. She desperately wants this one beautiful thing to be real, and someone knows exactly how to use that against her. The ending is bittersweet. The tragedy eventually brings her closer to her daughter, and I like that something real comes out of something fake. But I honestly wish the film went much deeper into that mother daughter relationship. After spending so much time watching Virginia chase connection somewhere else, I wanted more time seeing what rebuilding the connection at home actually looked like. The film ends with something hopeful, but I wanted the emotional repair to hurt a little more before it healed. Still, I like that a story about a romance scam ultimately became a story about an older woman wanting to be seen again. Watched at SM Aura Director's Club 2
