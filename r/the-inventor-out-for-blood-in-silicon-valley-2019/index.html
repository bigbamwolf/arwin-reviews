The Inventor: Out for Blood in Silicon Valley (2019) review

★★★½
Rated 3.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-03
652 reviews · 1,578 films logged · 174,449 words
Fake it till you make it queen. Call me crazy, but I admire these kinds of people. Not the fraudster side, but the ones who have relentless passion and belief that they can singlehandedly change the world. A crazy dreamer who stops at nothing to achieve their endgame. What I admire is that level of ambition and absolute conviction. The ability to walk into a room, sell people a future that does not exist yet, and make them believe you are the person who can build it is fascinating to me. That kind of confidence is part of what makes founders and visionaries so interesting to watch. But this is also exactly where the line gets crossed. She did not choose an industry where failure just means losing money or launching a bad product. She went into medicine. Once inaccurate test results can affect diagnoses, treatment decisions, and people’s health, the whole “fake it till you make it” mentality becomes incredibly dangerous. I was more interested in the business story and the machinery around Theranos than Elizabeth Holmes herself. She is creepy as fuck. The voice, the stare, the way she performs certainty all the time. I can understand why people found her convincing, but I also find her deeply unsettling. The documentary itself did not feel that gripping to me. It feels more like a hard-pounding exposé, laying out the claims, interviews, failures, and deception one after another. Informative, yes. Suspenseful? Not really. Still, this works really well as an introduction before I watch You Can See Everything (2026). Now I have the background, the business context, the scale of her ambition, and the reason everything collapsed. That should make the next film much more interesting to watch. Verdict: Interesting Watched on HBO Max
