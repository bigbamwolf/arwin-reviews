Aliens (1986) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-07
Action, Adventure, Horror · Directed by James Cameron · 137 min
621 reviews · 1,547 films logged · 160,724 words
<i>"Get away from her, you bitch!" </i> Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I really appreciate the old school filmmaking craft here. The painted backdrops, miniature ship models, practical sets, and tactile lighting give the whole film a physical weight that I can actually feel. Even when some environments clearly look studio built, the craftsmanship still carries so much texture and effort that I stay immersed. It makes the world feel massive without depending on CGI. What I enjoy most is how the film balances military sci-fi with rising suspense. The lighting, production design, and industrial atmosphere keep building pressure as things get darker and more dangerous. I also like that this feels more intense and emotionally charged than the first film, with Sigourney Weaver giving Ripley a commanding presence that is both tough and deeply human. For me, this is a great example of James Cameron turning scale, tension, and physical filmmaking into something unforgettable. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I really appreciate the classic filmmaking craft here. The painted backdrops instantly reminded me of Ralph McQuarrie style matte paintings from Star Wars. Those large environmental paintings used to extend the world beyond the physical sets. The same technique appears here with the planetary skies and distant colony environments. Then the spaceship shots rely heavily on miniature scale models, again very similar to the way Star Wars filmed its ships. Instead of CGI, they built physical models and shot them with controlled lighting and motion rigs. That tactile quality makes everything feel real. I can almost feel the metal and the weight of the ships moving through space. There are moments where outdoor environments clearly look like studio sets, especially with some of the colony exteriors. But that is forgivable considering the year it was produced. The craftsmanship still shines through. Even with those limitations, the film still feels massive and immersive. Watching this now, I can clearly see early Avatar DNA all over the film, especially in the military aesthetics. The colonial marines, the armored vehicles, the drop ships, and the entire military industrial look feel like early prototypes of the RDA soldiers in Avatar. Even the group dynamics are similar. A corporate backed military force entering a hostile environment thinking they are in control, only to realize the planet has its own rules. The color grading also feels related. That bluish green industrial tone appears a lot in the interiors here, very similar to the cool metallic palette Cameron used in Avatar for the base facilities and night operations. But I still prefer Aliens visually because everything is tactile. Real sets, real lighting, real objects. I can feel the texture. The lighting throughout the film is fantastic. It constantly reflects the level of danger they are in. As the situation gets worse, the environments become darker, more chaotic, more shadow filled. By the third act the cinematography becomes top tier filmmaking. The framing, the movement, the lighting inside the industrial corridors all build incredible tension. I also like that this one leans more into suspense and horror compared to the first film. Because I already know what the xenomorphs are capable of from part one, the danger here carries more weight. My heart was thumping in a completely different way. The addition of Newt raises the emotional stakes even more. Suddenly it is not just survival, it becomes protection. And that epic climactic score adds another layer of urgency. I was literally holding my breath. Just when I thought everything was over, Bishop gets stabbed. And I swear these xenomorphs really love hitchhiking. Every time they escape, one of them is secretly riding along. Ripley herself is more heroic and aggressive here. She feels closer to Neytiri in Avatar Fire and Ash, more unforgiving and more deadly when protecting someone she cares about. James Cameron is really great at handling powerful female characters. They are strong not just physically but emotionally driven by purpose. I also cannot ignore the fact that Sigourney Weaver was really a sex symbol when she was young. She has that strong and seductive natural screen presence. Not the typical glamorized kind, but the kind that comes from confidence and authority. Ripley just commands attention the moment she walks into the frame.
