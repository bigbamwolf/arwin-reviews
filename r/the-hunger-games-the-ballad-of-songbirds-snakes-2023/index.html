The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes (2023) review

★
Rated 1/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-09
652 reviews · 1,578 films logged · 174,449 words
Katniss really spoiled me because this prequel just does not hit the same. I don’t know, but I don’t feel as much connection here as I did with the original saga. Katniss is far more interesting. I cared about her survival, relationships, anger, and everything happening around her. Here, I never became that emotionally invested in Snow or Lucy Gray. But the choreography and camera work during the first battle royale are good. It is chaotic, but still a decent action scene. I like how frantic the camera feels once everyone starts scrambling around the arena. It is messy, but the chaos actually works for that moment. Jason Schwartzman is probably the most interesting character. His personality and humor make him stand out from everyone else. In such a serious movie, Lucky Flickerman gives the film some much needed personality. The resemblance to Stanley Tucci’s Caesar Flickerman is surprising too. I can’t remember if the original saga was this dark cinematography wise, but here I had a hard time seeing some scenes. The whole film feels too dark. I usually like moody cinematography, but not when I have to work just to understand what I am looking at. I don’t like the CGI arena either. The background extensions do not feel as tangible as the arena and environments in the first Hunger Games (2012). That first film felt dirtier and more real to me. Here, I can feel the digital environment too much, which removes some of the danger. I don’t know much about the prequel books. I’ve read all the original books, first editions, and I still have the hard copies here. Maybe that is also why I am naturally comparing everything to the original trilogy instead of approaching this completely fresh. And I know the singing is not literally what saves her from the snakes, but the way the scene is presented makes it look like she is calming them down by singing. What absurdity is this? I understand what the scene is doing, but visually, it still looks ridiculous to me. Rachel Zegler doesn’t have the charisma and star power to hold my attention. Even without comparing her to Jennifer Lawrence, I just don’t find her that interesting. But compared with Katniss, the difference becomes even bigger. Jennifer Lawrence could hold my attention. Rachel never really gave me that same pull here. The world building about the earlier Hunger Games did not really interest me that much either. I expected the origins of the Games to make me more curious, but I was more interested in getting back to the characters from the original saga. Aside from the arena action and Jason Schwartzman's performance, I genuinely struggled to find anything that made this film worth watching. I was bored, never connected with the characters, and even the ending did nothing for me. I honestly regret spending nearly three hours on this. Verdict: Failed TL;DR: I wanted to be as invested in this prequel as I was in the original Hunger Games saga, but I never really connected with Snow or Lucy Gray. I enjoyed the chaotic arena action and Jason Schwartzman's humor, but the overly dark cinematography, unconvincing CGI, and Rachel Zegler's lack of screen presence made it difficult for me to stay engaged. Even the origins of the Games weren't as interesting as I expected. Having read all the original books, I couldn't help comparing the two, and this one just doesn't have the same emotional pull for me.
