The Northman 2022

★★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-25
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): The scale is wide and imposing. The cast performs intensity, relying more on physical presence and expression than dialogue, making every interaction feel heavy and purposeful. Visually controlled and tonally severe, it asks for patience and immersion, then delivers a final act that feels raw, inevitable, and elemental rather than conventionally exciting. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ This immediately feels like Vikings because of the period and aesthetic. Parang anytime lalabas si Ragnar Lothbrok. At the same time, it also carries the power struggle and revenge DNA of Game of Thrones and House of the Dragon. Usurpers, bloodlines, and vengeance are baked into the narrative from the start. Technically, the film shines most in its scale and movement. The siege sequences done in one continuous shot is impressive, controlled, and immersive. The traveling wide shots across mountains, coastlines, and icy plains are the highlight for me. Eggers also leans into stylization. There are moments where the entire frame drops into black and white, with only a specific element retaining color. This color isolation effect feels mythic, almost like stepping into a vision or prophecy rather than reality. Some stealth and hunter-prey staging really works for me. Parang Assassin’s Creed, with Amleth surveying, observing, waiting, and striking at the perfect moment. Grabe yung brutal Viking game, Knattleikr, feels like Quidditch but stripped of fantasy and turned into pure savagery. The Christian persecution angle also echoes Silence by Scorsese, showing belief systems colliding in cruel, uncompromising ways. Performance-wise, Alexander Skarsgård carries a feral intensity, and you can definitely hear Stellan’s voice and presence in him. Nicole Kidman delivers a chilling monologue that completely reframes the story. Sobrang fucked up nya. Sinong nanay ang gustong patayin ang anak nya? A mother wishing death on her own son, even with the context of rape, is horrifying. Her character is deeply manipulative, power hungry, and morally broken. She doesn’t seek justice, she seeks control from the shadows, and for that, she deserves Amleth’s vengeance! The film’s intent is very clean. That said, during the first and second act, it felt “too clean” for my taste. Almost distant, like it was holding back its full cinematic potential. But the third act completely redeems that hold back. The final siege and volcano battle is pure cinema. Parang Anakin vs Obi-Wan on Mustafar, but make it barbaric. This is where everything finally ignites. The film could honestly be shorter so the entire experience feels more compact. Overall, The Northman is controlled, brutal, and myth-driven. It may take its time getting there, but the final act justifies the journey. A clean revenge tale that waits patiently before unleashing its fury.
