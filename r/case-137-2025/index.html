Case 137 2025

★★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-12
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Tense investigative drama about accountability and the difficulty of confronting institutions that protect their own. The film also touches on the fragile relationship between police and the communities they serve. It shows how mistrust grows on both sides and how power, perception, and fear shape the narrative. For me, the detective process is engaging because it feels like piecing together fragments of truth while navigating pressure, silence, and loyalty. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Police violence. Police bashing. Internal conflict of Stephanie: instead of chasing criminals, the investigation turns inward toward fellow officers. The film shows how the police force protects its own, covering up mistakes and abuses while one investigator tries to push through the truth. There is conflict and heart throughout the investigation, which keeps the story engaging. The tension between white officers and minority communities is also touched in this film. It shows how police power can easily turn into a power trip when there are no real consequences. Many people believe the police are untouchable and invincible, so they assume nothing will happen even if they come forward with information like the crucial testimony in case 137. Nakakagalit yung pagsisinungaling ng mga pulis. And the way they justify the violence is unbelievable! I really admire Stephanie’s calm demeanor while dealing with all the deception and manipulation from the accused officers. Kahit kaliwa’t kanan ang kasinungalingan, she keeps her composure. The film also explores how both sides see each other as enemies. Police often assume that everyone in protests is automatically a bad person. That stigma becomes a simple good versus bad narrative for both camps. To be honest, I am also guilty of sometimes thinking demonstrators are just disruptive or delinquent. But some of them are actually the voices of the voiceless. At the same time, because of the past violence committed by certain officers in the Philippines, there are moments when even being surrounded by police does not make me feel safe. There is also a small but meaningful look at how this affects police families. Stephanie’s son feels ashamed telling his classmates that his parents are police officer because he fears being judged. As a detective story, the film works really well. It feels like collecting fragments of truth and slowly piecing them together. I was never bored during the process. Side note, as an ex hotelier it was actually nice hearing someone explain what turn down service means. That little detail unexpectedly made me smile. The most frustrating part is that no one gets charged because of technicalities. I could really feel the disappointment of the victim’s mother. After everything that happened, justice still slips away. It got me thinking that sometimes leaking evidence online might force authorities to act because public pressure can move things that official systems refuse to touch. The film ends on a heavy note. Despite all the evidence Stephanie gathered and all the effort she poured into the case, nothing really changed. And that truth is probably the most painful part of the story.
