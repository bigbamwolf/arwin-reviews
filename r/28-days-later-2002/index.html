28 Days Later 2002

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-17
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): 28 Days Later opens with a deliberately harsh and abrasive visual style that can feel difficult to read at first, almost punishing in its roughness. As the film progresses, the imagery begins to settle, revealing more intentional shot choices and an aesthetic that favors atmosphere over clarity. Weather, light, and environment gradually become more expressive, giving the world texture and presence. While tension is uneven and some character portrayals lean exaggerated, the film eventually finds a visual and tonal rhythm that makes its approach feel purposeful rather than accidental. It is a challenging aesthetic that asks for patience, but once it locks in, the atmosphere becomes its strongest asset. Spoilers ahead. Read on if you’ve seen the film. From here on, plot details are discussed. It’s really hard to see details at the start. I don’t know why the director pushed this kind of aesthetic. The image is rough and unwatchable but after a while, my eyes naturally adjusted. When you really pay attention to the shot choices, you start to see that it’s also artistic. The film really clicked for me during the rainy blockade scene with Jim and Henry. From that point onward, the shots and lighting became much stronger. The lightning flashes and heavy downpour of rain added texture, contrast, and atmosphere. The environment finally felt alive. Their wardrobe looks like characters straight out of Left 4 Dead. Very utilitarian and, survival focused. That said, there’s still not much tension overall for me. The soldiers, in particular, feel over the top. More annoying than threatening. Still, there is a nice payoff. It’s not consistently tense, but once the film finds its rhythm visually and tonally, it becomes more engaging. The aesthetic is hard to love at first, but once it settles, you can appreciate what the director was trying to do with the imagery and atmosphere.
