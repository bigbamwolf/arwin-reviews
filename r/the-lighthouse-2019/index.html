The Lighthouse (2019) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-20
Drama, Fantasy, Horror · Directed by Robert Eggers · 109 min
629 reviews · 1,555 films logged · 164,190 words
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): The film immediately stands out through its immersive sound and production design, where the environment feels alive and oppressive rather than just background. Rain, wind, and sea noise are used as constant sensory pressure, trapping both the characters and the audience. Visually, the black and white cinematography is rich and expressive, with striking control over light and shadow that gives every frame texture and weight. The score functions less as music and more as psychological tension, slowly tightening unease. Performances are intense and theatrical, with both leads fully inhabiting a harsh, mythic world that feels decaying, claustrophobic, and punishing. This is not an easy watch, but a deliberate sensory experience that prioritizes atmosphere, madness, and endurance over comfort. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Sound design and set design ang unang nakaagaw ng aking atensyon. The rain, the wind howling, the splash against the stones, the foghorn, mist sprays, waves splashing. Lahat ng yan ramdam, hindi lang siya background noise, parang character mismo yung environment, constantly assaulting the senses and trapping both the characters and the audience in this isolated place. Lighting is also great even if this is a black and white film. The cinematography makes it alive and vibrant. Ang galing laruin yung highlights and shadows. This is cinematic black and white. Rich, textured, and expressive. Unlike the flat and boring look of C’mon C’mon (also by A24). The musical score grips my attention. There’s something insidious about it. It works more as psychological pressure than melody, slowly crawling under your skin and amplifying the descent into madness. The camera positioning feels like a mix of old school and modern filmmaking. Old school in the sense that it uses static, centered compositions, tight framing, and restrictive aspect ratio that feels theatrical and claustrophobic. But modern in how it weaponizes proximity. Extreme close-ups, distorted perspectives, and uncomfortable framing that pushes the audience into the characters’ personal space. It feels deliberate, controlled, and punishing. Set design feels real and tactile. Everything looks heavy, damp, and decaying. Ang galing din ng accent nila, parang old English, reinforcing the mythic and folkloric tone of the story. Mustached Robert Pattinson gives me strong Daniel Day-Lewis as Daniel Plainview vibes from There Will Be Blood, especially in the third act nung nagwawala na siya. That same raw desperation, ego collapse, and unhinged physicality. You can feel him unraveling mentally. Willem Dafoe is equally intense. His performance is theatrical, and unrelenting. Parang siya yung embodiment ng the sea, tradition, and madness all rolled into one. Every monologue feels like a curse. Overall, The Lighthouse is a sensory and psychological assault. Not just a film you watch, but something you endure.
