The Bride! 2026

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-05
Jessie Buckley really feels like Bellatrix here. Not just the wild energy but that unpredictable madness she carries in her eyes. The way she moves, talks, and explodes emotionally reminds me of Bellatrix Lestrange during her chaotic moments in Harry Potter. That mix of danger, seduction, and unhinged confidence. You never feel like she is fully in control, but that is exactly what makes her so captivating to watch. Christian Bale on the other hand felt meh to me. He is fine, but nothing about his performance stood out the way Buckley’s did. The film is artistically lit. The gothic feeling mostly comes from the lighting rather than heavy color grading or overly elaborate sets like what we see in Frankenstein 2025. A lot of the mood comes from shadows, and how the light shapes the faces and textures of the environment. This film is full of violence and romance at the same time. Parang Joker and Harley tandem but placed in a world that openly confronts sexual violence and social repression. The film also digs into loneliness, mental health, and identity. Two damaged people trying to define themselves in a society that already labeled them as monsters. Honestly this feels like a better treatment of what Joker Folie à Deux was trying to explore. The themes are almost the same. Broken people, chaotic love, rebellion against society. But Joker 2 went too far with the musical aspect. I think that is why people rejected it. Here the emotions feel more grounded even when the characters are unstable. In a weird way this almost feels like a non canon sequel to Guillermo’s Frankenstein. If someone finished that film and wanted more of that tragic monster world, this scratches a similar itch. But tonally and in terms of scale, I would still prefer Guillermo’s Frankenstein. That one feels grander, while this one feels more intimate and psychological. Both interesting, but Guillermo’s version still hits harder for me.
