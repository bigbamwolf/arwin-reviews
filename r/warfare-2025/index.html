Warfare 2025

★★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-10-18
Tahimik ang simula, pero punong puno ng tensyon. Parang ticking time bomb na alam mong sasabog anytime. The film opens with surgical, tactical precision: scouting, probing, and formation. Kahit walang dialogue, ramdam mo na agad yung anticipation of danger. It feels like being a rat trapped inside a cage, quietly breathing, waiting for chaos to begin. The production design is solid. The suburban apartment setup looks authentic, too normal for a war zone, which makes the violence that follows hit even harder. You can almost smell the dust and heat. Sound design is also top tier: the ringing, muffled echoes, and overlapping radio chatter pull you straight into shellshock. Every gunfire feels deafening, and even the quiet moments have that heavy hum of dread. Parang last na naramdaman ko yung ganito ay during noong nanood ako ng Dunkirk sa IMAX. Joseph Quinn carries the middle act with pure physical agony: His moaning and screaming agony practically becomes the film’s score, blending into the background as an audio manifestation of pain and trauma. Will Poulter’s raspy voice adds weight to the tension, as if you’re stuck in the same smoke filled room with them. Prosthetic work is insane! The burned limbs and shrapnel wounds look disgustingly real, the kind that makes you flinch instead of look away. Nakakadiri pero effective, because it pulls you into their suffering. Cinematography wise, most shots are steady and deliberate, proving that you don’t need excessive shake or rapid cuts to feel the chaos. Each frame has purpose and timing, creating intensity through focus, similar to how John Wick achieves tension without disorienting the viewer. The framing is tight, the movement is controlled, and the camera lingers just long enough to make you feel trapped in the moment. The CASEVAC sequence: two platoons merging into one to rescue their wounded is masterclass tactical filmmaking as it is is brilliantly staged. Ramdam mo talaga yung focus, discipline, and desperation all at once. Every shout of “Move!” or “Go! Go! Go!” carries weight. Ramdam mo yung camaraderie. Walang iwanan. Kahit ang hirap ng sitwasyon, they move as one. Charles Melton stands out with commanding presence: composed under fire, grounding the chaos with authority, calm but deadly. Quinn’s vulnerability balances that leadership, showing both extremes of courage and fear. On the other side of the spectrum, the Arabian civilian family caught in the middle adds another emotional layer. You realize no one truly wins, and civilians suffer the most. A24’s signature pacing is here again: slow burn start, ramping up to pure chaos midway. Tahimik sa una, tapos biglang mageexplode all at once. The suffering, the noise, the humanity are all wrapped in dread and dust.  Warfare feels like Black Hawk Down filtered through A24’s minimalist intensity. It doesn’t glorify battle, it immerses you in it through body, mind, and sound.
