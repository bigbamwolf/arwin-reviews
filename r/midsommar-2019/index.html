Midsommar (2019) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-10-27
620 reviews · 1,546 films logged · 160,269 words
Ang ganda ng color contrasts sa opening sequence. Si Florence Pugh, grabe, she carried the whole film. I can feel her grief, confusion, at yung slow decline nya emotionally and mentally. Her performance perfectly matches the director’s visual storytelling that follows. Napansin ko agad na mahilig gumamit ng mirror shots si Ari Aster kapag may kausap na may opposing idea or conflict. Parang symbol siya ng reflection o duality. Tapos yung transition mula dorm room papunta sa airplane toilet, ang smooth! Seamless cut, pero unsettling din parang nagpapahiwatig na dala nya yung emotions nya wherever she goes. Yung camera shake sa airplane window, signifying that what is about to happen will shake her to her core. Yung long upside down shot habang papunta sila sa commune, haunting talaga. Literal at metaphorical na "turning her world upside down." Alam mong may mangyayaring masama. The flute music that welcomes them feels hypnotic, parang sinasadya kang dalhin sa trance. Tapos nung sinabi yung line na “90 years since our last feast,” ayun na, alam mong may mangyayari. At first, kala ko cannibalism pero buti hindi naman pala. Aster’s direction thrives on patience and control. The long, uninterrupted wide shots make the experience suffocating in a good way. Wala kang ligtas. Wala kang escape. Typical A24 rhythm mabagal pero deliberate. Every scene feels like may pinapatagong sagot. Hindi siya nagmamadali. Parang binibigyan ka ng oras para makaramdam ng discomfort, para mapansin mo yung bawat detail, at para maramdaman mong trapped ka rin kasama nila. The slow pacing works because it mirrors Dani’s emotional state. Heavy, lost, and looking for meaning. The tension quietly builds through repetition and long silences, until the chaos finally erupts in the last act. The high scene was trippy. The way Ari shot the trees breathing and the ground pulsating ay sobrang psychedelic. Ang tahimik ng mga scene, pero ramdam mo ang tension. Silence becomes a sound of its own. Yung long staring moments ng mga cult members, sobrang creepy. At yung sequence ng pagkain nila sabay sabay, synchronized pa ang galaw, parang ritualistic nightmare. There’s something about doing psychological horror in broad daylight, as opposed sa nakasanayan nating dark films. Dito they are out in the open pero ang creepy parin parang there is something evil in the air; you can feel and breathe it. At the height of that daylight dread, the horror finally lands. Yung cliff jumping scene, grabe. The sound, the smash napakavivid. At yung nagpan ang camera sa tapestry foreshadowing na ng "love potion" buti di pinakita yung paggupit sa k*ffy. Foreshadowing din pala yung unang 20 minutes na pinag uusapan nila yung impregnating Swedish girls hindi ko akalaing literal nilang ipapakita sa dulo. Yung dance scene sobrang ganda. Nakakahilo pero in a good way. Habang umiikot ang camera, ramdam mo na parang umiikot na rin ang utak ni Dani. The sex scene, sobrang weird. Habang nagppump may kumakanta sa gilid, tapos ginagaya pa yung ungol. Collective insanity na disguised as culture. Napaisip ako, ganito siguro sa cult, no? They seek out people’s vulnerability and use it to appear empathetic, until you end up joining them. Sa ending, habang nasusunog ang temple at nakangiti si Dani, parang ang dami kong nafeel. Masaya ba siya kasi nahanap na nya ang community nya na nakakaintindi sa kanya and yung boyfriend nyang di sya iniintindi, siya na mismo ang dahilan ng pagkamatay nito? Or baliw na siya completely? Or baka in her own twisted way, naging masaya siya kasi this time, hindi na siya nag iisa sa grief niya. Ang pinakamalalim na theme ng Midsommar ay grief disguised as enlightenment. Sa surface, parang tungkol lang sa cult at breakup, pero underneath it all, it is about how grief can rewire someone’s entire identity. The commune didn’t just manipulate Dani. They reflected her pain back at her. Parang sinasabi ng film na kapag masyado kang nasanay sa collective suffering, nagiging dependent ka na, pero hindi pa pala na pprocess yung trauma mo. It’s the psychology of grief turning into belonging. Sa dulo, you’re not sure if she’s healed or just fully consumed by her own pain.
