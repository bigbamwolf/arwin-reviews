Lights Out (2016) review

★★½
Rated 2.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-01
Horror, Mystery · Directed by David F. Sandberg · 81 min
640 reviews · 1,566 films logged · 168,702 words
Some horror concepts really are better left short. Oh, how I miss Teresa Palmer. I loved her in I Am Number Four (2011). As much as I love her, the movie lacks substance. She is still good here for me. It was genuinely nice seeing her again, and I enjoyed her performance even when the story around her felt thin. The story is too thin, and Diana is not interesting enough for me to carry an entire feature length movie. The short film is better than this version. I actually rated that one highly because the simplicity of the concept made it terrifying. Lights off, something is there. Lights on, it disappears. That is basically all it needed. Stretching that idea into a full movie means adding mythology, family drama, and repeated variations of the same scare, and for me that actually weakens what made the original concept so effective. The light and dark gimmick still works at first. I think the first couple of appearances of Diana were genuinely creepy, but after that, the horror quickly lost its effectiveness. Once I already understand the rules, each new setup becomes easier to anticipate. I also saw the ending coming. Diana is basically unkillable as long as she remains connected to the mother, so once I started connecting the pieces, there were not many ways left for the story to permanently get rid of her. The ending makes sense, but that also makes it predictable. This feels like one of those cases where expanding a great short film idea actually exposes how little story there was underneath it. I still found some entertainment in the concept and Teresa Palmer's performance, but the repetitive scares and thin story made the rest of the film pretty underwhelming. I don't regret watching it, but I wouldn't actively choose to watch it again. Verdict: Barely Watchable
