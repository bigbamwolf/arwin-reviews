Coraline (2009) review

★★★½
Rated 3.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-09
652 reviews · 1,578 films logged · 174,449 words
I don’t love stop motion, but damn, I respect the hell out of this. The gravy train made me say wow! I love the camera movement when it travels inside the tunnel. I initially thought they might have used a probe lens to achieve that shot, but I could not verify the exact lens or technique they used. I love the floor turning into a giant spider web too, especially when the darkness suddenly turns into light. Apparently, that giant web was actually cut out of steel and rigged so the floor could physically fall away into it. That makes the shot even more impressive to me. So visually nice! I’m not really a fan of stop motion because I feel like the movement can be clanky, but I appreciate the great amount of time and effort it takes to create one. But I do love the physicality and tangibility of it. The puppets, sets, props, lighting, and environments actually existed in miniature form, and I think that is where stop motion wins me over. I think the message for me is that parents are imperfect, and sometimes imperfect is okay compared with someone pretending to be perfect but only offering conditional love. Coraline’s real parents may not always give her everything she wants, but their love is real. The Other Mother gives her the illusion of everything she wants, but only if she accepts her conditions. That contrast works for me because perfection starts looking a lot less attractive once love comes with terms and conditions. I was more interested in the visuals, craftsmanship, and ideas than emotionally connected to Coraline herself. I also see this more as dark fantasy than something genuinely scary. The creepy imagery works, but I spent more time admiring how they physically made everything than actually feeling afraid. I think this is a genuinely good film, especially when it comes to visual creativity and craftsmanship. But I was frequently disengaged from the story and never became particularly emotionally invested in Coraline herself. I appreciate the artistry more than I enjoyed the entire viewing experience. Verdict: Interesting
