Seven Samurai 1954

★★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-04-02
There are silent stretches in this film where the performances really shine. I like how the farmers actually look like farmers, struggling, hungry, and worn down by life. That level of realism makes the story stick more. It is also why I enjoyed the assembling of the seven samurai. Surprisingly, their bond becomes fun and light even with the danger waiting for them. Even the villagers slowly change because of their presence. From fearful and helpless, they begin to grow more courageous. The highlights and shadows are also great. Even in the earlier parts of the film, I could already tell why this holds so much prestige. The lighting is so controlled, even in outdoor scenes. The sunlight never feels like it is just washing everything out. The image still has shape and depth. Even the night cinematography feels way ahead of its time. This film does not feel old at all. Honestly, it looks better than some modern films. The strategy in the story is also really good. The film makes the preparation feel just as important as the battles. On top of that, there are so many great and likeable characters. The level of direction and coordination here is insane. Truly a masterpiece!
