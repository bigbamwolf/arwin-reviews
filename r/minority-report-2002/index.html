Minority Report (2002) review

★★★½
Rated 3.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-12-24
600 reviews · 1,526 films logged · 155,998 words
Sa simula pa lang, thriller na agad. Walang warm up. Tension agad. Visually, it relies heavily on real sets mixed with early years of CGI. Parang Men in Black era, but slightly below the high level of detail of Phantom Menace. You can really feel na transitional period siya between practical filmmaking and full digital cinema. The core question of the film is strong: are you really going to imprison someone for a crime they haven’t committed yet? That moral dilemma carries the story well. Lighting and color grading remind me of the first Transformers movie. Cold, bluish, metallic. It fits the futuristic surveillance heavy world, but it also makes the CGI stand out more. The CGI itself clearly shows its age. The textures feel different from the practical elements, and even the lighting on the digital objects looks fake at times. That said, the action sequences are still impressive. The chase scene with Tom Cruise climbing the fire exit ladder is masterful. That entire sequence blends practical stunts, special effects, and green screen really well. It feels seamless and kinetic. The conveyor belt scene immediately reminded me of Attack of the Clones, specifically the Genosha factory sequence. I checked the release dates. Star Wars Episode II came out May 16, 2002, while Minority Report was June 17, 2002. It really feels like George Lucas and Steven Spielberg shared similar ideas or inspirations around that time. While thinking about that, I also noticed the scoring style. It felt very John Williams. And when I checked, yes, it was him. Ang ganda rin ng long continuous shot during the robot spider deployment. The way the camera moves while the spiders search each room builds tension really well and shows Spielberg’s control of space and movement. Reminds me of John Wick 3. Story wise, I had a strong gut feeling early on that Lamar was the villain. When the detective finally cracks the case and then suddenly gets shot, that moment hit hard. Sayang siya. It felt unfair in a very deliberate way. The precog female is still weird to me. There is something unsettling about her presence that never fully settles into clarity. When the story finally comes full circle, I think the payoff works. The resolution is satisfying enough and ties the themes together well. That said, I still think Deja Vu is the better film overall. It feels more emotionally grounded and cleaner in execution.
