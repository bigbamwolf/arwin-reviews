Sore: A Wife from the Future 2025

★★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-12-08
The film opens with beautiful landscape photography, and that visual quality stays consistent all throughout. The early hook works immediately: Sore is actually Jo’s wife from the future. She has that confident, know it all look with a playful seductive smirk that hints she knows more than she is letting on. Karlo is the comic relief, parang Joey Tribbiani type na auditioning actor, and his scenes balance the heavier moments well. Halfway in, the big reveal hits hard. Sore is stuck in a time loop that resets every time Jo relapses and she fails to stop it or kapag nirereveal nyang mamamatay si Jo in the future. That alone says a lot about their relationship. When she finally breaks the cycle, she briefly feels freedom, but she still chooses to fight for Jo. That emotional shift feels real. There is love, frustration, hope, and exhaustion all in one. You also see the real life version of this idea. Every relapse or refusal to change kills the people around us a little bit. It hurts them to watch someone they love destroy themselves slowly, and sometimes the stubbornness is disguised as “care” or “control.” The film captures that well. Her mission changes too. She is no longer trying to prevent him from messing up. She is trying to help him change from within. And the revelation that Elsa and Sore are basically the same type of presence in his life. Someone trying to shape Jo, adds another emotional layer. Ang ganda ng travel montage nila papunta sa dad ni Jo. The whole world around them is blue, cold, and muted, while Sore wears an orange or pinkish dress. Parang siya lang yung nagbibigay kulay sa mundo ni Jo. I love these subtle choices. Just when you think she finally succeeded, the film shifts again. There is another POV: Time itself. First time ko makakita ng ganito sa film and it works surprisingly well. The line that hit me the most is when Jo said, “I don’t think people will change out of fear, but because they feel loved.” I agree. We only let go of destructive habits when we feel understood, needed, and unhurried. By the end, the film confirms two things: One, the Arctic exhibit will work per Sore. Two, parallel universes exist because they now live in another reality where they choose each other again. The film gives off vibes of Netflix’s Dark, Edge of Tomorrow, and Time Traveler’s Wife. Acting quality of both Jo and Sore is really great. Sheila Dara Aisha is so beautiful!
