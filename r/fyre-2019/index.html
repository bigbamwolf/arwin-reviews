Fyre (2019) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-28
629 reviews · 1,555 films logged · 164,190 words
A visionary without risk management is just an expensive nightmare. Yeah, from an advertising standpoint, they rocked. They understood hype, exclusivity, influencers, and how to sell people an image before there was even something real behind it. But logistics? They sucked. They should have hired someone like me who plans for contingencies. Someone who is not a yes man and contradicts every pipe dream they want to make it realistic. You need somebody in the room willing to say, “No, that cannot be done with this budget, timeline, and manpower.” This is like when I was listening to a person with a big vision but is not grounded by facts and experience on how to do those things. They just have money but no way of thinking it through to execute. Vision is exciting, but execution is where reality starts collecting payment. I mean, I would love to have the same attitude about unwavering spirit, but it has to be set in reality too. If you’re going to get things done for the first time, get the experts in that field first. Visionaries are sometimes not good with projections, finances, especially expenses, and that is based on my experience working with business partners. They are just out of touch. They see where they want to go, but sometimes completely underestimate how much money, manpower, time, and contingency it takes to actually get there. And they are not the ones who suffer. The people below them suffer because of their misaligned version of the future. People on the ground suck it up because it is their pride and joy to execute an event or idea even if they know it is far fetched. That part pissed me off the most because these are talented people trying to salvage someone else’s impossible promises. It will always be a nightmare when you don’t listen to people with enough sense of risk management. There’s a lot of wasted talent, and it’s hard to lick the wounds. This is what happens when every salesman with great charisma and charm thinks they can be an entrepreneur. Being able to sell a vision is completely different from knowing how to build the systems needed to deliver it. What Billy did to the people under him and the subcontractors was pure evil. He laid waste and left personal debts to the people. His ambition did not just fail financially. Other people were left carrying the consequences. The documentary is so effective because I am so mad at Billy’s guts. This is like The Tinder Swindler (2022). I can admire the confidence and hate the deception altogether. Both made me think about how charisma, confidence, and absolute conviction can be impressive skills until they are used to make other people trust something that was never grounded in reality. Really no conscience. Verdict: Absolute Cinema Watched on Netflix. TL;DR: From an advertising standpoint, they rocked. They knew how to sell hype, exclusivity, and an image, but their logistics and execution were a complete disaster. As someone who deals with planning, finances, and risk management, it pissed me off watching visionaries make impossible promises while ignoring the people who actually know how to execute them. The worst part is that Billy wasn't the one left suffering. It was the employees, subcontractors, and talented people forced to clean up his mess and carry his debts. It reminded me of The Tinder Swindler (2022), where charisma and confidence become dangerous when there's absolutely no conscience behind them.
