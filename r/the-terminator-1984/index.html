The Terminator (1984) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-08
601 reviews · 1,527 films logged · 156,111 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I love how the film establishes the Terminator as a pure force of intent from the moment he arrives. Arnold’s mechanical presence, paired with that pulsing score, creates constant dread that never really lets me relax. The gritty future war aesthetic and practical stunt work make everything feel tangible, especially the chase sequences. Some effects show their age, but the atmosphere and tension still carry the experience. Overall, it’s raw, suspenseful sci-fi that works because I can feel the weight and danger in almost every frame. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ That’s how you make an entrance! The Terminator arrives with pure intent and zero hesitation, and that iconic exchange, “You can’t do that.” “Wrong.” immediately defines his character. He does not give a shit, he does not react emotionally, he simply executes the mission. That single moment tells you everything about the threat level. Arnold’s stoic, mechanical presence makes the hunt for Sarah Connor genuinely tense. He feels inevitable, like a force rather than a person. The score plays a huge role in building that tension. The pulsing techno theme acts like a ticking clock, constantly reminding me that danger is approaching. Other musical cues layer in dread and urgency, creating a relentless atmosphere. The film’s future war aesthetic reinforces this mood. The world is dark, dirty, and depressing. The practical sets, debris, smoke, and metallic textures give it a raw physicality that feels tangible. It’s the kind of gritty sci-fi environment that sells the illusion of an apocalypse, similar to X-Men: Days of Future Past. I can really feel the texture of the world. Earlier sequence from a flashback reminds me also when Chani and Paul destroys the spice harvester in Dune: Part Two. Visually, the film balances grounded action with stylized spectacle. The Sarah Connor chase sequences are tight and suspenseful, emphasizing pursuit. The tunnel chase is a standout. Smoke filled lighting, glowing colors, and the Terminator emerging from chaos create pure cinematic adrenaline. The explosion of the truck adds to that realism. I feel the impact because the stunt work is believable. In contrast, prosthetics are mostly unimpressive. The eye removal scene has a rubbery quality that shows its age, but it is easy to forgive given the ambition. The score cleverly fooled me. Just when the score suggests everything is done, it shifts back into a threatening rhythm, revealing that the nightmare is far from over. The burned flesh peeling away to expose the machine underneath is a memorable frame. It transforms the villain from a damaged pursuer into an unstoppable horror icon. When the skeletal Terminator is shown through practical puppetry, it is genuinely creepy. The stop motion shots are less convincing and funny today. Overall, The Terminator succeeds because of its gritty production design, the physical craftsmanship and tension driven storytelling still hit hard. That tunnel sequence alone is enough to elevate the experience into something memorable.
