We're All Going to the World's Fair (2021) review

★
Rated 1/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-16
674 reviews · 1,599 films logged · 185,761 words
Feels like a low budget school project that somehow found one really good actress, with Anna Cobb carrying the whole horror experiment while losing her shit. The low budget digital look and experimental style make it feel unfinished at times. There are so many random filler shots where almost nothing happens that I started feeling like some of them were only there to stretch the runtime enough to call it feature length. It also feels like every horror trope is mashed into one film. Found footage, webcam horror, creepy internet challenge, body horror, psychological breakdown, and analog horror vibes. Instead of making the film feel richer, it sometimes feels like an experiment trying different horror ideas to see what sticks. Anna Cobb is easily the strongest part for me, especially considering this was her feature film acting debut. Her awkwardness feels natural instead of performed. Then when her mood starts shifting and she begins screaming and spiraling, I genuinely believed Casey was losing her mind. I was not really emotionally invested in her, though. I was watching the performance more than caring about the character. That is probably the biggest disconnect for me. The film wants me to sit inside Casey’s loneliness and deterioration, but because I never became attached to her, the long stretches of isolation felt more like waiting than tension. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The one body horror moment that actually got me was when the ticket came out from under the skin. That was disturbing. For a brief moment, the weird internet horror finally became physically uncomfortable instead of just abstract. I just wish more of the film gave me that same reaction. Veridict: Failed TL;DR: I appreciate the ambition of making an unconventional horror film with limited resources, but the result felt more like an experimental student project than a fully realized movie. I kept waiting for the horror to get under my skin, but most of the experience was just tedious rather than unsettling. Anna Cobb delivers an impressive debut performance and is easily the best thing about the film, although I never became emotionally invested in her character. A few disturbing moments showed some potential, but the excessive filler, lack of tension, and scattered execution made this a disappointing watch for me. Failed.
