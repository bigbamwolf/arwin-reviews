Smile (2022) review

★★★½
Rated 3.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-31
Horror, Mystery, Thriller · Directed by Parker Finn · 115 min
634 reviews · 1,560 films logged · 166,661 words
Trauma really said: pass it on. I found myself more fascinated by what the film was trying to communicate than by the actual horror. It has a genuinely interesting premise, and I appreciate horror films that give me something to think about beyond the scares. But I never really felt the fear or emotional impact that I was expecting. I was more intellectually engaged than emotionally invested. Sosie Bacon made the experience convincing even when the horror itself wasn't getting under my skin. I can appreciate what the film accomplished, especially with its central idea, but I wish the experience had affected me as much as the concept interested me. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ It reminded me of Final Destination 5 (2011). The moment they discovered that the curse passes on to the next person who witnesses the suicide was when the whole cycle clicked for me. Kill to break the cycle or be killed by the chain of events. What stayed with me more than the horror itself was the thought behind it. I was not really scared, disturbed, tense, or sad. I just appreciated the idea of this never ending cycle of trauma. Sosie Bacon’s acting is so good. Her whole performance worked for me, not just one particular scene. I completely bought her gradual breakdown throughout the film. Well, I think the intention of the film is also to say that trauma can lead to suicide. And witnessing someone harm themselves can inflict or transfer that trauma to the beholder. I think the film communicated that idea effectively because I picked up on it without needing it to be spelled out too much. Rose wants to treat mental illness, but eventually she becomes someone who cannot escape it herself. She also wants to stop the curse, but no matter what she does, the cycle keeps moving. Confronting childhood trauma is really not enough, as one of the doctors said earlier in the film. That is simply the nature of trauma. The trauma is still there even after confronting it. The ending was frustrating because I kept telling the cop through the screen to look away. He already cracked the case, but he still could not stop himself from watching the suicide. That reminded me of Nope (2022), where people cannot look away from spectacle even when looking puts them in danger. Here, that inability to look away becomes the final reason the cycle continues. Verdict: Interesting Watched on HBO Max
