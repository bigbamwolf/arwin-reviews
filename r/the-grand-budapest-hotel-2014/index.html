The Grand Budapest Hotel (2014) review

★
Rated 1/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-21
Comedy, Drama · Directed by Wes Anderson · 99 min
598 reviews · 1,524 films logged · 155,326 words
Maganda ang visuals. The symmetry and color palette are very deliberate, almost storybook-like. Yung blacks are not pure black but more greyish or muted, parang may faded pastel wash over the whole image. This makes everything feel flat in a controlled way, removing harsh contrast so nothing ever feels truly dark or threatening. It creates a nostalgic, illustrated look rather than a cinematic one grounded in realism. Everything is always centered. Frames are locked, balanced, and composed like dioramas. The use of miniatures is also very prominent, serving as a visual language that reinforces the artificial, handcrafted world. While I get the intention behind it, it’s not really my cup of tea. For me, it feels too simplistic. I don’t feel the depth of a cinematic experience because the style keeps reminding me that I’m watching something carefully constructed rather than something I can emotionally sink into. Clean dolly shots, static shots, push-ins, pull-outs. Lahat malinis, precise, and controlled. Walang mess, walang chaos. That level of stability and perfection actually makes me sleepy. There’s no visual tension. It’s technically impressive but emotionally distant. There are comedic beats scattered throughout, and they work in a dry, quirky way. Ang ganda rin ng close-up shot ni Agatha. Overall, it’s quirky and fun, but I’m just not the right audience for it. Even if I like OCD things and controlled aesthetics, my mind keeps drifting to how the style feels constrained, almost like a low-budget approach or something from a pre–technological advancement era of filmmaking. Not because of lack of skill, but because of intentional limitation as an aesthetic choice. This is my second viewing, hoping I’d feel something different this time around. Unfortunately, the same feeling came back: boredom. I respect the craft, the discipline, and the identity, but it still doesn’t connect with me on a deeper level.
