The Bridges of Madison County 1995

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-11
Meryl Streep’s charisma is undeniable here. Much younger compared to the last time I saw her in Don’t Look Up as the president, and she carries this quiet, restrained presence that slowly opens up as the film goes on. There’s a very clear sexual tension between Robert and Francesca. Francesca feels like she’s losing her sense of grounding and becoming playfully confused. Bagay sa kanya yung ganitong roles, yung nasa pagitan ng pagpili, emotionally torn, unsure which path to take. She comes across as a lonely, bored housewife who hasn’t been touched or truly seen by her husband in a long time, and the arrival of Robert suddenly injects thrill, attention, and desire back into her life. You can really feel the burning desire underneath everything. They crave each other deeply. Ang tanda na nila, pero yung pananabik parang teenager! That contrast is one of the strongest emotional pulls of the film. Maganda rin yung mga love songs. They’re soft, melodic, and nostalgic, mostly leaning toward gentle folk and classic romantic ballads. The music doesn’t overpower the scenes but instead quietly amplifies longing, regret, and intimacy, making the moments feel suspended in time, almost like memories being formed as you watch them. Eventually, the film traps them in an emotional dilemma: the family Francesca built versus a new life with her newfound love. Personally, I find it highly unlikely that something like this realistically happens at their age. The drama deepens in the latter part, but I couldn’t fully invest in their “cause.” No matter how romantic the lens is, it’s still cheating. They wouldn’t be in this situation had they not played with fire. They fucked around and now they found out, and what follows feels like shared trauma born from their own choices. What really bothered me is how Francesca acts completely normal when her family comes back, welcoming them as if nothing happened. Walang kamalay malay yung asawa niya. I can’t sympathize with that, especially as someone who doesn’t want to be betrayed behind my back. Maybe in another life or reality, I’d cry at the idea that they can’t have what they want, that they found something they’re absolutely certain about but are denied the freedom to choose it. A kind of forbidden love where happiness exists but is unreachable. So maybe the unintentional lesson here is simple: love your spouse well, so they don’t go looking for romantic love elsewhere. Still, what a performance by Meryl Streep! I guess it’s poetic in the sense that the bridge which started it all became the only “bridge” for them to be together in the end. Though not in this lifetime, but in the afterlife.
