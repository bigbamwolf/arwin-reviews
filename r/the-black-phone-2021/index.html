The Black Phone (2021) review

★★★½
Rated 3.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-31
654 reviews · 1,580 films logged · 175,559 words
Prisoners with a supernatural escape room. I honestly went into The Black Phone without expecting much, but it turned out to be a pleasant surprise. I like films that reward me for paying attention, especially when things that initially seem insignificant become important later. There is something satisfying about realizing that the storytelling was more carefully constructed than I initially gave it credit for. I wouldn't say I was completely hooked from beginning to end. There were stretches where I wanted the film to move faster, but the payoff made the patience worthwhile. It's one of those films I appreciated more after finishing than while I was actually watching it. Interesting, with a particularly satisfying final act. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ It reminded me of Prisoners (2013) because of the kidnapping of kids. But this one goes in a completely different direction by turning the kidnapping story into a supernatural escape film. It also reminded me of prison escape films where the characters cleverly use whatever materials are available inside their prison to plan their escape. I love how every failed attempt and every piece of advice from the previous victims eventually becomes useful. Nothing feels wasted. There is also some Doctor Sleep (2019) in the investigative supernatural ability to track the kidnapper and killer. It also reminded me of Eleven from Stranger Things (2016–2025), communicating with other people through supernatural means. But here, I like how the supernatural element directly helps move the escape plan forward instead of simply being there for scares. The lighting and color are good. Nothing in particular stood out to me, but the film consistently looks intentional. Ethan Hawke’s voice was terrifying. I honestly did not notice much from the rest of his performance because the voice itself was already doing so much work. The calm way he speaks somehow makes him even creepier. Nice payback in the third act. Really satisfying death. Totally deserved. All the planning pulled off. This is easily the strongest part of the film for me. Every little thing Finney learned from the other kids finally comes together. I also like the character development of the father. He starts appreciating his kids more after one of them goes missing. I did not really feel emotional about his arc, but I noticed the change. And I like how Finney is finally respected by his schoolmates. Before this, he was constantly being bullied. One of the kids even told him earlier that he needed to stand up for himself one of these days. By the end, he finally does. He does not just escape the Grabber. He becomes the kind of kid who can finally fight back. I did not expect this to be this good. I think the buildup could have been more engaging because I was mostly waiting for the escape and final confrontation. But the third act was so satisfying that it made me appreciate everything that came before it. Every little detail suddenly mattered. I think the storytelling is clever, especially in hindsight, but I enjoyed the payoff more than the entire experience itself. Verdict: Interesting Watched on HBO Max. TL;DR: Prisoners (2013) with a supernatural escape room. I didn't expect much, but I love how every failed escape attempt, piece of advice, and seemingly insignificant detail eventually pays off in the third act. Ethan Hawke's voice was terrifying, and watching Finney finally fight back against the Grabber was incredibly satisfying. The buildup could have been more engaging because I wasn't completely hooked throughout, but the clever storytelling and rewarding payoff made me appreciate the film more after finishing it. Interesting.
