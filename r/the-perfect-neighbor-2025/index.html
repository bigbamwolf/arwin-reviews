The Perfect Neighbor (2025) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-15
613 reviews · 1,539 films logged · 158,229 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): The film keeps shifting my confidence from one side to the other, making every new detail feel like it could completely change how I see the situation. That uncertainty is what pulled me in the most, because I felt like I was constantly recalibrating my own judgment. The emotional beats land hard when the film slows down and lets them breathe. For me, it works best as a tense, emotionally loaded mystery where doubt is the real engine. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I honestly did not know who to believe anymore while watching this. I kept giving the benefit of the doubt to both sides. Every new detail makes me question what I just believed a few minutes earlier. The score plays a huge role in keeping the tension high. It quietly pushes the uneasiness forward, and I found myself unable to look away from the screen. The moment that really broke me was when the father told his children that their mom was dead. That scene hit hard. The emotional weight of it felt very real. The legal technicalities also became frustrating at times. There are moments where the law feels almost incomprehensible, like it is operating in a completely different language from common sense. Still, the detectives did a really great job piecing everything together.
