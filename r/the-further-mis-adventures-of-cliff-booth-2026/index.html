The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth (2026) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-26
674 reviews · 1,599 films logged · 185,761 words
Tarantino tension with Fincher precision. A combination that feels illegal. And if you feel like the Manson Family is not enough, Cliff finally found opponents who might actually hit back. Fincher’s control and precision meet Tarantino’s snappy and humorous dialogue, with Tarantino’s signature buildup eventually exploding into violence that releases all the tension. The combination works surprisingly well because I can still feel both filmmakers without one completely swallowing the other. Collab of the year! The dialogue, humor, strange detours, and sudden violence are unmistakably Tarantino. But the framing, pacing, and visual control feel much more Fincher. Even that yellowish, low-key lighting immediately feels like his world to me. Great idea bringing in Fincher so he can give fresh eyes and breathe new life into Tarantino’s world. I love Elizabeth Debicki’s Karen Crosby. I really like women in positions of power. So hot! She is also one of my Hollywood crushes. Karen has that confidence, authority, intelligence, and elegance where she can talk to Cliff almost like an equal instead of being intimidated by him. Debicki really owns that presence. I also love Karen’s whole view of movies being like hamburgers. I actually agree with her to some extent. Not every movie needs to be dissected like it contains some profound message about humanity. Some films are art. Some are made for the mass market. Some are garbage. Some exist simply to entertain people. And those things can coexist. Sometimes I want something layered enough that I can keep thinking about it afterward. Sometimes I just want a really good hamburger. Cliff as a Hollywood fixer also feels like the perfect evolution of the character. He can basically be placed in any type of job and somehow blend in. Bartender, stuntman, driver, projectionist, fixer. He has the charm and confidence to make people comfortable, but underneath that is someone constantly reading the room. That skill becomes incredibly useful when his job is essentially solving problems other people cannot solve themselves. The film specifically positions him as a fixer doing odd jobs around Hollywood after his stunt career. This is too good for a Netflix movie, and I am glad I watched it in IMAX. This just feels like something made to be experienced theatrically first. The cast, craftsmanship, cinematography, sound, and whole cinematic texture deserve more than becoming something I casually click on at home. This limited IMAX run before its Netflix release, made catching it this way feel even more worth it. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The whole wife situation kind of reminded me of Fargo (1996), but this one twists the setup differently. Fargo has a husband hiring criminals to kidnap his own wife before everything spirals completely out of control. Here, Charlie hires the Verdusco brothers to kill Toni, changes his mind, then suddenly needs Cliff to somehow clean up the disaster he created. Same criminal stupidity of inviting dangerous people into your personal life, then realizing way too late that criminals do not come with an undo button. And the Verdusco brothers feel like much more rightful opponents for Cliff than the Manson Family. The Manson crew in Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood eventually becomes a playground for Cliff’s brutality. These guys actually feel competent enough and violent enough that I can believe Cliff might finally be dealing with people who can hurt him back. I also love the Filipino touch, especially how Cliff uses cultural connection as part of his fixer skill set. Once the brothers learn that he helped Filipinos during the war against the Japanese, their entire attitude changes. Their guard goes down. Suddenly they are sitting with him, drinking more of his booze, and treating him less like an outsider. That is where Cliff becomes dangerous in a completely different way. He does not always need to intimidate people. Sometimes he just needs to understand what matters to them, find the connection, make them comfortable, and let them lower their own defenses. Then when all that talking and maneuvering has finally built enough pressure, the violence arrives. That is where Fincher and Tarantino become such a fun combination for me. Fincher controls the pressure. Tarantino finally lets it explode. Verdict: Near Absolute Watched at SM Aura IMAX. TL;DR: I love how two filmmakers with such distinct identities can complement each other this well. Fincher gives Tarantino's world a darker and more calculated visual treatment without losing the humor, personality, and tension that make his writing so entertaining. Cliff Booth is such a fun character to follow because his intelligence and charisma are just as interesting as his capacity for violence. Elizabeth Debicki is also a highlight for me, both for her commanding presence and her character's refreshing perspective on movies. I especially enjoyed how much entertainment the film gets out of conversations alone, making the eventual action feel earned rather than excessive. This is the kind of filmmaking I would much rather experience inside a cinema than casually stream at home, and I'm glad I caught it in IMAX.
