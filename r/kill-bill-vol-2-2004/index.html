Kill Bill: Vol. 2 (2004) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-17
Action, Crime, Thriller · Directed by Quentin Tarantino · 137 min
620 reviews · 1,546 films logged · 160,269 words
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Kill Bill: Vol. 2 takes a very different approach from its predecessor, trading constant spectacle for restraint, dialogue, and character driven tension. The pacing is slower and more deliberate, allowing scenes to breathe and ideas to settle rather than overwhelm. Quentin Tarantino leans heavily on atmosphere, composition, and sound design to create unease, often letting silence and darkness do the work instead of action. The film feels more reflective and intimate, focusing on identity, consequence, and confrontation rather than pure revenge. While it may not deliver the same visual highs as Vol. 1, it closes the story with intention. Spoilers ahead. Read on if you’ve seen the film. From here on, plot details are discussed. Ang galing nung burying scene. It is just pitch black, and it lets the audience feel the dread of being buried alive. All you hear is the sound of thudding and rumbling, hard soil weighing down on you. It forces you to sit with the fear instead of distracting you with visuals. Medyo inantok ako sa Pai Mei training flashback at first, but it eventually paid off. Kailangan talaga siya to justify how The Bride survives and gains that punching strength needed to climb her way back from the coffin to the surface. In hindsight, necessary talaga siya kahit medyo dragging. Ang ganda nung silhouette shot with the red backdrop. It immediately reminded me of the cartoon opening of Avatar: The Last Airbender. Simple composition, strong color, and very striking. Quentin knows how to burn an image into your memory. As always, the fight between The Bride and Elle Driver shows Quentin flexing his mastery of coordination. The choreography, camera movement, and sound effects all work together seamlessly. Ramdam mo bawat suntok at sipa. Hindi lang siya action, may rhythm at weight. Totally different ang pacing dito compared sa Vol. 1. Mas tahimik, mas mabagal, at mas dialogue driven. Dito, words ang ginamit ni Quentin as weapons to build tension and toy with the audience instead of flashy visuals and nonstop action. It feels more like a character confrontation than an action showcase. Yung ending, medyo bitin for me. Ang bilis lang namatay ni Bill gamit yung Five Point Palm Exploding Heart Technique. But I don’t think yun talaga ang point ng duology. It feels like Bill just wanted to remind The Bride of who she really is, and that she shouldn’t pretend to be someone she’s not. At the same time, maybe Bill is just a selfish, possessive, and jealous murdering prick. Vol. 2 trades spectacle for meaning. It’s quieter, more reflective, and more interested in identity than revenge itself. It may not hit as hard visually as Vol. 1, but it closes the story in a way that feels intentional.
