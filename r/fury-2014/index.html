Fury (2014) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-11-17
607 reviews · 1,533 films logged · 157,261 words
Fury opens with a silhouette slicing through a blue washed battlefield, like a painting unfolding in real time. The moment we enter the tank, ramdam mo agad yung claustrophobia. Cramped interiors na parang kabaong. And the pyrotechnics? Feels brutally real. Bullets tearing through armor, sparks flying, tracers glowing like neon death. Parang sensory overload pero grounded. Parang first time ko makapanood ng tank vs. tank sequence. The whole thing plays out like a chess match, only with tanks as the pieces. There are gore shots that feel disturbingly real, with bombs and shells reaping heads off. Machine guns reaping limbs off. There are also moments where the guttural score swells, emphasizing their march to death feeling. Logan Lerman is the perfect POV characte: innocent, confused, morally lost. Lens thru a newbie. Day one pa lang, he’s already forced to kill, torture, and watch horrors, and you feel him slowly corroding inside. You see his hands shake from fear, then eventually shaking from anger. His journey is the real payoff. From a boy who couldn’t pull a trigger to a soldier willing to stand his ground even when hell is literally surrounding him. The last stand sequence feels like Only The Brave, soldiers making their final stand against a raging, unquenchable fire of destruction and imminent death. Fury is painful, gritty, and beautifully shot. Ang ganda ng shots na mata lang nila ang may ilaw while everything else is pitch black. Sa mata pa lang, kita mo na agad yung acting: fear, anger, determination, hopelessness, etc. Ang ganda rin ng silhouettes against fire, lalo na yung night scenes with orange and yellow haze, parang they’re fighting in hell. Every fire lit silhouette is perfection. The way the flames hit the lens, how it glows and bleeds into the dark, parang painting na growing hot and angrily red. In the end, what Brad Pitt considered his home ultimately became his tomb.
