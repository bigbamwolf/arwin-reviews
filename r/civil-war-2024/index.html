Civil War (2024) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-10-20
598 reviews · 1,524 films logged · 155,326 words
Grabe agad ang impact. The delayed explosion sound with the reflection of America’s map shaking, brilliant way to open the film. Ramdam mo agad na something’s broken, not just sa country, but sa mga taong nandito. Familiar faces all around: Nick Offerman (The Founder), Kirsten Dunst (Spider-Man trilogy), Cailee Spaeny (Romulus), Wagner Moura (Narcos), Stephen McKinley (Dune). Ang galing ni Kirsten Dunst dito, makikita mo yung trauma at fatigue ng isang veteran photojournalist. Yung moral struggle niya, “Should I capture this or stop it?” hits hard. I can feel her pain and trauma of capturing events like that. Naalala ko tuloy yung story nung photographer who took that haunting vulture and child photo. The dynamic between the veteran and the newbie lens is perfectly done. One driven by experience and exhaustion, the other by curiosity and adrenaline. It’s like watching idealism slowly turn into realism. Warm color grade despite the sadness of the story. May mga shots na dreamlike, almost surreal, na parang trapped memory sa gitna ng chaos. Nakakatense ang paggamit ng silence sa film na ito. The absence of sound, every echo, breath, and camera click feels magnified. Jesse Plemons is still a nightmare and menacing kahit short scene lang. Isa siya sa mga character na kahit ilang segundo lang, tatatak sa utak mo. It is the kind of movie where you think everything is okay, smooth sailing, then bam, one naïve kid messes everything up. Magandang metaphor yung forest wildfire kay Stephen, parang matchstick slowly burning to its end. Nice VFX shots with the military and solid production design selling the idea that this is a real warzone. Practical and special effects blend so well together that the chaos feels tangible. Gunfire feels claustrophobic and chaotic. Every bullet, explosion, and scream echoes like you are trapped inside the frame. Sa last act makikita mo yung hunger, grit, at pagiging risk taker ni Cailee Spaeny versus yung sawa na veteran ni Kirsten. Goes to show how photojournalists are as important as writers, sila ang nagdodocument ng history habang nilalagay ang sarili nila sa peligro. Every shot could be their last. Surgical clearing and invading sequence feels so raw and frightening. When Kirsten got shot, it felt like a symbolic passing of the torch. Cailee instantly takes the mantle, that shift in energy is goosebumps level. That ending, “I need a quote.” “Don’t let them kill me.” “That’ll do.” Perfection. Parang compressed meaning of the whole film in three lines. Another Alex Garland masterpiece.
