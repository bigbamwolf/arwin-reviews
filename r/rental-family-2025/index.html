Rental Family 2025

★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-29
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): A gentle, emotionally grounded film about care, boundaries, and the quiet cost of empathy. The film is focused on how connection forms. Performances feel natural and unforced, especially from the younger cast, giving the story a sincerity that carries through to its restrained ending. Instead of chasing devastation, it chooses reflection. Softer, calmer, and more human than expected. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ From not wanting to do the job and pretending to be someone for other people, he slowly shifts into caring too much and helping too much. What starts as pure performance becomes emotional labor. He crosses the line between acting and genuinely wanting to fix the lives of his clients. I’m especially sad for Mia. She got attached to Phillip so deeply. Watching that made me think, I wish I had a Phillip too when I was a child. Someone who believed in me, pushed me to dream big. Maybe I wouldn’t have grown into a scared and restrained adult, always afraid of taking too much risk and playing it safe. Brian’s situation is lonely in a different way. He lives multiple lives for other people, yet he doesn’t really have a life of his own. He’s present everywhere, but never truly present for himself. Is this really Shannon Gorman’s acting debut? She’s great. There’s a natural vulnerability there that never feels forced. Props to Aiko as well. From being a constant yes-man, she slowly reclaims her willpower. Seeing her finally say no feels empowering. I honestly thought the story would go in a darker direction. I expected him to want Mia to be her real daughter, struggle to detach once the job was done, and completely break. That version would’ve been heavier and more devastating. But instead, the director chose a self reflective path. Honesty over drama. Acceptance over tragedy. I still prefer my version because it would hit harder emotionally, but I understand why the film chose restraint. The ending is beautiful in a quiet way. Less dramatic, but more human.
