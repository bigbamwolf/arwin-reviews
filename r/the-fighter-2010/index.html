The Fighter (2010) review

★★½
Rated 2.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-31
Action, Biography, Drama · Directed by David O. Russell · 116 min
620 reviews · 1,546 films logged · 160,269 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): The performances carry the experience. Mark Wahlberg is physically disciplined and restrained, while Amy Adams brings warmth and emotional stability. Christian Bale completely dominates with a raw, uncomfortable performance that adds chaos and intensity. At its core, the film is less about boxing glory and more about family pressure, discipline, and finding control in a life constantly being pulled apart. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The first thing that really stood out for me was the smooth transition from a cinematic film look to a broadcast documentary style during the first fight. First time ko talaga makita yun in a boxing film. Wala niyan sa Rocky, Raging Bull, The Smashing Machine, or even The Iron Claw. It immediately grounded the film in realism, like you’re watching an actual televised bout unfold. Amy Adams is really beautiful here. My one and only Lois Lane! She brings a quiet strength and warmth to her role, acting as both an emotional anchor and a shield for Micky against the chaos surrounding him. First time ko rin nakita si Mark Wahlberg this ripped. Physically, he really committed to the role, and you can feel the discipline in how he plays Micky. He’s focused, reserved, and determined. Christian Bale as Dicky completely steals the film. Frail, erratic, and fully convincing as a crackhead. Their family dynamic is insane, and because of that, I honestly can’t fully blame Dicky for how he turned out. He’s a product of a toxic environment that keeps enabling and dragging him down, even when he’s trying to get back up. I just hope Micky would punch his mother in the face. They’re constantly sabotaging him. Instead of protecting his career, they manipulate his choices and treat his success like it’s theirs to control. That part really frustrated me. I like the filmmaking style, but I’m not a big fan of the family drama and constant feud. That said, I really appreciated Micky’s fight strategy. He lets his opponents wear themselves out, takes the punishment, absorbs the hits, and once they’re exhausted, that’s when he strikes and knocks them out. It’s patient, calculated, and smart! The ending works really well for me. It’s triumphant not just in terms of boxing, but also in Dicky’s sobriety. It doesn’t feel like a solo victory. It’s a literal team win for the brothers, both inside and outside the ring.
