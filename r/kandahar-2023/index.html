Kandahar 2023

★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-26
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): This movie is a solid cat and mouse war thriller elevated by strong cinematography and tension driven action. What really stands out to me is how well the film captures the desert environment. The wide landscape shots make the terrain feel both beautiful and oppressive, which gives the whole chase a more cinematic quality. What keeps me engaged most is the modern warfare atmosphere. The film uses scale, movement, and combat staging in a way that feels grounded and intense, even if the overall story is still familiar. For me, it works best as a visually sharp popcorn action movie. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Popcorn movie about a cat and mouse chase between a CIA asset and terrorists. It reminds me of films like Body of Lies, 13 Hours, and even parts of Sicario where the tension comes from constantly being hunted in unfamiliar territory. What really stood out to me is how the cinematographer clearly understood the assignment. The desert sequences are beautiful. It almost looks like a car commercial for long drives across harsh terrain. The wide landscape shots make the environment feel massive and oppressive at the same time. I especially like the night cinematography during Thomas versus helicopter sequence. That whole stretch builds tension so well. It feels inescapable, like death is just hovering above. The visuals keep shifting from natural night photography to night vision, then to thermal imaging. That black and white heat signature look adds another layer of intensity and makes the sequence feel more tactical. So raw and very modern warfare style! The rescue of Roman is also a strong action set piece. The use of real explosions and heavy gunfire makes it feel grounded and chaotic. What I found interesting is how it shows different Muslim factions fighting each other while all believing they are doing it for the same cause. That contradiction adds weight to the conflict. The final stretch in Kandahar is pure scale and spectacle. I cannot even imagine how many explosives were used to create those massive blasts! What a way to close the story. There are so many factions involved, and Ismail’s survival strategy stands out. He switches sides depending on what benefits him most. It reminded me of that idea in The 48 Laws of Power, adapting to whoever holds the advantage at the moment. Overall, it is a cliche action movie, but elevated by strong cinematography and well executed set pieces.
