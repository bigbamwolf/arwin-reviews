Tokyo Taxi (2025) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-21
668 reviews · 1,593 films logged · 181,283 words
Started as a fare. Ended as found family. Japan sepanx! This is exactly how I remember Japan, whether during the day or at night. The streets, buildings, roads, convenience stores, trains, and night lights all feel familiar. There is something comforting about just watching the taxi move through Tokyo and recognizing the atmosphere. And the taxi itself is such a simple but effective storytelling device. What starts as transportation slowly becomes a way of revisiting an entire life. The conversations feel natural. Sometimes they are funny. Sometimes awkward. Then suddenly something heartbreaking comes out. Both leads work really well too. The whole film depends on me believing that these two strangers could slowly become comfortable with each other, and I did. Their chemistry never feels forced. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I especially love the sequence about her love for her son and how she finally stands her ground. When she finds out that her husband is hitting him, she retaliates. There is something powerful about watching a mother who has tolerated so much finally draw the line when it comes to her child. And then the ending got me in tears. Not really because of some huge dramatic twist. It is the fact that these two people barely knew each other at the start of the ride, but somehow managed to forge a genuine friendship in such a short amount of time. A whole lifetime of memories gets shared inside one taxi. Then she gives all her savings to the driver. That really got me. It is such a simple gesture, but by that point it feels like gratitude, trust, and affection all arriving at once. The ride ends, but somehow it feels like these two strangers became part of each other’s lives. Verdict: Near Absolute Watched at SM North Cinema 3 during the Japanese Film Festival 2026
