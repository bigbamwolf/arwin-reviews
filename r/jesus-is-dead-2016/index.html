Jesus Is Dead (2016) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-22
670 reviews · 1,595 films logged · 182,983 words
Nothing brings a Filipino family together like death, insults, and a road trip. Super hilarious! The reactions inside the cinema were wild. Never a dull moment. This is definitely the kind of comedy that becomes even funnier when you are watching it with a crowd and everyone is losing it at the same time. The comedy comes from the stupidity, comedic timing, absurdity, and especially the banter between the characters. Somehow, the whole cast has their own way of being funny. Nobody needs to become the designated comic relief because the humor naturally comes from how this dysfunctional family talks to each other. This is also my first time watching a Bisaya comedy, and it feels quite different from the Tagalog comedy I am used to. The humor here feels drier and more blunt. Lots of sarcasm, condescending remarks, bashing, teasing, and saying completely outrageous things with a straight face. Like Little Miss Sunshine (2006), there is a sense of reconnection within the family and slowly discovering that each of them carries emotional baggage. But this one feels much more Filipino in how they reconnect. They insult each other, argue, make fun of each other, and barely know how to communicate properly, but the road trip becomes their only time and way to finally open up. I also love how the film breaks the stigma around people with Down syndrome. Hubert is never reduced into someone I am supposed to pity. He is funny, stubborn, capable, and can hold up by himself. The filmmakers even added his getting lost sequence specifically to show that he could independently find his way to the port, which makes that part even better. I love Iyay saying she has three sons instead of two too. No dramatic acceptance speech. She simply calls Jude her son. That's it. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The part that really got me emotionally was when Hubert went missing. Iyay admits that she thought Hubert would be the "pabigat" of the family, only for it to turn out to be the complete opposite. He knows how to take care of himself. Meanwhile, everyone else is carrying their own mess. The person the family probably worried about the most turns out to be one of the least problematic among them. Verdict: Well Made Watched at Gateway Cineplex Cinema 12 during the QCinema International Film Festival
