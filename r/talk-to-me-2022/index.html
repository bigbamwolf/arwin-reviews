Talk to Me (2022) review

★½
Rated 1.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-02
654 reviews · 1,580 films logged · 175,559 words
All fun and games until someone starts smashing their head into a table. I actually like the idea that the possession becomes almost like a drug to them. They keep chasing the feeling, showing it off to one another, testing the limits, and getting more reckless every time. Peer pressure! Then the movie suddenly reminds me that they are playing with something they do not understand. There are some really graphic and disturbing surprises. The first stabbing incident already caught me off guard, then the kid banging his head on the table took it even further. The violence is effective, but I was more impressed by how graphic and uncomfortable it became than genuinely frightened by it. I also did not really connect with Mia, which probably limited how much the emotional side worked for me. Once the horror becomes more personal to her, I understood what the film was doing, but I was never deeply invested in her. It is an okay film, especially as a possession movie with a clever addiction angle. But compared with Bring Her Back (2025), this feels much less complete to me. Bring Her Back had stronger characters, more emotional weight, a scarier atmosphere, better filmmaking, and a story I connected with much more. Knowing this came from the same directors actually makes the difference more obvious. Talk to Me feels like the raw version of what they could do. Bring Her Back feels like they took that same instinct for disturbing horror and gave it much more emotional depth. Verdict: Hollow
