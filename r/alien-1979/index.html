Alien 1979

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-07
Atmospheric start. Calm before the storm. Ramdam mo agad yung unease kahit wala pang nangyayari. Ganda ng production design ng ship nila. Very tangible, futuristic with an analogue touch, parang sa Star Wars. Yung hallways remind me of the Millennium Falcon and even the Death Star. In contrast, yung alien ship feels completely different. Parang same texture ng interior ng ship ni Superman sa Man of Steel. Organic, biomechanical, cold, and alien talaga. Maganda ang sound mixing. Hindi kami naka surround sound pero parang naka ganun yung experience nung pinanood ko sa TV. You can really feel the space, the silence, and the sudden bursts of sound. This film also features early motion control photography with blue screen compositing using practical models, pioneered by Star Wars. Visually impressive pa rin siya for its time, especially how seamless the models are integrated into the shots. Maganda ang cinematography in the dark. Ang ganda ng lighting and contrast. Ridley Scott did a very good job selling the difference in scale between objects, whether it’s the ship versus space, or humans versus the alien. Laging may sense kung gaano ka kaliit at kavulnerable yung tao. I can hear Kiri in the way a young Sigourney Weaver talks here. Familiar cadence and presence. Medyo slow burn ang pacing, even with a few jump scares here and there. It takes its time to build dread instead of relying purely on shock. I already knew Ash is the bad guy since I watched Romulus before this. Pero honestly, halata naman si Ash kahit wala kang prior knowledge. Hindi lang siya curious as a science man. Masyado siyang halata with his unjustifiable decisions. May something off agad na nakakainis sa kanya. The score feels like a hybrid of space opera and horror flick. May moments of grandeur, then biglang unsettling at eerie. The third act is pure chaos. Handheld shots mixed with strobe lights create disorientation. You really feel the danger and gravity of the situation, especially with the impending self destruction while being hunted by a Xenomorph that could appear at any corner. It reminds me of playing the old Half-Life game as a kid, where you’re navigating dark industrial corridors, alarms blaring, lights flickering, and danger always just out of frame. You’re constantly moving forward, not because you want to, but because staying still means death. Good film, but nothing groundbreaking for me. What stood out the most are the close up shots of Ripley and the lighting and color grading during the final act. That’s where the film feels the most alive and intense.
