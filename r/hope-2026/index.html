Hope 2026

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-08-30
The first act feels like it was directed by Michael Bay. Instead of Decepticons, just replace them with monsters or creatures. I really love the car chase scenes. Such epic, breathtaking shots. I was on the edge of my seat. Speaking of Michael Bay, there are also a lot of tilt-up shots here. Whether intentional or not, it feels like a nice little nod to his visual language. The production design showed the destruction that the monsters are capable of even though they were not shown early on (which I think was a good decision). Their absence actually makes them more threatening because the ruined environments tell the story for them. I was really immersed in the rural setting of South Korea. The score gave every scene and frame an electric energy. I felt the urgency and danger. It is accompanied by really good sound mixing too. Bullets were firing all around the IMAX theater, and every piece of debris could be felt. The acting really helped sell the danger too. I felt their pain, panic, and exhaustion. But it can be hilarious too. How can a tense and bloody action thriller (props to the makeup and prosthetics team) be hilarious at the same time? Ang galing! Then from the second act to the third act, it feels as if the director suddenly shifted from Michael Bay to Steven Spielberg. Once the aliens are revealed, the film becomes less about surviving monstrous attacks and more interested in understanding what they are, why they are here, and adding emotional depth to the story. The creatures stop being purely monsters and become part of a larger emotional and thematic idea. I should have given this 4 stars because the first act had me completely locked in, but the shift in direction kind of removed me from the immersion. I personally wish the director had committed to that ferocious Michael Bay-style energy all the way through. The first act established such a strong cinematic identity that I wanted the rest of the movie to keep riding that same adrenaline.
