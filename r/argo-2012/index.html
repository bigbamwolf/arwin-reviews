Argo (2012) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-22
Biography, Drama, History · Directed by Ben Affleck · 120 min
624 reviews · 1,550 films logged · 162,502 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): What pulls me in most is the concept itself. It is a very engaging mix of Hollywood filmmaking and espionage, with tension that keeps building until the final release. That contrast gives the story a fun hook while also making every scene feel like it is balancing performance and danger at the same time. The editing and pacing keep the pressure building, especially as the mission gets tighter and more fragile. For me, it works as a very engaging thriller because the tension comes less from action and more from the fear that one small mistake could collapse the whole illusion. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ It is interesting how the film shows the process of making a movie, but used as a cover for a CIA operation. Funny thing is, the fake film is basically a Star Wars rip off set in Iran. The whole situation reminded me of 13 Hours: The Secret Soldiers of Benghazi, but this one feels more stressful for me. Instead of direct combat, they are constantly living a lie. Every interaction feels risky, especially during the airport sequence. That's where it really got intense. One wrong move and everything could fall apart. When they finally get out of Iranian airspace, sobrang triumphant ng feeling. In this story, the CIA is clearly positioned as the good guys. I did not expect Ben Affleck to deliver like this. The direction is really solid and the editing plays a huge role in building tension. The pacing during the final stretch is especially effective.
