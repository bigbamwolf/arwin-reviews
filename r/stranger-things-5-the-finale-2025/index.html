Stranger Things 5: The Finale 2025

★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-14
Feels like an Inception x Transformers: Dark of the Moon x The Dark Knight Rises. I went in blind here with only the memory of S1-S4. I just watched it like a regular movie and not an episode. Hopper knocking on the sensory deprivation tank immediately reminded me of the kick in Inception. That feeling of “this is the trigger to wake up” gets reinforced even more when they enter the room after the theater curtain closes. Parang layered realities collapsing into each other, same visual and narrative language of crossing thresholds between worlds. The merging of Earth and the Upside Down really feels like Transformers: Dark of the Moon, where two realities attempt to occupy the same physical space. Similar vibes can be felt in films like Man of Steel with Krypton’s World Engine, and Annihilation where the shimmer slowly consumes Earth. Jamie Campbell Bower as Henry is genuinely menacing. Parang mala Anakin energy here. That tragic villain arc where raw power, isolation, and resentment curdle into hatred. He commands the screen every time he appears. Yung characterization ni Henry dito, apart from what we knew in S4 feels very similar to demon narratives in Demon Slayer. Where evil is constantly softened by trauma explanations. For me, that made it anticlimactic. Not every villain needs sympathy. Ito yung ayaw ko sa Netflix style finales. They spent so much time building Henry as the villain, then at the very last minute sabihin na hindi pala siya all along. Walang finality kung sino ba talaga ang ultimate evil. Biglang balik sa Season 2 logic na ang Mind Flayer pa rin ang tunay na villain. And then it gets defeated too easily. Walang real stakes akong naramdaman. Kung hindi ko pa nakita yung spoiler, mas disappointing siguro. It felt like the twist existed just for shock value, not narrative necessity. A lot of the dialogue here is pure fan service. Callbacks, emotional closure, and full circle moments for characters who’ve been around since season one. For long time fans, it mostly works. CGI works here because it’s mixed with practical effects. The physicality blends well with digital. One moment that did stood out was Winona Ryder beheading Vecna while flashes of his crimes play. That felt earned. That was a clean emotional full stop. "You messed with the wrong family" is a memorable line. The Mike and El closure also worked well. The callbacks from Season 1 up to now, the flashbacks, Mike’s reaction, and everyone else witnessing it. That sequence felt surreal in a good way, like watching childhood finally end. Sadie Sink is still a standout. Her intensity and emotional range are incredible. She’s consistently operating on a different level. I really hope she gets a massive breakout role after this. And then there’s Will revealing that El is still alive. That trope instantly reminded me of films where the protagonist is believed dead in the third act but survives, like The Dark Knight Rises, Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows Part 2. It’s that classic emotional fake out to give both despair and hope in one move. Overall, the finale delivers spectacle, callbacks, and emotional beats, but fumbles narrative clarity and villain payoff.
