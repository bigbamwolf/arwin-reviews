Joy (2015) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-20
Biography, Drama · Directed by David O. Russell · 124 min
616 reviews · 1,542 films logged · 158,812 words
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): This is a grounded startup story that takes its time building empathy for an underdog boxed in by financial pressure, family dynamics, and self doubt. The film focuses less on cinematic flair and more on process: rejection, persistence, and the uncomfortable stretch between idea and execution. It captures how starting something from nothing is exhausting, repetitive, and often humiliating before it ever looks successful. What stands out is how honest it feels about risk taking, selling, and resilience, without turning the journey into instant inspiration porn. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The first act was slow, mainly because the film is carefully establishing her struggles so the audience can genuinely root for the underdog. It takes its time showing how boxed in she is, emotionally and financially. Natuwa ako nung nagmamanufacture na sila. You can feel the shift from idea to execution, from dreaming to actually risking something tangible. Ganda ng scenes about rejection. Paulit ulit siyang tinatanggihan, but instead of glorifying instant success, the film sits with the discomfort and humiliation that comes with trying to sell something no one believes in yet. Then ito nanaman tayo sa mortgage ng house, parang sa Tetris at The Founder. That familiar startup trope where personal security is sacrificed for a shot at something bigger. It’s reckless, but also very real. Grabe yung weight nung hindi nabenta yung 50k units nya. Napa “legit ba to?” ako. Ang akala ko nananaginip lang sya. And the fact that she went back to the studio to do the selling herself shows pure relentlessness. No ego, just survival mode. I think I would do the same if my back is against the wall. Yung TV selling live on air feels like an old version of what we now know as TikTok live selling. Same pressure, same energy, just without the algorithms. You have to call instead of checking out your yellow basket. I love her best friend. She is so supportive, parang wingman talaga. Not just emotional support, but someone who keeps believing when the Joy is already doubting herself. Nice to see Dascha Polanco again from Orange is the New Black. Sad yung namatay yung lola niya. Siya talaga yung tunay na naniwala sa potential ni Joy, even when Joy couldn’t see it herself. About her sister naman, ang hirap talaga pag nangengeelam ang kapamilya sa negosyo. It blurs boundaries and adds emotional weight to already difficult decisions. Ang tindi ng drama nung bankruptcy scene. Her world, her identity, everything she attached her self worth to comes crashing down all at once. Maganda rin yung naging resolve niya with the patent issue mixed with embezzlement. The film quietly lays out business lessons without being preachy: risk taking, kapal ng mukha to sell, toughness in negotiations, don’t trust anyone blindly, and the importance of hiring a patent lawyer to protect what you built. Overall, clean execution. Not very cinematic, but a very real portrayal of business startups. It shows that success isn’t glamorous, it’s exhausting, humiliating, and often lonely before it becomes anything else.
