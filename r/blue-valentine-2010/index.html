Blue Valentine (2010) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-12
Drama, Romance · Directed by Derek Cianfrance · 112 min
609 reviews · 1,535 films logged · 157,567 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I really love how the lens choices and lighting separate the two timelines, with one feeling warmer and more hopeful, and the other colder and heavier. What stays with me most is how human the whole thing feels. The film understands that love is not just about falling into it, but about what happens when that feeling changes over time. I can feel both the longing for a simple, peaceful life and the ache of wanting something more. For me, that heavy realism is what makes the film painful and honest. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I always thought Ryan Gosling mostly just plays himself in many of his films. But here he actually looks and acts very different. His usual charm is almost gone in the present timeline. Instead he feels tired, frustrated, and emotionally drained. I love the lens choices between the two timelines. The present scenes use lenses that create a deeper sense of space and depth, which makes the environment feel heavier and more melancholic. The contrast is stronger and the mood feels colder. The past scenes on the other hand use wider lenses and softer lighting. Everything feels brighter, warmer, and more optimistic. The visual language alone already tells the story of a relationship moving from hope to exhaustion. Dean is clearly fighting to save their marriage while Cindy is slowly drifting away. I honestly felt Ryan’s character deeply because he is also deprived of intimacy. At some point it feels like he is begging for affection. When he says he is a good husband and a father, I actually believe him. Especially when it was revealed that he is only the stepfather but still fully committed to the child. Mas lalo akong naawa sa kanya. Cindy sometimes made me think she should have been more grateful for what he was trying to give. The tension finally explodes in the hospital scene. That moment is painful because once those words are spoken, they cannot be taken back. The way the film cuts back to memories of their early love makes it even worse. Why show the beautiful past when the present is already broken? That contrast is brutal. But I guess that is the sad reality of life. Love can slowly fade even when it once felt permanent. Staying in love is not automatic. It is something people have to keep choosing, and sometimes one person stops choosing. I think this is the best acting I have seen from Ryan Gosling. I can definitely feel his character's pain. The moment that really broke me was the child running after her father. Mas naawa talaga ako sa bata. By the end I felt parts of both Dean and Cindy living inside me. I understand Dean wanting a simple and peaceful family life. But I also understand Cindy’s desire for something bigger than what she thinks is possible. That conflict feels truly human.
