Disclosure Day 2026

★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-06-11
Right from the start, I was already impressed with the blocking, continuous shots, and cinematography. It feels like Steven Spielberg simply flexing his filmmaking skills. Even ordinary scenes are staged with purpose and movement. Early on, I also got some Sense8 vibes because of the whole interconnectedness aspect. It immediately added intrigue and mystery to the story. The film works best as a thriller. The cat and mouse aspect keeps the pacing flowing smoothly, while the mystery keeps piling up. There is also a certain technology here that reminded me of Possession and even Ino's jutsu because of the way it operates and affects people. One idea that really stood out to me is the suggestion that empathy might be the next requirement for evolution. I do not think I have encountered that concept before, and I found it fascinating. What I appreciate most is that the film is more interested in humanity's reaction (especially religion) rather than providing all the answers. In that sense, it feels like a more serious version of Don't Look Up (2021). Also, early nominations from me for Best Actress for Emily Blunt and Best Musical Score for John Williams. Both are outstanding here.
