Wuthering Heights (2026) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-11
618 reviews · 1,544 films logged · 159,192 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I experience this as a tragic love story where the music and visuals pull me into a romance that feels beautiful, obsessive, and doomed. The emotional weight sits front and center, with atmosphere and imagery amplifying longing, jealousy, and heartbreak without overshadowing the human core. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The score immediately pulls me in. It feels thrilling, dramatic, hopeful, sorrowful, and romantic. It constantly shifts with the emotional state of the characters, making the love story feel grand but also doomed in some cases. I can feel the weight of longing and heartbreak through the music alone. The gothic atmosphere is present, but it feels more like texture. The mood is grounded, allowing the emotions to stay front and center. The cinematographer is clearly flexing here. Whether it is intimate close ups, wide landscape shots, or horizon framed compositions, every image feels with purpose. The visual language makes the romance feel intense and personal. The intimacy between the characters is what drives the film. It feels erotic but fragile. Margot Robbie looks stunning and naturally regal, fitting perfectly into this royalty setting. Story wise, I read it as a tragic mix of rags to riches ambition (The Favourite 2018) and revenge, similar in spirit to The Count of Monte Cristo (2002), blended with the aching “too late” romance energy I associate with Past Lives (2023). The emotional tension is heightened by the production design & lighting. I notice how the walls shift toward red tones during moments of rage, jealousy, and passion, visually amplifying the characters’ inner turmoil. The ending reminds me of lovers bound so tightly that one cannot exist without the other. When one fades, the other follows. Overall, I experience Wuthering Heights as an expertly crafted tragic love story. The music, cinematography, acting, and design work together to immerse me in a romance that is beautiful, obsessive, and destined to fall apart. It is intimate, emotional, and visually expressive without losing sight of the human core.
