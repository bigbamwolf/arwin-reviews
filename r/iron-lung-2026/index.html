Iron Lung 2026

★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-25
I appreciate the choice of aspect ratio because it gives the image a few extra vertical frames, making the cramped submarine feel taller yet somehow even more suffocating. Instead of cinematic openness, the added headroom ironically emphasizes how little real space there is to move. The aesthetic reminds me of old digital machines like in Time Lapse 2014, full of rusty interiors and primitive technology. They also both use a green screen terminal or green phosphor monitor. Those were used in early computers, giving that industrial analog computer vibe. The constant close up shots of either the character or tiny mechanical details make me feel claustrophobic, like I’m trapped inside the sub with him and there’s no escape at all. It honestly feels like a low budget escape room video project from YouTube, more like an experimental concept exercise than a fully realized cinematic experience. Watching the film feels like staring at blank X ray photos without knowing what they mean. I keep waiting for interpretation or payoff, but the visuals stay cryptic without emotional translation. The actor is not believable for me. I cannot feel the pain, frustration, or fear, which is a big problem for a film that relies almost entirely on performance. It became painful to watch, no one in the cinema even reacted at all. Some even looked like they fell asleep, which unfortunately matched how disengaged I felt.
