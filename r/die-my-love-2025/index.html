Die My Love 2025

★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-12-29
The film starts energetic, chaotic, and completely unhinged. Right away, there’s a sense of danger, and I was honestly terrified for the baby from the very beginning. The use of circular anamorphic lenses gives everything a dreamy, unstable feeling, like reality is constantly slipping. There’s foreshadowing everywhere, and it never lets you feel safe. One of the most striking moments is when the ink mixes with her breast milk. It’s subtle but powerful, clearly signaling that this is a story about womanhood, and motherhood. I noticed a lot of night for day photography. Sometimes it works beautifully, especially with silhouettes and composition. Other times, it looks fake, similar to what Jordan Peele did in Nope. But here, I think it’s intentional. It adds to the idea that they no longer know what is day or night, what is real or not. Everything feels disoriented. I understand the postpartum angle, but some choices really confused me. Bringing in a puppy when you already have a newborn feels irresponsible. And Jackson not touching his wife at all? Grace is clearly still attractive, so the emotional distance feels deliberate and strange. What really bothered me is Jackson’s sudden character shift. He starts off as a decent guy, then suddenly becomes careless. Leaving the baby alone just to go for a drive is insane. That alone made them both feel reckless. At some point, I stopped believing this was just postpartum depression. Killing a dog, self harm through mirrors, scratching walls, destroying the bathroom. This feels far beyond that. At this level, she should have been hospitalized immediately. The risk to the baby was too high. Instead of addressing it properly, they even got married. Jackson honestly comes off as low IQ here. I felt bad for him though. He’s clearly helpless, watching someone he loves deteriorate. But it’s also frustrating that Grace is released from the facility when she’s clearly not well. That decision felt irresponsible. I’m not really a fan of the ending, but thank God the baby is fine. That was the only real relief in the entire experience. Disturbing, stressful, and intentionally uncomfortable. The film wants you unsettled, and it succeeds, even if some choices are hard to accept.
