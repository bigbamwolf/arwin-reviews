Dumb Money (2023) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-27
Biography, Comedy, Drama · Directed by Craig Gillespie · 105 min
624 reviews · 1,550 films logged · 162,502 words
The Big Short, but this time the little guys fought back. As a former stock trader, I have witnessed this kind of hype in the Philippine stock market with DITO before they launched the new SIM. With terms like “to the moon,” “diamond hands,” and “HOLD,” this is a pretty familiar environment for me. The memes, conviction, echo chambers, and people constantly convincing each other not to sell immediately brought me back to that kind of trading environment. This kind of cult following is like a double edged sword. It is good for hyping, but bad for making the right decision because it creates analysis paralysis about when to sell and when to hold once you’ve gained an enormous amount. The paranoia kicks in because you never know when the herd will break up and press the sell button. You can be sitting on life changing gains while still thinking, what if it goes even higher? But at the same time, everyone else is probably asking themselves the exact same question. I also heard about this GameStop and Robinhood thing before, but I knew that by the time it reached my ears, it was probably already too late and the top was about to pop. That is one thing trading taught me. Once everybody around you is already talking about the same stock, I immediately become suspicious of how much upside is actually left. I like the movement and the principles embodied and stood by the followers. It really feels like a revolution against the whales, and it shows that as small as retail traders are individually, when combined, they can really move stock prices. I also like that it shows the hubris of the hedge fund managers, dismissing retail traders like they were just some kind of ants that couldn’t affect them no matter what. They felt invincible until they lost billions. That reversal is probably the most satisfying part for me because the people they considered insignificant suddenly became impossible to ignore. This is my favorite Paul Dano role. He has that quiet, ordinary guy presence that works perfectly here because he doesn’t feel like some larger than life Wall Street genius. All the cast also did great. The feeling is the same thing I felt with The Big Short (2015), but from almost the opposite side of the battlefield. That film made me feel like I was watching a small group realize the financial system was broken before everyone else did. Dumb Money gives me that same excitement of watching money, ego, risk, and Wall Street collide, except this time I was rooting for ordinary retail traders trying to make the institutions finally feel their presence. Verdict: Absolute Cinema Watched on Netflix.
