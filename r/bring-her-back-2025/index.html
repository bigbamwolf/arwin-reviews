Bring Her Back 2025

★★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-10-29
Simula pa lang hindi ko na tinuloy, pinagpabukas ko na. Nakakatakot agad sa simula palang eh haha. My scaredy ass cannot! The directors did not waste time on slow burn. You’re thrown straight into the mess. The film is like Weapons meets Parasite. It deals with the illusion of safety inside domestic spaces. Yung idea na ang isang tahanan, which should protect, is actually the site of danger, secrets, and suppressed power dynamics. Dito sa film, that same structure repeats itself. The foster home looks calm, almost healing sa unang tingin, but the longer you stay, the more it feels alive: watching, waiting, and hiding something. The half rotate upside down shot when they arrive at the foster home immediately reminds me of Midsommar’s foreshadowing. Alam mong A24 DNA talaga. The moment the camera flipped, I already felt the unease. Something’s not right, and that’s what this film does best. It makes you feel danger even before it shows it. Mapapatamang hinala ka nalang talaga. Yung music choice ng “Alive” by Empire of the Sun was brilliant. The line “Can you help me see? (They won’t get right) Loving every minute ‘cause you make me feel so alive” hits different once you understand the film’s ending. From the start, I thought tungkol ito kay Piper dahil bulag sya but yun pala it already hinted at Laura’s blindness and her need to “see”. Not just her child again, but the truth she’s denying. Laura, the foster mother, is very effective at being creepy perfectly mixing warmth and unsettling. Medyo creepy ang kindness tbh, alam mong may tinatago. She carries that fake safety you can’t quite trust. Laura’s manipulation was masterful. Divide and conquer, laying groundwork, planting false evidence that will serve her side of story. Really manipulative! I literally had to remove my earphones doon sa Andy and Ollie scene. Alam kong may mangyayaring masama. Who leaves a knife with a child in the middle of tension like that? The musical score and sound design make every second worse: water drops, shower hits, the echo of rain. It all reflects Andy’s trauma perfectly. Creepy prosthetics and makeup work elevate the horror. Really showing what an abomination looks like! Especially the gory skin, teeth, and mouth design. The last time I looked away from the screen this hard was during 127 Hours. I can handle gore, but when paired with perfect sound design, it becomes too real. Nice close ups with the eyes. Eyes can really tell a lot, and the intimacy of the hand shots shows how personal the pain is. The director isn’t shy about lingering on those close ups, giving every gesture emotional weight. Lens choice is on point, too. Something I learned about after watching the film: each main character was shot gamit ang iba’t ibang lenses: Laura was shot with lenses that make the background look swirly and circular, giving her scenes a slightly dizzy, dreamlike feel that matches her emotional chaos. Piper’s shots use lenses that blur and glow a bit more, making things look softer and less defined. Billy’s scenes use clearer lenses with a more natural look, showing how he’s the most grounded and stable sa kanilang lahat despite what Laura was trying to paint him with. For Oliver, they used slow zooms that slowly tighten in, to make it feel like predatory intent. That mirror shot in the car side mirror where Laura’s reflection shows how broken she already is after killing Andy and Wendy. This is a film about morality: how far will a mother’s love go before it becomes something else entirely? It’s horror and drama rolled into one. The empathy I felt for Laura as someone who lost a child too. There’s something really disturbing with witchcraft samahan mo pa ng themes of grief. The film uses that combination to mirror a mother’s desperation to rewrite reality. Pwede ring metaphor ng faith and control. Witchcraft here isn’t just supernatural. It is Laura’s desperate attempt to regain control where she has none. This film is about getting lost inside your own grief, and realizing that the thing you’re trying to bring back might destroy you. Ito ang malalim na palaisipan: when faith fails to give answers, will we create our own gods to justify our pain? How far someone will go to undo trauma, even if it means inviting darkness in? The horror ends not through death but through acceptance. That same “Alive” lyric from the opening finally makes sense. Piper helped Laura “see,” helped her “get it right.” She can’t bring her daughter back, and that realization, that moment of letting go, broke me. I was like, how can a horror film make you feel wrecked and emotionally damaged? This film is that good. It’s terrifying in form, but deeply human in heart. By the time the credits rolled, I was in tears. This is the kind of film na babalikan mo not to be scared again, but to trace where grief turned into madness. I will definitely rewatch and recommend it to others, not just as a horror but as an emotional case study of love and loss.
