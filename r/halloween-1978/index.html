Halloween (1978) review

★½
Rated 1.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-02
643 reviews · 1,569 films logged · 169,802 words
Hot take: Leatherface is scarier than Michael Myers, and it's not even close. The opening sequence is immersive with the POV shot and that dreadful music. Right away, I feel like I am being pulled into the killer’s perspective. The famous score is instantly recognizable. Even my daughter said she heard it from TikTok. That alone says a lot about how deeply that theme has entered pop culture. But I do not know. I did not care much about the characters, at least not as much as I cared about the ones in The Texas Chain Saw Massacre (1974). The first part worked much better for me. I actually wish the whole movie stayed closer to that creepy, stalking atmosphere. The chase is not that intense for me. Michael Myers feels more like a stalker than an unhinged crazy person. I had the same kind of disconnect with Scream (1996), although I do not remember that movie well enough anymore to compare them properly. I think The Texas Chain Saw Massacre works better for me because everything feels more unpredictable, physical, and believable. Leatherface and that family feel completely unhinged. I never know what they are going to do next, and that unpredictability makes me genuinely scared for the characters. I can appreciate the stalking, the score, and the atmosphere, but I am more aware that I am watching a slasher movie instead of feeling trapped inside one. And once Michael starts surviving things that should obviously take him down, the fear drops even more for me. Getting stabbed multiple times and continuing like it is nothing starts feeling ridiculous and unbelievable rather than terrifying. Jamie Lee Curtis did not really stand out to me either. So when the movie eventually becomes more focused on Laurie surviving Michael, I do not have the same emotional attachment that I had watching Sally desperately trying to escape in The Texas Chain Saw Massacre. That is probably the biggest difference for me. The Texas Chain Saw Massacre made me fear for the people. Halloween made me appreciate the filmmaking, but I was never genuinely frightened by Michael Myers, and I eventually found myself getting bored. Verdict: Hollow TL;DR: I love the creepy opening, POV shots, and iconic score, but the stalking atmosphere eventually gives way to chase sequences that never really terrified me. Compared with The Texas Chain Saw Massacre (1974), I found the characters less engaging, the danger less believable, and Michael Myers surviving ridiculous injuries more frustrating than frightening. Jamie Lee Curtis didn't leave much of an impression on me either. I appreciate the filmmaking and its influence on the genre, but Leatherface made me genuinely fear for the characters, while Michael Myers eventually just bored me.
