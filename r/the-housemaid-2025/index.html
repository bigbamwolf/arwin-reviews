The Housemaid 2025

★★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-13
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Watching this a second time gives me a very different appreciation for how the film is built. Instead of relying only on surprise, it becomes more about performance, control, and how carefully certain moments are planted early on. I end up noticing more of Amanda Seyfried’s choices. What I like is that the film fully commits to its own logic and carries that confidence all the way through. Even without the shock factor, it remains effective, well presented, and easy to get pulled into again. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Watching this for the second time hits differently because I already know the twist. The story plays in a new way now. I appreciate Amanda Seyfried’s performance more, especially that quiet “thank you” at the beginning. The line carries more weight because I now understand what she actually mean that. Knowing the twist makes that moment feel liberating for her. The whole plan unfolds smoothly, which some people might see as self serving or too convenient. But honestly it still works for me. The film commits to the idea and follows through with it. Amanda’s crying scene when she gets kicked out also lands differently now. The first time it felt like pure defeat. Watching it again, it feels triumphant. It is like she already knows the game she is playing and where it will eventually lead. What surprised me is that I caught myself reacting the same way I did during the first watch. Even though I know what will happen, certain moments still trigger the same emotions. I honestly do not understand the hate the film gets. For me it was well presented and effective in telling its story. Maybe not everyone sees it as well made, but both me and my wife really enjoyed it.
