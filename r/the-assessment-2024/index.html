The Assessment 2024

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-12-27
The production design immediately stands out. Especially the architecture and color of the house. Very unique. Yung mosaic windows talaga yung pinaka tumatak. Ang galing umarte ni Elizabeth Olsen. She carries a lot of the film emotionally. Day two pa lang, weird na agad. The assessor starts pushing buttons. Psychological, emotional, parental. Ramdam mo yung intrusion. The musical score is great. May mga voices and breathing sounds that reminded me of Dark on Netflix. Mixed with percussions that feel like a mystery puzzle or a psychological thriller. Yung title cards for the days are honestly painful to look at. The color choice is harsh and distracting. As a parent, my instincts kept kicking in. And honestly, bagsak rin sila for me. But at the same time, no one is ever really prepared to be a parent. That discomfort feels intentional. Ang galing ng acting ng assessor when she switches to childlike behavior. It made me think she might have a split personality. Very unsettling, very effective. The world building is mostly in the script. Hindi masyadong shown visually, but you can imagine the dystopia through dialogue and context. It works in a subtle way. The argument about population control makes sense. Those who witnessed famine and calamity are naturally against adding more people. They see having a child as vain because it consumes limited resources. Meanwhile, those who didn’t experience those horrors don’t think twice about conserving or giving up the possibility of having a child. That moral divide is one of the strongest parts of the film. Ang ganda ng color palette during the greenhouse fire scene. Yellow and brown cinematic tones. It reminded me of the first few frames in Dune Part Two. Very striking. Having a child, in the context of this film, means exposing everything about yourself. Your fears, guilt, selfishness, and capacity to love. Behind all the assessor’s facade, she is also human. And the performance really sells that reveal. The twist that no one has actually passed the assessment in six years is strong. The system exists not to approve parents, but to give people hope. To teach them how to love a child. In the end, the three of them each make a choice. One chooses to stop the guilt. One chooses to live with a lie. One chooses to face reality beyond comfort. Deeply philosophical. Uncomfortable in a good way.
