Spider-Man: Brand New Day (2026) review

★★★½
Rated 3.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-07-31
Action, Adventure, Sci-Fi · Directed by Destin Daniel Cretton · 145 min
598 reviews · 1,524 films logged · 155,326 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I came in expecting another fun Spider Man movie and ended up getting something much more personal than I anticipated. It doesn't always hit the emotional highs it aims for, but I really appreciated how willing it was to slow down and let its characters breathe. It left me pleasantly surprised, even if it didn't completely win me over. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ No amount of trailers hyped me for this, and quite frankly, I didn't expect that much. Mystery and detective vibes. The theme is quite dark and mature in its tone. It feels more like character drama than a blockbuster flick. Actually, the lighting and color grading in some scenes kind of remind me of Obsession (2025). It feels like all the Spider-Man movies rolled into one. The emo and lonely and emotionally broken Spider-Man from Spider-Man 3, the love story of The Amazing Spider-Man, and the grounded coming-of-age journey of the MCU films. This movie is an intimate story about Peter's struggles rather than a superhero adventure. Jean Grey showing off Omega-level powers. His motivations kind of feel like Magneto's. The third act gives the emotional "core memory" plot like in Inside Out 2 mixed with the defeat the enemy with power of love and understanding in Thunderbolts. Peter Parker is no longer a kid here. He has accepted what he cannot control and moved forward with what he can control. He's also way past the mentoring kind of story. Instead, he feels like an equal to Yelena and Bruce, and should I say, even more mature than Frank Castle. Speaking of Frank, their banter was one of the highlights of the film. It perfectly balanced Frank's aggression with Peter's wit and humor. The audience reactions in the cinema were wild, especially during the romance scenes, but yeah, they didn't hit quite work for me. The ending should have been as painful as Past Lives, but it didn't fully land. The conclusion of MJ and Peter's relationship is like Peter and Gamora's. They naturally drift apart because there's only unrequited love left between them. It's an enjoyable ride with lots of emotional ups and downs. I just think my standards have naturally become higher after watching so many great films, which is probably why I can't rate this one any higher. Coolest and simplest low-budget end credits sequence with a lot of impact.
