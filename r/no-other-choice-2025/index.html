No Other Choice 2025

★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-31
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): At first, that unpredictability is intriguing, but over time it can feel disorienting rather than purposeful. What grounds it is the central moral tension. A family pushed by loss of stability, a father forced into calculated choices, and a household quietly orbiting around compromise. It’s more focused on the uncomfortable gray area of survival and denial. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Di ko talaga alam kung ano ang gustong maging ng film na ito. Comedy ba siya? Family drama? Erotic? Identity theft? Thriller? Or pure despair lang dahil nawalan siya ng trabaho? Para siyang film na deliberately nagrerefuse mag-stay sa isang genre, which can be intriguing at first, pero eventually nakakagulo rin. Not Son Ye-jin flirting with another man younger than her agai hahaha. Reminds me of something in the rain. What I did like is how methodical the father is in devising his plans. He’s careful not to incriminate himself directly. Instead, he frames his victims in a way that the crimes will never be traced back to him. It’s cold, and calculated. By the end, what I got from the film is a grim question: how far can a father go just to provide for his family and maintain their lifestyle? And on the other side, how much can a wife tolerate and consciously ignore the dark sides of her partner for the sake of family stability? This film leaves you sitting with the messiness of survival, compromise, and the quiet justifications people make when they feel they have no other choice.
