The Devil Wears Prada (2006) review

★★½
Rated 2.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-08
Comedy, Drama · Directed by David Frankel · 109 min
600 reviews · 1,526 films logged · 155,998 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I’m immediately pulled in by the star power, but it’s Meryl Streep who anchors everything. Her quiet authority makes the world of fashion feel both glamorous and suffocating. I read the film less as a fashion story and more as a reflection on ambition, pressure, and how growth reshapes identity and relationships. It doesn’t romanticize success. It shows the emotional cost behind the sparkle. The ending lands softly but meaningfully, leaving me thinking about what kind of success is actually worth chasing. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ What immediately stood out to me is how star studded this movie feels. Ang daming high regarded and attractive women on screen that the star power alone already carries presence. But what really pulls me in is Meryl Streep. I guess I am really drawn to older women in power. Her Miranda is intimidating without raising her voice. Every look feels like a command. I feel her authority in the room even before she speaks. At its core, this is a world built on superficial appearances. Fashion, status, image. Everything is curated. Yet behind that polished exterior is pressure, sacrifice, and constant performance. The film does a great job showing how glamour is not just rainbow and sunshine. It is work, politics, and emotional cost. One of the most impactful moments for me is Stanley Tucci’s dialogue with Andy. When he tells her he does not pity her because she is not trying, she is whining. That hit close to me. I remember being in a similar pressure environment where expectations were brutal. I had a boss who constantly pushed us past what we thought was possible. That experience shaped me. So when Nigel talks about growth through discomfort, I understand exactly where he is coming from. The film is really about growth and how success changes priorities, identity, and relationships. As Andy climbs higher, I see how the people around her react. Some feel left behind. Some feel betrayed. It mirrors real life. Ambition often demands tradeoffs, and sometimes the people closest to us are the ones who feel the shift the most. What I appreciate is that the movie does not romanticize success. Glamorous life is not pure spotlight. It can strain friendships, relationships, and personal values. There are moments where advancement requires stepping on uncomfortable territory or choosing career over loyalty. That tension is where the film feels honest. The ending lands in a way that reminds me of Whiplash. There is no big emotional speech, just a quiet acknowledgment. Miranda recognizing Andy’s capability feels like a mentor silently approving a student who proved herself. Overall, I see The Devil Wears Prada as a film about ambition, identity, and the cost of growth. Beneath the fashion and wit is a very real story about pressure, transformation, and deciding what kind of success I am willing to pursue.
