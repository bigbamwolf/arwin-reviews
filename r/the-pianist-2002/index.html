The Pianist 2002

★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-02
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I felt shaken more by the human suffering than by the violence itself. The film does not rush into chaos but shows how cruelty slowly becomes normal, and that gradual shift disturbed me more than any single shocking image. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I encountered some extreme visuals here that immediately pulled my attention. Seeing a wheelchair bound man thrown off a balcony sets the tone right away. Then the image of a man eating spilled food on the street shows pure desperation without needing explanation. This film helped me understand the genocide more clearly. I watched how it slowly moved from exclusion to execution. Every moment feels like it could be their last, lives are taken casually, and violence becomes normalized. I could feel the denial among the victims, as if they believed this was only extreme discrimination and not the beginning of extermination. There is so much suffering, and the children feel the most vulnerable. Their helplessness hits harder than anything else. The hunger also stood out to me. It reminded me of Grave of the Fireflies in how survival itself becomes the entire story. The sets are very impressive. The ghetto and surrounding locations feel authentic, and even the explosions feel real. A lot happens within the first hour, then the second half becomes mostly quiet and deeply contemplative, focusing more on endurance than on action.
