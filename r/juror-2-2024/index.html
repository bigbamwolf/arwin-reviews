Juror #2 (2024) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-09-11
Crime, Drama, Mystery · Directed by Clint Eastwood · 114 min
602 reviews · 1,528 films logged · 156,200 words
12 Angry Men meets How to Get Away with Murder. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I was hooked immediately because the film takes a simple courtroom setup and turns it into a tense moral puzzle. Rather than relying on twists or flashy filmmaking, it trusts its premise, performances, and difficult questions to keep the story engaging. It's another reminder of how Clint Eastwood can make straightforward storytelling feel incredibly compelling. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Good premise. A man sits on a jury trying to determine whether someone is guilty, while slowly realizing that he himself may actually be responsible for the crime. The fact that one of the jurors is actually the guilty one? What are the chances, right? It is intriguing to see whether he will tell the truth or sacrifice his morals for his family. Is he going to let someone else take the fall, or will he admit what he did? Is protecting your family enough reason to sacrifice someone else? Such an impossible dilemma. I do not think even a morally upright person could give a straight answer when placed in that kind of circumstance. The cast was great. Toni Collette, Chris Messina, Zoey Deutch, and Nicholas Hoult were all good. This might actually be Nicholas Hoult’s best performance for me. Another Clint Eastwood banger. I really admire how egoless his direction feels. Nothing flashy, nothing begging for attention. Just clean, controlled filmmaking that trusts the premise, performances, and moral dilemma to carry the movie.
