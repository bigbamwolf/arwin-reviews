Daniela Forever (2024) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-11-13
602 reviews · 1,528 films logged · 156,200 words
The film opens with a dreamy, neon haze, pink and blue colliding like a fever dream like Vice City. The first shots already feel like memories slipping in and out of focus. Everything looks ethereal, cinematic, and slightly unreal. The aspect ratio feels off center on purpose. It’s uncomfortable, but that’s the point because nothing here is meant to feel stable. The film grain, the anamorphic lenses, the deliberate imperfections all make the fantasy feel tangible. Then comes the crash back to reality. Suddenly, the world looks flat, blurred, and lifeless. Like a downgrade from 4K to 360p. The lines jag, the colors fade, the exposure feels wrong. It’s a deliberate degradation of dynamic range and lens depth to create emotional separation. The result? Reality feels dull, cheap, and distasteful compared to the hyper vivid dream state. i think the idea behind that decision is for us, the audience to not want his reality and prefer the more vibrant dream state. We’re meant to crave the illusion more than the truth. His real life looks like an old Spanish telenovela: desaturated and stiff. It’s a brilliant, risky stylistic choice that makes us complicit in his delusion. The film’s energy blends Black Mirror’s technological anxiety with Eternal Sunshine’s emotional surgery, Vanilla Sky’s layered dreams, and Bruce Almighty’s human as God guilt. At its core, it’s about control, about a man who rebuilds the past until he becomes its prisoner, trapped by his own emotional dependency. That confrontation scene where Daniela (Beatrice Grannò) confesses she’s still in love with Teresa is the heartbreaker. Cold blue light, rain tapping outside, and the line “Do you still love me?” followed by “Just hold on to that and erase everything.” Instantly, nag iba yung lighting sa set piece, the lighting literally transforms from icy blue to warm yellow in that moment. The warmth of denial replacing the chill of truth. It’s a stunning visual metaphor for emotional manipulation through artificial reality. Henry Golding gives one of his most emotionally fragile performances. You can see his character slowly unravel as he begins doing what he swore he wouldn’t: controlling Daniela’s memories, rewriting her past, deciding who deserves to exist. The world bends to his will, and with every edit, he loses another piece of his own soul. The score is great, a fusion of melancholy piano and cold techno pulses. The film often uses partition framing, reminiscent of Parasite’s visual echoes of social imbalance shots, where characters are divided by glass, mirrors, or architecture to show who holds power. It’s subtle but constant: whoever occupies the open frame dominates; whoever’s behind a divider becomes powerless. There’s also a distinct use of vertical power framing. A blocking technique that measures emotional authority through height and positioning. When one character stands while the other sits, it tells you who’s momentarily in control. The film often lets Nicolas (Henry Golding) tower over Daniela, but when he’s back in reality, he’s framed lower or trapped within cramped compositions, stripped of dominance. By the end, the narrative folds into itself like a dream within a dream because he just couldn’t let go of her and losing her forever but in the process he loses himself instead. The dream becomes his new prison. The brighter the illusion, the darker his reality becomes. And when he chooses to stay inside it, you feel the weight of what that means that sometimes, people would rather live in a beautiful lie than face an honest loneliness. So by the ending, I think Nicolas wants Daniela to live again, to believe that everything that happened: Teresa’s breakup, meeting Nicolas, her death, were all just part of a dream. He can rebuild her world, but he’ll never truly belong in it. The more I think about it, the more it feels like a tragic illusion. Maybe the reason I felt before that there was never real chemistry between them is because he was never meant to exist in her story at all. He’s not reviving her; he’s inserting himself into memories that were never his to begin with like a trespasser. So sad, but did not deliver an ultimate gut punch.
