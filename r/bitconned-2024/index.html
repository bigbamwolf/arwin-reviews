Bitconned (2024) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-29
626 reviews · 1,552 films logged · 162,974 words
This is such an entertaining documentary about how easily confidence, marketing, and perceived legitimacy can become more valuable than whatever is actually being sold. Like Fyre (2019), there’s a great vision but poor execution. The difference is that here, the illusion itself becomes part of the product. If enough people believe the company is legitimate, suddenly that belief starts creating its own momentum. I know I am missing a lot of opportunities by not dabbling in cryptocurrency. I know some of it works with proper due diligence. But I just don’t want to participate in an environment with lots of gullible people who don’t even do proper due diligence of some sort. There’s really a pattern to my recent watches. Dumb Money (2023), Fyre (2019), and this. Everything in this world is just marketing. It’s up to the people with clout and influence. Influencer marketing is very important nowadays. And it is a cheap kind of marketing. I find it very useful in my business too because they are low maintenance and spread fast with minimal effort and money spent. Their followers already have trust in them, so whatever comes out of their mouths, even an unknown and less credible company can suddenly become a very legitimate brand they want to put their money into. That is what fascinates me here. They were not only selling cryptocurrency. They were borrowing credibility from people who already had an audience. Once someone influential says something is legitimate, thousands of people can skip the part where they ask why they should believe it. Whether it is for good or bad intentions, it is going to work almost every time. That’s why the number of followers and the gravity of a person’s influence are some sort of currency themselves. Come to think of it, that is one of the oldest tricks in the book that politicians use. Influencer marketing is just the modern version of that. It’s funny that the scammers here got scammed by a kid too when they thought they were hiring a lawyer. Goes to show that desperation can really cloud one’s judgment. Suddenly the people whose entire business depended on other people not doing enough due diligence failed to do enough due diligence themselves. The thing with con artists is that they are high on their own minds. Their hubris is actually their downfall. As said in the documentary, it’s like giving the middle finger to the FBI and saying, “Come and get me.” Often, their vanity is what gets them caught. They want more money, more attention, more credibility, and more clout. But every new photo, interview, flex, and public appearance also creates another trail leading back to them. Well, the sentencing is really unfair for all of them. The people who also got involved and were ratted out, the people who got scammed and invested in them, and the person who made a case against them. The lawyer’s response was actually appropriate. What does that tell the next criminals? Just rat everyone out and you’re free to go even if you are one of the masterminds? The justice system is really a mystery. The ending also made me suspicious about what happened to the cryptocurrency seized by the government and why the victims had not yet received it. That part adds another layer of frustration because even after the scammers are caught, getting the victims their money back becomes another complicated process. The sooner you accept that: 1. they are all in this together, and 2. money of any sort is not real, the better. It’s all just ideas and perceptions. All fugazis. Nice B-roll and reenactments too. I like how the dramatized shots visually recreate what the interviewees are describing instead of just making me stare at talking heads the entire time. It gives the documentary an unexpectedly cinematic texture. Verdict: Absolute Cinema Watched on Netflix. TL;DR: Such an entertaining documentary about how confidence, marketing, and perceived legitimacy can make people believe in something without doing proper due diligence. It reminded me of Fyre (2019) and Dumb Money (2023), where hype and influence can become more valuable than whatever is actually being sold. As someone who uses influencer marketing in my own business, I find it fascinating how easily clout can turn an unknown company into something people trust enough to invest in. The irony of scammers getting scammed themselves was hilarious, but the sentencing and the victims still waiting for their money pissed me off. I also love the cinematic B-roll and reenactments. Everything is marketing, everything is perception, and everything is fugazi!
