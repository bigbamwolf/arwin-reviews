Eden 2024

★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-27
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): A slow burn survival drama that gradually peels away the idea of civilization and replaces it with something far more primal. What starts as an experiment in ideals and escape slowly curdles into paranoia, power imbalance, and moral collapse. Performances carry the weight here, especially in how desperation, hunger, and authority distort people over time. Visually, the film leans into earthy, sun scorched textures that make the setting feel isolated and unforgiving, with the camera becoming more expressive as the story darkens. It’s more about watching belief systems rot under pressure, revealing how thin the line is between principle and savagery. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Unang napansin ko agad yung accent ni Ana de Armas. Medyo weird and distracting sa pandinig. One of the most insane moments in the film is when Sydney is giving birth while warding off wild dogs. Grabe yung tension. Great performance matched with a swelling score. I caught myself holding my breath, just waiting for the baby to cry for the first time. I honestly don’t get the hate towards Sydney. She’s not the best actress out there, but she’s far from bad. She delivers when it matters. Ironic yung character ni Jude Law. He preaches about building a new democracy, rejecting money, hierarchy, and old systems, but he slowly turns into exactly what he claims to hate. Acting like a dictator, treating the island as his own, enforcing isolation. When pressure hits, he can’t stick to his ideals. From vegetarian to carnivore, from visionary to survivalist. He says he wants a democracy without money, yet uses money to secure the Baroness’ possessions. Hypocrite. Unstable. Crazy. It’s regression, not growth. From a brilliant doctor to a savage man. This might be one of Jude Law’s best performances for me. I don’t remember seeing him this raw and unhinged. Ana’s character is the kind of woman who uses sex to gain favors and leverage. Batshit crazy, manipulative, unhinged. Magaling yung acting niya kasi kuha niya talaga yung inis ko. She’s dangerous in a very human way. Vanessa’s character, on the other hand, has firm principles. She sticks to what she believes in, but she’s also very stiff, rigid, and negative. Moral, yes, but emotionally cold. Parang survival by discipline rather than adaptation. Production design and color grading are strong. Mukhang nasa probinsya talaga. The palette reminds me of indie Filipino films set in rural areas. Earthy, muted, sun-baked. The third act is where the film becomes more cinematic, with silhouette shots, and terrain wide frames. The film ultimately shows how men resort to savagery when pushed to the edge. Ideals collapse under hunger, fear, and power struggles. Civilization is thin, and survival strips people down to their ugliest instincts. By the end, the irony completes itself. The tourists became the locals. Their descendants become hotel operators, exactly the kind of transformation Ana de Armas’ character once desired. What began as an escape from society eventually turns into a new version of it.
