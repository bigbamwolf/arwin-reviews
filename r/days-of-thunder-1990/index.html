Days of Thunder (1990) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-23
Action, Drama, Sport · Directed by Tony Scott · 107 min
607 reviews · 1,533 films logged · 157,261 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Feels like Top Gun on asphalt. The film treats racing not just as a sport but as identity. Ego, masculinity, pride, and obsession expressed through engines instead of jet fighters. The relationships matter more than the races, and the drama comes from learning when to compete and when to trust. Beautiful lighting and strong presence from the cast carry it even when the sound mixing struggles. What stood out to me is how the movie frames speed as personality. The hotshot lives to prove himself, the veteran races to survive his past, and the mentor figure tries to keep them from destroying each other. It follows a familiar sports rhythm but it works because the emotions feel grounded in character. Beneath the racing, it becomes a story about maturity, humility, and learning that winning sometimes means letting someone else take the lead. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The opening immediately reminded me of Top Gun because of the way the machines were introduced like living icons, then matched with a loud energizing rock track that makes the scene feel like preparation. The lighting also mirrors Top Gun, heavy backlight from the sun hitting the metal surfaces, and that golden glow wrapping the subjects. Knowing they share the same director makes perfect sense because the visual language treats racing the same way aviation was treated, as masculinity, ego, and adrenaline turned into cinematic poetry. I watched this to commemorate Robert Duvall. He actually carries a huge presence here and is not just a sidekick mentor. And wow, Yondu is here too! I almost did not recognize Nicole Kidman because she looks very young and natural here (before the plastic surgery). I could feel F1 inside the plot structure. There is a trio dynamic and the struggle to function as a team, learning to set aside ego and pride between the hotshot and the veteran while a central figure tries to make them reconcile, then the inevitable accident, then the celebratory release at the end. The sports drama rhythm feels satisfying. Cole thinking the doctor was just role playing was funny because he still saw life as a competition and assumed everything was another game. At the latter part of their interaction, the silence, dim lighting, and close distance of their faces created very strong sexual tension. Watching their chemistry honestly made me want to watch Eyes Wide Shut immediately. This film is beautifully lit. The shapes and colors of light always feel intentional, quietly suggesting emotion beneath the dialogue. My only complaint is the sound mixing. Like many older films, dialogue is extremely hard to hear but the tire screeches and engine revs suddenly explode in volume. The balance is inconsistent so I kept adjusting volume every scene. But at least the story works better for me than Ron Howard’s Rush.
