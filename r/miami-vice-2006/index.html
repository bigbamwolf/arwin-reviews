Miami Vice 2006

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-19
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Miami Vice feels like Michael Mann operating at a much larger scale, with more locations, moving parts, and glossy production value than his earlier work. The film leans heavily into luxury, speed, and scope, which makes it feel more like a modern big budget production than a tightly controlled cinematic experience. While the story explores blurred identities and emotional entanglement within undercover work, the focus often drifts away from procedural tension and into romance. Compared to Mann’s sharper, more restrained films, the usual slow burn dread feels diluted. It looks sleek and expensive, but it lacks the raw, disciplined tension that once defined his strongest work. Spoilers ahead. Read on if you’ve seen the film. From here on, plot details are discussed. This is Michael Mann showing a bigger scale. More cast, more locations, more logistics, luxury cars, jets, speed boats. And I think because of that, this feels like a movie instead of a film. It feels like a modern big budget production rather than a cinematic experience. Naalala ko tuloy kung bakit hindi ko rin masyadong nagustuhan Blackhat. Parang ganito din yung itsura nun. Ito yung klase ng undercover movie na hindi mo na alam kung undercover pa rin ba sila or totoo na yung nararamdaman nila. Everything gets blurred, identities overlap, may double crossing, and the emotional stakes start to overpower the operational ones. Nice to see Elizabeth Rodriguez so confident and fierce here, especially compared to her insecure mother role in Orange Is the New Black. It really shows during the rescue of Trudy. That sequence gave her character presence and strength, even if the film itself felt less focused than classic Mann. The typical Michael Mann tension wasn’t as apparent here, especially in the third act. Normally, Mann uses silence, restraint, and space to build dread. But here, during the shootout, a rock song was playing. Wtf? It killed the tension for me. If I’m being bold, I’d say the film focuses too much on the romantic relationship between the characters instead of fully committing to the procedural intensity. Overall, it looks expensive and sleek, but it lacks the raw, controlled tension that made Michael Mann’s earlier films feel sharper and more immersive.
