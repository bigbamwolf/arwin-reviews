Psycho (1960) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-01
649 reviews · 1,575 films logged · 173,102 words
The shower scene killed more than one thing. It killed my interest too. I love the cinematography and the score. The black and white photography, framing, camera movement, shadows, the Bates house, the motel compositions, and especially the shower sequence all work for me. There is something about the way Hitchcock frames the house above the motel that already makes the whole place feel sinister before anything even happens. I also love how aggressive the score feels in the first half, especially around the murder. The second half feels much quieter to me, and once the film starts leaning more into dialogue and investigation, I could feel some of that intensity disappear. The pacing slows down, the tension drops for me, and I simply found Marion's story more interesting than the investigation that follows. Once she is gone, the film asks me to become more invested in Norman and in solving what happened. But I was never particularly interested in Norman, and Anthony Perkins' performance was nothing special for me. That is probably why this and American Psycho (2000) both have a weird disconnect for me, but in opposite ways. American Psycho took a while before anything really grabbed me, while Psycho grabbed me immediately and then lost me after its biggest moment. In both cases, I can appreciate individual things like the cinematography, atmosphere, performances, or certain scenes without being fully engaged by the whole movie. I think that is also why an investigative film like The Little Things (2021) works better for me. There, the investigation itself becomes the psychological hook. Here, the investigation feels like a step down from the tension and atmosphere of the first half. I think the first half is exceptional, and I would gladly revisit some of those sequences just for the cinematography and score. But the second half loses so much of what made the film interesting to me that I can't say I enjoyed it as a whole. I appreciate Hitchcock's filmmaking, but I wish the story had maintained the same intensity after Marion's murder. Verdict: Watchable
