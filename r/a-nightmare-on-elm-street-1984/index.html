A Nightmare on Elm Street (1984) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-02
Horror · Directed by Wes Craven · 91 min
648 reviews · 1,574 films logged · 172,352 words
Nancy said enough dreaming. Time to set traps. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Tina’s death is what really hooked me. That was the moment I realized normal physical rules do not apply in Freddy’s world. Seeing her body dragged across the walls and ceiling immediately establishes that once you enter the dream, gravity and reality can basically stop mattering. The score is good too. It does not have the typical horror vibe. It feels more mysterious and uncanny, which fits the dream sequences really well. Instead of constantly telling me to be scared, it gives me that feeling that something is just slightly wrong. The practical effects are also awesome, especially Glen’s bed. The moment he gets pulled down into the bed and all that blood erupts is amazing. Even when what is happening is impossible, it still feels tangible because something real is moving, falling, or exploding inside the frame. And knowing they used a rotating room for both Tina’s death and Glen’s blood sequence makes those scenes even more impressive. The whole set could rotate around the actors and effects, allowing gravity itself to create that dream logic instead of relying on CGI. The sets and lighting are also cool. They give the film this dream vibe with dread without making every location feel completely distorted. It is more about familiar places suddenly feeling strange because of the lighting, atmosphere, and the way Freddy enters them. Nancy was so brave and clever! I love that she does not just keep running from Freddy. She studies him, figures out the rules, and prepares to fight back. That alone made me more invested in her because she actually adapts to the danger. The third act is some Home Alone (1990) shit. Traps everywhere. Once Nancy starts preparing the house, the whole movie suddenly turns into a survival battle where she is actively controlling the environment instead of just reacting to Freddy. Really a strong female character like Ripley in Alien (1979). Both are brave, but what I like more is that they are smart under pressure. They survive because they stay composed enough to understand the threat and act on it. Verdict: Near Absolute
