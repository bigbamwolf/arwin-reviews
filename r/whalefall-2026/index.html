Whalefall (2026) review

★★½
Rated 2.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-30
629 reviews · 1,555 films logged · 164,190 words
127 Hours inside a whale, but with daddy issues. The sound design was so immersive in Dolby Atmos, like the wave hits ,the deep underwater sounds, whale calls, and muffled noises. I appreciate the cramped sets and the use of lenses to create claustrophobic, tight framing. There is barely any breathing room around Austin Abrams once he is inside, which helps sell how physically trapped he is. Austin Abrams has the same panicky, scared whimper as in Resident Evil (2026). His acting is great too. That nervous breathing and almost helpless panic work here because so much of the survival portion depends on him carrying the screen alone. It reminded me of 127 Hours (2010) and The Shallows (2016), but those films worked better for me as straight up survival experiences. Whalefall keeps breaking the physical survival with the father and son story, which is more ambitious emotionally, but I think that is also where the pacing started disconnecting from me. But sadly, I dozed off far too many times. Very rare for me to fall asleep during a movie. The last time was Supergirl (2026). Maybe it is a mix of fatigue and, I think, just a mismatch of expectations. I’d like a straight up survival movie, but this movie isn’t simply about a man trying to escape a whale. It’s about a son who spent years trying to escape his father, only to discover that the lessons his father taught him are what help him survive. I applaud them for an ambitious drama about grief and reconnection. The technical presentation is great, and the underlying emotion is buried into it, but I think there’s just a disconnect with the pacing and the stitching of the story. The constant movement between survival and memories did not always flow smoothly enough for me, even if I understand what the film was trying to accomplish. I appreciate the thought that when one life ends, one begins. And the toughness of a father trying to toughen up a child can become too extreme. Glad I really don’t have a son! Verdict: Barely Watchable Watched at Power Plant Cinema Dolby Atmos
