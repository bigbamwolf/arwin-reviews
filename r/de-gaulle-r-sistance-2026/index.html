De Gaulle: Résistance (2026) review

★★½
Rated 2.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-11
654 reviews · 1,580 films logged · 175,559 words
Politics first. War later. Thankfully, the tanks eventually arrive. The sound design is actually good. The gunfire, explosions, tanks, and aircraft effects are immersive. Once the war starts creeping into the film, the audio does a lot in making everything feel much bigger. The cinematography is good at times, especially during the war scenes. I love the smoke and fire. There is something about silhouettes moving through smoke with explosions burning in the background that immediately makes the film feel more cinematic. The score is actually good too. It works during the political tension, military buildup, and actual combat. Even when there is not much action happening yet, the music helps create the feeling that something much bigger is coming. For a huge chunk of the film, I was just waiting for the war to finally explode. It feels like one long history lesson and is very heavy on politics. But I think that is really the purpose of the film. This is less interested in immediately throwing me into WW2 action and more interested in showing the political collapse and decisions surrounding De Gaulle before everything fully erupts. The film itself is a historical drama centered on France’s collapse in 1940 and De Gaulle refusing to accept defeat. But when the tank sequence finally comes, it hits the right spot. The physicality of the tanks, the explosions, the sound, and the smoke finally gave me the war film energy I had been waiting for. After so much political discussion, that sequence felt like the film finally releasing some of the pressure it had been building. I admire De Gaulle because even when the odds are against him, he refuses to bow down. Even when the military and political forces around him think his position is hopeless, he keeps pushing forward. I respect that kind of conviction. At the same time, I respect him more than I am emotionally connected to him as a character. Right now, I am more interested in what he represents and what he is fighting against than personally attached to him. Let’s see what Part 2 offers. I’m hoping it becomes more war driven and less political. Watched at SM Manila during 29th French Film Festival.
