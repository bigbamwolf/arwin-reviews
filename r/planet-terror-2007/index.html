Planet Terror (2007) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-26
Action, Comedy, Horror · Directed by Robert Rodriguez · 105 min
600 reviews · 1,526 films logged · 155,998 words
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): This is pure grindhouse energy turned all the way up. The look alone is doing a lot of work, dirty textures, aggressive colors, exaggerated violence, and a deliberately battered visual style that feels more like a sensation than a narrative. It leans hard into practical effects and physicality, committing to a tactile, flesh forward aesthetic that makes the world feel sticky and dangerous. The tone never takes itself too seriously, mixing splatter horror with parody and deliberately dumb humor, which oddly becomes part of its charm. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I think this is my third or fourth time watching this. I remember seeing it as a teenager and, honestly, it got me really horny alongside Zack Snyder’s Dawn of the Dead. I don’t even remember how I got a copy. Maybe LimeWire? Aside from the sexy nuances, what really made this film stick with me was its unique retro style. The gritty cinematography, thick atmosphere, heavy prosthetics, aggressive color grading, over the top gore, and a strong woman main character. I didn’t really see that combination much back then. Watching it now, I realize that without fully understanding cinematic language at the time, this must have been pure sensory overload for my teenage brain. Everything is cranked up. The textures, the blood, the smoke, the scratches, the intentional “damaged film” look. Prosthetics play a huge role here, turning it into a body horror. The special effects department did a lot of heavy lifting too, from the explosions to the production design that fully commits to building this infected world. The film feels like Kill Bill: Zombie Edition, but filtered through parody and grindhouse aesthetics. May mga stupid jokes din na very 2000s comedy, parang Scary Movie levels. The stupidity is part of the texture. Rewatching it now, I still get why it left such an imprint on me. It’s messy, loud, and shameless, but that’s exactly the point. And even years later, it still holds up.
