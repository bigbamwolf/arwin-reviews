Together (2025) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-11-02
618 reviews · 1,544 films logged · 159,192 words
The dogs fusing together and the nightmare of Millie’s parents set the tone perfectly. It tells you exactly what this film is: a quiet horror about connection. It’s a tender apocalypse shared by two broken people. From there, the film begins to visually reinforce this unraveling. The upside down shot at the well flips the world. A visual metaphor that their lives are now inverted. I think this has been a common shot for horror films signaling a moment when everything changes. The imagery evolves into playful but calculated, especially in the scene where Tim (Dave Franco) showering mirrors the movements of Millie (Alison Brie) while driving. Ang ganda ng pagkakaexecute ng camera shots making it a seamless almost choreographed movements parang may invisible hand puppeteering them. Another detail worth noting is the pull away shot after Dave whispers “Keep going” is haunting before he disappears into darkness. Parang scene sa Get Out. It’s this same discomfort that also appears in how the film treats intimacy. There’s a brilliant use of intimate close ups that linger on their body parts. And when that intimacy crosses the line from emotional to physical horror, the film doesn’t hold back. Prosthetics are insane! The fused couple in the cave is a nightmare worthy creature. What makes the scene even more effective is how the film uses sound, not just visuals, to communicate connection and transformation. The sound design is unsettling, whenever their body contours, I can hear every crack of bone, every scrape of skin sliding against skin, every breath feels amplified. Yet what stays with me more than the technique is the ache behind it. Millie’s desire for physical touch is painfully human. I get it. Tim also wants connection, but he’s terrified of vulnerability. This emotional weight is mirrored in the performances, especially Dave’s. Dave captures that exhaustion perfectly. His eyes look like they haven't slept in months. He’s physically present but emotionally fading. And that’s why their intimacy hits differently. The sex scene was raw, passionate, but it turns into horror. Parang naalala ko si Shaina at John Lloyd! Ahaha. That intimacy foreshadows the ending. Connection not as freedom, but as entrapment. By the end, I thought it would end in tragedy but they survive. Together gives acceptance since they don’t have much of a choice. They accept that they will eventually fuse literally, physically. Skin to skin, bone to bone, identity as one. No screaming, no escape. Just quiet surrender. Because being stuck together feels better than loneliness and the fear of being vulnerable. Even if it means that being bound to one another means losing your name, your shape, your self. Creepy yet beautiful. Terrifying yet intimate. And that final image: two faces merging, reminded me of Jojo Alejar’s comedic face morphs on late night TV, except this time it’s not funny. It’s love beyond logic. They’re part of the cult now. There’s peace in their eyes, like enlightenment. But in our modern world, it still feels sick. Because this film was never really about monsters but rather it’s about human need. It’s about connection. Wanting to be held. And the film asks: if someone loved you so much they’d fuse with you forever, is that still love or is it possession?
