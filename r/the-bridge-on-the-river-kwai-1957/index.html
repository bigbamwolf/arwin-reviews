The Bridge on the River Kwai 1957

★★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-09-24
The scale and number of extras are impressive. I love the visual presentation. Old but gold. Super admirable. I also admire Colonel Nicholson’s determination and principles, especially how he refuses to back down from the Japanese even when it would be easier for him to just give in. I love that they actually built a real bridge for the film. Wow. Old school. No shortcuts. I also like how the bridge itself almost feels like a character. It has so much presence on screen. There is pride, obsession, conflict, and meaning attached to it, so by the time the final act arrives, I already feel like I know this structure. The last act is quiet but suspenseful. The train chugging and whistling almost works like a countdown timer. I know something is about to happen, and the closer that train gets, the tighter everything feels. And when the climax finally happens, it feels incredibly real because everything we are looking at was physically built. It also made me appreciate Christopher Nolan’s plane crash sequence in Tenet (2020) even more because that is basically the same old school filmmaking mentality. Build something real, put the camera there, and destroy it for real. For me, that kind of craftsmanship ages beautifully. Definitely worth a rewatch.
