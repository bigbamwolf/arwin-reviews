Pearl Harbor 2001

★★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-03
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Big, emotional, and unapologetically cinematic. The film opens with striking imagery and a warm, nostalgic color palette that immediately sets it apart from Michael Bay’s later, more digital look. When it leans into aerial photography and large scale action, it’s genuinely impressive. The flight choreography is elegant, and the romance driven motivation gives the spectacle an emotional spine. This is Bay at his most controlled, using scale, music, and physical effects to create moments that feel overwhelming rather than hollow. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ It starts off with picturesque shots of planes against an orange skyline, immediately reminding me of Apocalypse Now. I actually like the colors here more than Michael Bay’s later obsession with teal and orange. The palette feels warmer, more nostalgic, and less digitally aggressive. This film leans heavily on romance as motivation for the characters. When it wants to be artistic, especially with the aerial shots, it really delivers. The dogfights and flight choreography are elegant and cinematic. Of course, nothing really outmatches Top Gun: Maverick, but that’s an unfair comparison since they’re two decades apart. Tama yung hinala ko. Hindi basta-basta mamamatay si Ben Affleck. He’s too big of a star to just die in the first half. I knew it. I knew this would turn into a love triangle, but not the typical one. It’s framed more around duty, guilt, and loyalty rather than pure romance. Michael Bay did a great job making the Japanese forces feel genuinely terrifying. When the attack on Pearl Harbor begins, it’s pure confusion and chaos. This is where Bay truly shines. His expert use of real explosives gives the sequence an insane level of believability and physicality. I dare say mas ramdam ko ang stakes at scale dito kaysa sa Dunkirk. For me, this might be the first time Michael Bay actually defeated Nolan in a film. The most terrifying shots are the nurses running toward the hospital and the pilots sprinting toward their airplanes while Japanese planes rain bullets from above. It feels like an inescapable death trap. The size and impact of the bullets hitting the pavement are exceptionally shown, and it’s genuinely horrifying. The hospital scene got me really angry and shocked. Very effective storytelling. Napahawak ako sa bibig ko. Superb acting and emotional impact. I also specifically loved the musical score during the takeoff of the planes from the carrier heading to Tokyo. It feels heroic, solemn, and determined all at once, perfectly capturing the film’s emotional peak. Overall, Pearl Harbor commits to spectacle and emotion, it delivers something powerful and unforgettable.
