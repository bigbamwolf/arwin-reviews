Kubo and the Two Strings (2016) review

★★
Rated 2/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-10
655 reviews · 1,581 films logged · 175,968 words
Ne Zha energy, but handcrafted one frame at a time. Kubo and the Two Strings feels quieter. Because of its Asian aesthetic and heroic fantasy structure, it kind of reminds me of Ne Zha (2019). But this one has a calmer and more reflective energy, while Ne Zha is much louder and more explosive. The stop motion is much smoother than Coraline (2009). Some movements are so fluid that I almost forget I am watching stop motion. Laika was already building on techniques it developed from its earlier films, and director Travis Knight specifically talked about pushing the animation, camera movement, and martial arts action much further here. And yes, a lot of the effects I noticed are not purely stop motion. The film combines the physical puppets and sets with digital set extensions, volumetric clouds, and large CGI water simulations for oceans, waves, and other environmental effects. What I like is that the digital work is designed to blend with the handcrafted look instead of replacing it. I like the giant skeleton sequence. The scale, choreography, camera movement, creature design, and physicality are all impressive. And apparently, they actually built a fully movable skeleton puppet around 16 feet tall and roughly 400 pounds. That makes the sequence even cooler knowing there was something physically enormous in front of the camera. I think the highlight of this film is the action sequences. They are so cool and well choreographed. The boat fight especially has much more electric energy because of how big the scale and movement feel. The action is where stop motion stops feeling limited and starts feeling almost like live action choreography. But alas, another miss in emotion for me. I did not connect strongly enough with Kubo or his family, and some of the emotional beats also felt predictable. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I already predicted that Monkey was his mother and Beetle was his father. So when those reveals came, they did not give me the emotional payoff I think the film wanted. I genuinely admire the craftsmanship and action choreography, but I just didn't enjoy most of the film. I was frequently bored, never really connected with Kubo or his family, and the predictable revelations made the emotional payoff even weaker. The animation is excellent, but the storytelling didn't work for me. Verdict: Weak
