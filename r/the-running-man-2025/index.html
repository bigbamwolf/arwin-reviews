The Running Man 2025

★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-12-25
Right from the first few minutes, the problem of poverty is already evident. The couple’s acting feels believable, grounded, and real. I’m usually not a fan of Glen Powell since most of his roles come off as kupal, but I actually liked this side of him. It shows range. Mas okay siya dito. The premise feels like a mix of The Hunger Games and John Wick. You have the survival spectacle paired with a bounty style contract system where entertainment feeds on violence. World building is handled well through nice sets and background extensions. It really feels like a nearby dystopian future. Hindi siya sobrang sci fi, just close enough to reality to be uncomfortable. It’s a fun popcorn movie, especially when they reach Michael Cera’s house filled with booby traps. That whole sequence leans into chaos and absurdity in a good way. Nice to see Emilia Jones again after CODA. Even though she doesn’t have much screen time, she’s still a great actress. She also looks more grown up here, and her presence adds warmth to the film. The payoff at the end works. It delivers what it promises without overstaying its welcome. Underneath the spectacle, the film touches on deeper ideas like deep fakes, fake news, propaganda, network greed, conspiracies, and anarchy. It’s not subtle, but it fits the tone of the story. Overall, it’s entertaining, timely, and easy to watch. Not trying to be profound, but it knows exactly what kind of movie it wants to be.
