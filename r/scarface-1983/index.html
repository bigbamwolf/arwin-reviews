Scarface 1983

★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-09
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): This feels like a raw, flashy rise and fall crime story carried by Al Pacino’s intense presence and a neon soaked Miami atmosphere. I’m pulled in by the unapologetic violence, stylish lighting, and seductive criminal energy that makes the world feel glamorous and dangerous at the same time. Beneath the spectacle, I see a character driven by ambition and ego, chasing power at any cost. The film works for me as both a thrilling crime fantasy and a cautionary tale about how success without discipline slowly isolates and destroys. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Ibang Al Pacino ang nakikita ko dito. He looks rugged, poor, and middle aged. Mas raw at unrefined yung angst niya. Hindi na siya masyadong bata, hindi rin matanda. I also notice iba yung accent niya dito, more street swagger kesa slick and classy. This film is not shy about violence at all. Talagang in your face. The colors scream Miami Vice City energy because of the Miami setting. Yung neon pink and electric blue lighting give that flashy, seductive criminal underworld vibe. It feels glamorous but dangerous, like a city that never sleeps and is always one bad decision away from chaos. The sunset skyline shots also add this dreamy contrast, with that pink and orange horizon. If I want a crime thriller set in Miami, I would go with Scarface and not Miami Vice. Natawa ako sa paraan kung paano siya nireject ni Elvira. Michelle Pfeiffer looks so young and gorgeous here. The last time I saw her was as Janet in Ant Man, so seeing her here feels like time travel. I actually like Tony’s twisted mindset. Get the money, then get the power, then get the woman. Huge ambition. Grabe yung execution kay Omar. Parang ngayon lang ako nakakita ng ganun sa film. Savage talaga! Ang galing ni Tony Montana makipag negotiate. Wala siyang takot kahit harap harapan na yung threat. Pure intimidation and confidence. The atmospheric score adds a lot to the thrill and action. It feels tense, stylish, and dangerous. That comeback with Mel? First class ticket to resurrection. Nicely done. Being on top is brutal. Tony becomes paranoid and lonely. He gets everything he ever wanted, but he is lonelier than ever. That’s what happens when he ignore the lessons from his precedessor. Lesson number one, don’t underestimate the other guy’s greed. Lesson number two, don’t get high on your own supply. Despite everything, he still has a moral line. He refuses to kill a woman and children. I like that conclusion when the film explodes into chaos with gunfights and destruction. A loud, violent fall from the top. Literally!
