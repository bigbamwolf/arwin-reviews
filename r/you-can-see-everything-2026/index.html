You Can See Everything (2026) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-18
670 reviews · 1,595 films logged · 182,983 words
This docufilm is the epitome of INTER-VIEW. It really dives deep inside the views of its subjects, but the deeper Nathan goes, the less certain I become about who is actually controlling the interview. I love how Nathan’s questions feel like an interrogation. He does not care if they are invasive. The effort to uncover the truth is clearly there, but Liz and Billy are also extremely guarded with whatever strategy or secret they may have. Nathan keeps poking for cracks, but they rarely give him an obvious one. I also love the suffocating close-ups. It feels like the audience is scrutinizing Billy and Liz just as deeply as Nathan is. Their faces practically fill the frame, so every eye movement, pause, smile, bead of sweat, and reaction starts feeling like evidence. And that is where the film plays with me. I found myself looking for the usual things people associate with lying. Looking away. Stuttering. Swallowing. Nervous movements. But they rarely give me those obvious signals. Funny enough, after reading what deception researchers said about the film, those things are not reliable lie detectors anyway. Eye contact and facial behavior alone cannot tell me whether someone is being truthful. The score is great too. It adds to the mystery, but it is also enchanting. It feels like I am under a spell, which perfectly matches how uncertain the entire experience becomes. While watching, I can feel this constant tug of war over who controls the situation and the narrative. There is never a clear winner. Sometimes Nathan seems completely in control because he has the questions and the camera. Then Liz redirects the conversation. Then Billy enters with this overwhelming intensity. Then suddenly I am wondering whether Nathan himself is becoming emotionally compromised. Nathan feels like he is using a divide and conquer strategy. Separate Liz and Billy, compare their answers, find where their stories line up, find where they contradict each other, and figure out if one of them is somehow puppeteering the other. I also love Nathan’s funny way of saying certain words. Sometimes it sounds sarcastic. Sometimes it is just plain truth delivered in the most uncomfortable way possible. Either way, he knows how to phrase invasive questions in a way that unsettles them and potentially reveals key details. I love how Liz and Billy have such solid conviction. They speak with this absolute certainty that what they believe is true and nothing else matters. And that is probably what confused me the most. I do not know if they are consciously lying, genuinely believe their own version of reality, or are simply highly functional entrepreneurs who have constructed an entire narrative that makes perfect sense inside their own heads. Psychologists discussing the film also brought up something more interesting than simple body language: reflective functioning. Basically, how well someone can understand their own thoughts, motivations, and emotions, and also understand how those things affect other people. That feels much more useful here than trying to catch somebody looking away from the camera. Billy is on another level. The intensity, eye contact, speech pattern, confidence, emotional volatility, grandiosity, and unpredictability make him very difficult for me to grasp. Sometimes he seems protective. Sometimes controlling. Sometimes completely convinced that everyone else simply does not understand what he and Liz understand. The film slowly peels the story back layer by layer, and I love that. Every new conversation gives me more information but somehow creates another question. I was excited, confused, and constantly changing my mind about everyone. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I can also feel sexual tension between Nathan and Liz, mostly through their body language and eye contact. And apparently I am not alone in noticing that shift. Even Billy has acknowledged that some viewers leave the film thinking Nathan developed feelings for Liz, although he believes parts of that dynamic were deliberately played up to examine Nathan’s own bias. That makes me even more curious about what happened when the cameras were not rolling. Not because I think something definitely happened between them, but because their connection starts feeling intimate beyond just physical proximity. Emotional. Mental. There is a familiarity growing there, and I feel like there are parts of that relationship I was never allowed to fully see. Then the reenactment broke the spell for me. The documentary had already trained me to study real faces, real pauses, real reactions, and real uncomfortable conversations. Then suddenly the visual language changes and I am watching events being recreated. It felt like losing your SD card or forgetting to hit record and then having to recreate everything just to save the film. I understand why they did it. Nathan and Lance Oppenheim have explained that those early conversations happened before cameras were rolling, so they used notes and reenactments to bring that material into the documentary. But emotionally, it still felt like two separate films to me. And honestly, I just wanted more Liz. The ending also felt abrupt. I like that the film forces me to make my own judgment, but at the same time, I feel like it stopped before giving me enough evidence to feel confident in any conclusion. Is Elizabeth sincere? How much does Nathan’s growing sympathy influence his judgment? Is Billy protecting Elizabeth, controlling her, being controlled by her, or are they both participating in the same constructed reality? And maybe the biggest question of all: when people know they are being filmed, am I actually seeing them, or just the version of themselves they have decided to perform? For a film called You Can See Everything, I walked out feeling like I did not see everything at all. Watched at Ayala Manila Bay Cinema 4 Dolby Atmos
