De Gaulle: Liberté (2026) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-11
654 reviews · 1,580 films logged · 175,559 words
France may have fallen. De Gaulle refused to believe it ceased to exist. Right off the bat, it explodes with war. This is the war film energy I was waiting for in Part 1. I love the desert location. Desert power! Love the explosions, fire, and smoke! The color grading in the desert setting reminded me of Dune: Part Two (2024), with the warm sandy tones, harsh sunlight, silhouettes, and deep contrast against the sky. But this one grounds that visual language in an actual battlefield instead of science fiction. I was amazed by how immersive the sound was even in a regular cinema. The explosions, gunfire, tanks, and aircraft have enough impact that I could feel the scale without needing IMAX or a premium theater. The score once again gives the film texture. It works during the battles, political tension, and reflective moments. Sometimes it adds urgency, then elsewhere it makes the whole struggle feel almost mournful. But it also has reflective parts. While soldiers are physically fighting what feels like an unwinnable war, politicians are sitting comfortably debating whether to support them or not. I understand why those debates matter, but that contrast really stood out to me. People on the battlefield are dying while people far away are deciding whether their fight is politically convenient. Compared with Part 1, I was hoping this would become more war driven. It definitely gives me much more action, but the political sections still slow the momentum for me. I understand that this is ultimately a historical and political drama, though, so I think that friction is partly just what I personally want from a war film. I love Leclerc. His courage, loyalty, leadership, and willingness to keep fighting despite terrible odds made him one of my favorite characters here. There is something satisfying about watching someone who does not just talk about resistance but actually carries it onto the battlefield. I also love that the film adds spy elements that were not as prominent in Part 1. Espionage, resistance networks, soldiers, and politicians are now all fighting the same war in completely different ways. It makes Part 2 feel broader without relying entirely on bigger battles. I admire de Gaulle’s extreme patriotism even more here. In Part 1, I respected his conviction. Here, after seeing how hopeless France’s position becomes, I understand it more. His belief that France never ceased to exist says everything about him. The government can collapse. The army can lose. Territory can be occupied. But in his mind, France itself is still alive as long as somebody refuses to surrender it. I honestly was not aware that France reached rock bottom like this. Seeing one of Europe’s major powers reduced to occupation, political division, exile, and resistance made de Gaulle’s refusal to accept defeat feel even more extreme. The American side also made me think. At first I wondered if it was showmanship, arriving late and presenting themselves as the savior. So what I am seeing feels less like simple narcissism and more like power leveraging between Allies. They may be fighting the same enemy, but everyone is still protecting their own political influence. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I love the Resistance sequence in Paris. Seeing civilians fight back and begin reclaiming their own city gave the war a different kind of payoff. It is no longer only armies fighting over France. France itself is fighting back from within. But my highlight is the thunder and lightning moment with the ships at sea. Almost everything disappears into darkness, then every lightning strike briefly reveals rows of ships waiting offshore to be deployed. That image is fucking powerful! The darkness hides the scale, then nature itself flashes a light on this enormous force waiting in silence. One of those shots where I immediately thought: this is cinema. Verdict: Watchable Watched at SM Manila during the 29th French Film Festival TLDR: I love the cinematography, immersive sound design, score, and war sequences. That thunder and lightning shot revealing the ships at sea is fucking cinema! But the political sections still slow the momentum for me. I appreciate the historical storytelling, but I was only occasionally engaged throughout the film. Still, this is a definite improvement over Part 1, with a much more satisfying payoff.
