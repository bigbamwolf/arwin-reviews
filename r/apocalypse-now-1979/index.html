Apocalypse Now (1979) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-11-14
620 reviews · 1,546 films logged · 160,269 words
The cinematography and color grading shocked me. For a 1979 film, its visuals feel strangely modern. Sharp silhouettes, blazing fire in the distance, saturated skies. Same vibe as The Impossible. Coppola uses anamorphic lenses with circular flares, creating this dreamy distortion where war looks almost beautiful. By the 30 minute mark, I finally understood why people call this a visual masterpiece. Yung mga helicopter shots sa horizon? God tier filmmaking. Ito na ang pinaka visually appealing na war film for me. My only complain is the overuse of lens flares. May pagka JJ Abrams moment. The assault on the village is jaw dropping. Precise, choreographed chaos. Gareth Edwards clearly took inspirations from this film. I can see Rogue One all over this sequence. And that jet bombing scene? Pure spectacle. Practical explosions everywhere. No CGI, just raw violence captured on camera. Then there’s Kilgore: calm, arrogant, crazy cool. Surfing while bombs fall. It’s funny but disturbing. The film isn’t subtle: war turns men into creatures who think they’re invincible gods. When the film reaches the Do Lung Bridge, it becomes something else entirely. The nighttime cinematography is shot brilliantly. It feels like Left 4 Dead, there's no turning back! But everything changes the moment Willard meets the indigenous tribe and the photojournalist. The visuals shift again: stronger contrast, deeper greens and golds, parang Kong: Skull Island in mood and palette. Then Kurtz emerges. Shadows hide half his face, wiping water from his bald head like Baron Harkonnen. The story feels not like you’re journeying toward Kurtz. You’re descending into him. Willard sees his future in Kurtz, brilliance twisted by the weight of truth. “The horror… the horror” isn’t a villain’s final line. It’s the realization that in war, morality collapses, sanity dissolves, and survival requires becoming the monster you’re hunting When he steps outside and the tribe bows to him, he walks away instead. He doesn’t order the airstrike. He proved the woman wrong: Willard is not an animal nor a god. I came in expecting a war epic, but slowly it became psychological, as if peering through their minds. This film shows how crazy the soldiers become inside a war zone. Senseless killing and happy triggers. I left with something else entirely: a reflection on men at war, on the impossible idea that after seeing hell, the system expects you to return home normal. Could have been an easy 5 star film, but there are beats na mabagal. Maraming shots na pinakitang just for the sake of a cool shot but don’t necessarily move the story forward.
