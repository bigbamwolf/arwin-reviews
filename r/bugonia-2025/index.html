Bugonia 2025

★★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-21
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Much stronger on a second viewing because the film shifts from feeling like a mystery to feeling like an interpretation exercise. Once the ending is known, the tension changes into curiosity about motives and perspective. Instead of asking what is happening, the film becomes about who is right. It plays like a psychological character study disguised as a thriller, with a strangely humorous tone underneath the bleakness. A confined confrontation between two opposing worldviews where truth is uncertain. As paranoia, persuasion, and survival instincts collide, both characters believe they are protecting humanity in completely different ways. The film gradually reveals that the real conflict is not just physical escape, but deciding which reality to trust. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ This film is much better on my second viewing. Now that I already know how the film ends from my first watch, Teddy feels more heroic in trying to save us from the aliens, and the kidnapping and torture suddenly become justifiable. And Teddy makes 100% sense that aliens are very much masters of deception. But at the same time, Michelle is also heroic in her own way because she gives humanity the cure that could save us from destroying ourselves. During my first watch it felt like a crime thriller. Now that the noise, and doubts are out of the way, the motivations are clearer, it feels more like a character study for each of them. And if Yorgos intended this as a comedy, it still works as one. It becomes a dark comedy about a bleak commentary that people on Earth are doomed, no chance, no hope to save themselves from the harm they brought upon humankind.
