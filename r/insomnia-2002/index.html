Insomnia 2002

★★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-11
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I experience this as a tense psychological detective film where Nolan uses pacing, color, and performance to make me physically feel the character’s unraveling state. The atmosphere, fatigue, and creeping anxiety become the real engine of the story, pulling me into a sleepless spiral that feels immersive beyond just plot. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I love how Nolan directs detective style films. This feels thrilling and atmospheric right from the start. I get the same energy I felt in Batman Begins, especially in the pacing and color palette, which remind me a lot of that film’s first act. Al Pacino really looks sleep deprived here. Even I can feel that heaviness in the eyes while watching. That level of craftsmanship, where Nolan transfers the character’s physical and mental exhaustion to the audience, is top notch filmmaking. All the elements of cinema language feel carefully aligned to support that sensation of fatigue and tension. I also get a Following vibe when he goes into Brody’s apartment and leaves fingerprints behind. That moment adds another layer of anxiety because he could easily be framed. His judgment starts slipping as the sleep deprivation worsens, and I can feel how sloppy he becomes. It is a smart way to externalize his mental decline through his actions. I keep thinking about why that freaking lodge doesn’t have blackout curtains if the management knows the sun never goes down in that town. It feels wildly inconsiderate, and it adds to my frustration in a way that mirrors what the character is going through. That constant daylight becomes another antagonist, pushing him further into exhaustion. Overall, I experience Insomnia as a tense psychological detective film where Nolan uses pacing, color, and performance to make me physically feel the character’s unraveling state. It is immersive in a way that goes beyond plot and pulls me directly into his sleepless spiral.
