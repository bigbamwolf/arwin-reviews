The Lost Bus (2025) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-10-11
613 reviews · 1,539 films logged · 158,229 words
Sa unang tingin, akala ko isa lang itong typical disaster survival film: sunog, takbuhan, sigawan, tapos iyakan sa dulo. Pero iba pala. The Lost Bus doesn’t just show people trapped in a fire, it traps you with them. Isa ito sa mga bihirang pelikula na mararamdaman mo sa dibdib yung init, at yung takot. Pagdating ng 15 minute mark, parang nag multiply by ten ang stress level. Walang pahinga. From the crackling fire to the handheld shaky shots, lahat masterfully done para maramdaman mo yung panic nila. Super intense. Zero visibility, di mo alam kung what lies ahead. The direction and vision are clear. The director used the camera to communicate helplessness, and now that I realized he also directed Captain Phillips, the connection makes sense. The same kind of shaky realism and panic driven camera language is present here. Ang ganda ng sound design. Rinig mo bawat crackle at bawat ember. Kahit sa laptop ko lang pinanood, sobrang immersive. Pakiramdam ko nandun ako sa loob ng chaos. This deserves to be seen on a big screen. The fire simulations are outstanding and the lighting fits perfectly with every scene. The CG aerial shots showing how the fire spreads are haunting pero convincing. Yung last part lang with the bus, medyo halata na CGI model dahil sobrang bilis ng camera motion, pero understandable na given the scale of the sequence. The lighting and color psychology evolve naturally. As the tension and risk rise, the more the red hues dominate the frame. You can almost feel the temperature increase with every shift of color. It is both visual and emotional heat. The production design is on point. They did a really good job selling the visual that there’s fire everywhere. You wouldn’t feel that this was shot only in a studio or sound stage. You’ll feel like you’re out there with them, suffering, suffocating, and thirsty. Ito yung mga film na gusto mo na lang matapos kasi di mo na kaya yung tension. Parang torture in a good cinematic way. Lalo na kasi mga bata ang at risk. Kapag kids ang involved, iba ang bigat. Kapag adult lang siguro, di ganito katindi ang stress. The editing rhythm maintains high energy but still gives enough room for emotional beats. Every cut mirrors the characters’ heartbeat, frantic when they are in chaos, and lingering just long enough to let us breathe when they are safe. The film knows when to pause and let you breathe before throwing you back again into the fire. Si Matthew McConaughey is in top form here, best since Interstellar. Halata na he drew from his real life dad instincts. The desperation, the guilt, the love, lahat ramdam. At the end, napakapit talaga ako sa upuan. I found myself pausing, catching my breath. Tapos nung reunion scene ng mga kids at parents, naluha talaga ako. Parang yung moment na nakita na ni Tom Holland yung mga kapatid nya sa The Impossible(2012). A survival story that burns both the screen and your heart.
