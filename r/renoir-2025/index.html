Renoir (2025) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-21
668 reviews · 1,593 films logged · 181,283 words
Kids notice everything. Adults just think they don’t. Nostalgic Nescafé vibes. The warm, slightly dreamy look reminded me of that same Nescafé commercial feeling I got from Marlina the Murderer in Four Acts (2017) and Gohan (2026). Sometimes it even gives me In the Mood for Love (2000) vibes, especially in the way small gestures can carry more meaning than dialogue. There is something very simple about seeing boredom and curiosity through the eyes of a kid. Fuki is not trying to solve some grand mystery. She is bored. Curious. Watching television. Trying strange things. Listening. Looking. Slowly peeking into parts of the adult world she does not completely understand yet. She is playful, weird, impulsive, and sometimes surprisingly perceptive. Kids can tell. They might not understand the full context, but emotionally, they are much smarter than adults think. They can feel when something is wrong. When adults are lying. When someone is unhappy. When there is attraction in the room. When parents are fighting even if nobody explains anything to them. The director even builds a lot of Fuki’s perspective around simply watching. And Fuki is just standing there quietly absorbing all of it. The film works best for me when it simply lets a child watch adults and gradually realize that growing up does not automatically mean figuring life out. Verdict: Interesting Watched at SM North Cinema 3 during Japanese Film Festival 2026.
