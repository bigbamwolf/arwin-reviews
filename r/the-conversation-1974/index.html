The Conversation (1974) review

★★
Rated 2/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-13
629 reviews · 1,555 films logged · 164,190 words
Stanley saying “Let’s take a break” really resonated with what I was thinking while watching. What a stupid conversation. What the hell are they talking about? That feeling of irritation is intentional, and the film slowly makes you sit inside that frustration. At first, I honestly didn’t get why Harry was so obsessed with the recording. It felt trivial, almost pointless. But that’s where the film becomes effective. It traps the audience in the same loop as Harry, forcing us to listen again and again to a badly recorded, garbled audio, manipulating fragments just to make it barely understandable. You’re not just watching his process, you’re experiencing it. As the film progresses, the recorded conversation stops being just evidence. It becomes Harry’s conscience. He keeps hearing it, dreaming about it, replaying it in his head. By the time Harry realizes that he is now the one being bugged, his world completely flips upside down. The paranoia he once controlled turns inward. The irony is brutal. The man obsessed with surveillance becomes the victim of it. The film ends not with answers, but with isolation, and distrust.
