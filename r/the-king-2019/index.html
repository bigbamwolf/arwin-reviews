The King 2019

★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-13
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): What stands out to me visually is how the film embraces a raw historical atmosphere. The production design, language, and sound design all work together to place me inside that period. The muted colors, natural lighting, and shadow give the world a grounded and harsh texture. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ Timothée Chalamet already feels like he is channeling Paul Atreides here. The commanding presence is unmistakable. There is that quiet intensity in his eyes that slowly grows into authority. Lisan al Gaib energy! Watching him here almost feels like seeing an early version of the same kind of leader he would later portray in Dune. There is also a strong visual language that reminded me of Dune. The colors and lighting feel similar in my eyes. A lot of muted earth tones, natural light, and heavy shadows that give the world a grounded and historical texture. It does not look glossy or romanticized. It feels cold, and harsh. The language leans toward an older form of English but it never becomes distracting. It actually adds to the realism of the film and helps place the story firmly in its historical setting. The sound design is mostly atmospheric. Sometimes it becomes epic, especially during larger moments, but it never overwhelms the acting. The performances remain the focus. I also immediately recognized Ben Mendelsohn despite the different look. That voice is impossible to miss. The moment he spoke I instantly thought of Director Krennic from Star Wars. That same commanding villain tone. The politics and court drama honestly dragged too long for me. The pacing suffers because of it. There are stretches where the film slows down too much before the action returns. When the siege finally happens though, the visuals become very striking. The night attack looks great, especially with the flaming projectiles flying through the darkness. The scale of the battle is also impressive. The muddy terrain, the rain, the afternoon light, and the chaos of bodies struggling in the dirt all come together to create a very cinematic atmosphere. It feels brutal and grounded, like the battlefield itself smells of mud and death.
