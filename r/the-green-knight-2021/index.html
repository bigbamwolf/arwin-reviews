The Green Knight (2021) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-26
Adventure, Drama, Fantasy · Directed by David Lowery · 130 min
624 reviews · 1,550 films logged · 162,502 words
A24’s Lord of the Rings, where the hardest knight to defeat is the coward hiding inside yourself. Superb locations, landscapes, and cinematography. Beautifully shot. I love the journey. Even before I started unpacking what Gawain was going through, I was already absorbed by how mythic everything looked. This feels like A24’s The Lord of the Rings. But instead of giving me constant heroism, battles, and grandeur, it feels more interested in stripping down what being a hero actually means. It kind of reminds me of The Northman (2022) with its carefully composed images, earthy palettes, and almost ritualistic atmosphere. But The Green Knight feels more introspective to me. The journey is less about conquering something and more about Gawain slowly confronting himself. It also gives me The Legend of Ochi (2025) vibes because of its storybook fantasy aesthetic. The landscapes feel like illustrations brought to life, beautiful but also strange enough that I never feel completely safe inside them. I resonated with Gawain. In the court with the Green Knight, he wants to prove himself. I understand that impulse. There are moments when I also want to show that I am capable before I have fully considered the consequences or the weight of responsibility. Sometimes when the opportunity is already right in front of me, I just go along with it even if I am not fully prepared. There is probably a small person inside all of us who wants the glory first and worries about the consequences later. If I am wrong, then maybe I really have something off with myself. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you've seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ When he meets the mysterious woman, she questions why he needs a reward for helping her. That also hit me because I have a hard time believing anything in this world is freely given. I often see the world as transactional. Someone gives something, and eventually something is expected in return. Then there are the giants. I saw myself again in his desire for shortcuts. I also constantly look for ways to eliminate time between where I am and where I want to be. Sometimes I want the destination without enduring the hardship required to actually become the person capable of reaching it. That might be why this worked for me as a character study. I’ve always been drawn to films where circumstances slowly expose who a person really is underneath. Like Jay Kelly (2025), where the external journey reveals the person behind the image, or A House of Dynamite (2025), where pressure exposes how people actually behave when everything becomes real. Here, every encounter strips another layer off Gawain and reveals his ego, impatience, fear, entitlement, and need for self-preservation. Like him, I have moments of courage, but my courage is inconsistent. It wanes when things become difficult. When everything stops going my way, my instinct is usually to abandon ship. I almost always choose flight over fight. Sometimes I am enthusiastic about taking the long, grueling journey, but somewhere along the way, I start to backpedal when it takes too long and I can no longer see where I am heading. The green sash hit me the hardest. To me, it represents his desire to survive even if survival requires dishonesty. I have had my fair share of fake it till you make it moments too, pretending to be more confident, capable, or fearless than I actually feel just to convince other people to believe in me. I know. I consider myself a closeted coward at times. I wear this mask of a brave man because I have a family to support, and sometimes I feel like I cannot afford to show them my vulnerable side. Or maybe some of it comes from unhealed childhood wounds, this constant need to prove that I am worthy, capable, and strong even during moments when I don’t really feel worthy or strong at all. Even as an entrepreneur, I know I do not risk as much as I probably should. I play small. Then I wonder why I am still small and why the breakthrough never comes. Maybe I have been wearing my own green sash all along. Something I think protects me, but also keeps me leashed to my potential. I retreat toward peace of mind instead of growth. I want to become a certain kind of person, but sometimes I still fail to embody the characteristics that person would actually need. That is why Gawain worked for me. He is not the fearless knight I expected. He is someone who wants to become worthy before he has actually developed the courage required to be worthy. Worth a rewatch. It attacked me personally. Beautifully captured. Verdict: Absolute Cinema TL;DR: A24’s Lord of the Rings, where the hardest knight to defeat is the coward hiding inside yourself. Beautifully shot, with stunning landscapes and a mythic atmosphere, but what really got me was how much I resonated with Gawain. His desire for glory, tendency to take shortcuts, fear of failure, and instinct to retreat when things get difficult reminded me so much of myself. The green sash hit me the hardest because I realized I might have been wearing my own version of it all along, hiding behind a mask of courage while playing small and wondering why I haven't reached my potential. Worth a rewatch. This film attacked me personally.
