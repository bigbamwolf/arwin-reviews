Kill Bill: Vol. 1 2003

★★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-16
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. NON SPOILER THOUGHTS (SUMMARY): Kill Bill: Vol. 1 opens at full throttle and never pretends to slow down. Quentin Tarantino leans hard into precision, with fight choreography that is tightly synced to camera movement, framing, and sound design. Every sequence feels meticulously planned, turning violence into something rhythmic and almost dance like. Visually, the film blends kung fu influences with exaggerated physics and bold compositions, favoring spectacle over realism. The sound design is razor sharp, giving weight to every movement, while the music shifts wildly across genres, giving each chapter its own identity. It is loud, stylish, and unapologetically theatrical, a film that exists to flex rhythm, control, and cinematic confidence. Spoilers ahead. Read on if you’ve seen the film. From here on, plot details are discussed. Wow! Simula pa lang action na agad. Nanibago ako kay Quentin Tarantino. Ang galing ng fight choreography and coordination niya with camera movements and shot selection. Ramdam na ramdam na planned bawat frame. Lalo na yung The Bride vs Gogo fight. Ang ganda ng coordination. Choreography, camera movement, sound effects, lahat worked together. Pati ako napapailag habang nanonood. Ang ganda rin nung silhouette fight shot against the blue moonlit background. Very iconic. Some moments feel like John Wick before John Wick existed. One woman against every assassin. Visually, may halo siyang Kung Fu Hustle style. Wide compositions, exaggerated movement, playful physics, and stylized violence that leans into spectacle instead of realism. The final fight with O-Ren Ishii is pure cinema. Elegant, restrained, and beautifully shot. Kahit yung dialogue, ang linis at ang composed. Tapos yung ending pose ni The Bride parang last routine ng isang ballerina. Controlled and graceful. Parang sinasabi, that’s it, you can now clap. I watched this right after Stranger Things, so it feels like I am watching Maya Hawke twice in a different universe. Bad ass. Sobrang lakas ng appeal ni Uma Thurman as The Bride. One thing that really stood out is the sound design. Ang gaganda ng micro shots paired with very sharp, intentional sound effects. Every slash, every impact, may weight. Elite level sound design. The musical score is also great and very non conventional. He pulls from rock, funk, western themes, and Japanese pop. Sensory overload! Iba ibang genre per scene, parang bawat chapter may sariling musical identity. There’s a scene that reminded me of Boogie Nights, especially yung may band like energy. Nakakaindak yung rhythm ng music. The anime sequence pulled me out of the immersion. Personally, it broke the momentum for me. That’s really my only complaint. Yung gore dito hindi kadiri. OA siya by design. More theatrical kesa disturbing. Ang galing din ng make up, sobrang bloody. Overall, Kill Bill Vol. 1 is Tarantino flexing style and rhythm. The anime part didn’t work for me, but everything else is a masterclass in choreographed chaos and stylized revenge.
