She Rides Shotgun (2025) review

★★★★½
Rated 4.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-25
601 reviews · 1,527 films logged · 156,111 words
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Taron Egerton brings an intense, unstable energy that keeps a constant sense of danger on screen, while the film grounds itself as a father and daughter story about morality, protection, and survival in a corrupt world. It balances intimacy and violence well, with strong night photography, textured visuals, and smart staging that lets chaos exist around the characters without overwhelming them. Ana Sophia Heger is the emotional core and a real standout, delivering a performance that feels far beyond her age. While the first half drags and could have been tighter, the film ultimately works as a gritty crime thriller with surprising tenderness and emotional weight. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ From the start, Taron Egerton brings a very intense presence. There’s a clear James McAvoy energy from Split with his acting intensity. He carries a constant sense of danger, like someone barely holding himself together. At its core, this is still a father and daughter story. The emotional beats between them, especially their conversations about morality. It’s not preachy, but it constantly questions what kind of man a father needs to be when the world around them is corrupt. The film also doesn’t shy away from showing cops involved in drug business, with the force itself compromised and operating under drug money influence. Tonally and thematically, you can feel traces of Aftersun, Good Time, One Battle After Another, Sicario, and Breaking Bad with how it blends intimacy with violence and explores how crime seeps into personal lives. Performance-wise, Taron Egerton is excellent, but the real standout is Ana Sophia Heger as Polly. The future is bright for this kid. That ending is devastating. She’s dancing, full of joy on the surface, but her eyes carry a quiet sadness that says something is missing without her father. That emotional contradiction is hard to pull off, especially for a child actor, but she nails it! Pacing-wise, the first half feels boring and could have been shortened. The film takes too much time establishing ideas that are already clear, making the build up feel dragged rather than deliberate. Visually, the film shines in its night photography. The night drive sequences are beautifully shot, with tension baked into the framing and movement, especially during the car chases. The landscapes are also well used, supported by smart lens choices, controlled lighting, and a color grade that leans soft and intimate. The added film grain gives the movie texture. One of the strongest visual ideas is how the film stages action in layers. At the third act, you see Polly running as the clear subject in the foreground, while chaos unfolds behind her, gunfights, sieges, all happening at the same time. I can vividly remember something like this in the third act of Tenet with the forward moving army and the backward moving army. It’s a great way of showing how violence exists around her world even when she’s not directly participating in it. Overall, She Rides Shotgun works both as a gritty crime thriller and an intimate emotional drama. Stylish, tense, and surprisingly tender, with performances that elevate the material beyond genre expectations.
