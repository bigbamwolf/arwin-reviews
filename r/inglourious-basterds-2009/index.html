Inglourious Basterds 2009

★★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-10-15
The opening score sets the tone right away. Makaluma ang tunog, parang mga lumang films sa Pinas. May classic na slow grandeur, and ominous tone. Quiet terror talaga ang bread and butter ni Tarantino. The tension lives in the dialogue and how the camera moves to guide your subconscious. Ang ganda ng visual clue nung tumatakbo si Shoshanna palayo ng cabin, framed perfectly within the doorframe, parang boxed out na wala nang takas. Sabayan mo pa ng rising musical score with tenor vocals, tapos biglang papalagpasin lang pala siya ni Christopher Waltz. Grabe. The director really knows how to toy with his audience’s emotions. Pagpasok ni Brad Pitt as Aldo Raine, concise and clear agad. Parang COD Commander briefing the mission. Then Tarantino quickly shows his trademark gore, scalping German soldiers in all their bloody detail. The Bear Jew scene is masterful. The sound effects alone build the fear, the echoing footsteps, the slow tension before impact. You can feel the dread of the Nazis waiting for their punishment. Set pieces and wardrobe are superb, breathing life into that war torn period. The basement scene is another highlight, the use of dual point of focus adds layers to the tension, splitting our attention between calm conversation and imminent danger. Then the revelation, the soldier’s cover is blown, guns are drawn, and chaos explodes in seconds. Every element, color grading, lighting, camera movement, and lens choice, sync perfectly to build that cinematic rhythm Tarantino is known for. He also crafts strong female characters. Yung makeup scene ni Shoshanna sa Chapter 5 feels like a battle armor montage, fueled by vengeance. The soundtrack Cat People (Putting Out Fire) by David Bowie elevates it even more. I couldn’t help but laugh at Brad Pitt’s accent and bulldog like overbite, parang mimic ni Don Corleone, but it works. The whole movie feels like a deadly game of hiding something, everyone pretending not to know. Christopher Waltz steals the show. Enigmatic presence. One moment, charming and silly; the next, terrifying and calculated. Yung sound ng pag snap ng heels nung babaeng artista? Parang nail in the coffin. Reverse Cinderella moment. GG well played. By the end, the theater scene erupts into fiery chaos, parang Django Unchained meets The Wizard of Oz, with Shoshanna’s face projected on smoke like a haunting spirit. Oddly satisfying, like killing cockroaches in an infestation. Brutal but cleansing. Narratively, the chapter structure works beautifully. Each section sets up its characters and motivations, so by the time everything converges in the theater, it feels like a ticking time bomb. The payoff is both explosive and earned. And yes, this movie is worth rewatching. There are so many subtle hints, clues, and mirroring shots that make more sense when you already know what will happen. And the final line “You know something, Utivich? I think this just might be my masterpiece.” Tama si Tarantino. This is his masterpiece.
