Rocketman (2019) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-28
Biography, Drama, Music · Directed by Dexter Fletcher · 121 min
626 reviews · 1,552 films logged · 162,974 words
Bohemian Rhapsody meets Elvis, but much more interested in the man behind the music. Every actor disappeared into their role. Taron Egerton especially becomes Elton John instead of simply feeling like an actor impersonating a famous musician. This is about family acceptance, daddy abandonment, mommy resentment, longing for love and belonging, love for passion, sexuality, and manipulation. There is something sad about watching someone constantly surrounded by people yet still searching for the kind of love and acceptance he never really received growing up. It is also about getting all the fame, success, and attention you could ever want, yet still feeling completely alone. The bigger Elton becomes, the more isolated he somehow feels. It also has an element of Elvis (2022), where the managers treat their artists like products instead of people. They use them, control them, and keep pushing them to their limits until the artist can no longer take it mentally and physically. The person almost disappears behind the brand everyone is making money from. I also really enjoyed the music. I like how the songs are not just performances but are used to carry different emotional stages of Elton’s life, from the euphoric highs of success to the much darker moments underneath all that fame. It feels similar to Bohemian Rhapsody (2018), but for some reason, this had less impact on me. Both are musical biopics about larger than life artists struggling with fame, sexuality, relationships, and loneliness, but Bohemian Rhapsody gave me a stronger emotional payoff. And above all, it is about accepting yourself for who you truly are. Forgiving yourself. Loving yourself. After spending so much of his life wanting other people to love and accept him, one of the hardest things he eventually has to learn is how to give that same love to himself. I enjoyed the music, performances, and character development, but despite appreciating how everything came full circle, I never felt emotionally invested enough for the film to leave a lasting impression on me. Verdict: Watchable Watched on HBO Max. TL;DR: Bohemian Rhapsody (2018) meets Elvis (2022), but more interested in the man behind the music. Taron Egerton completely disappears into Elton John, and I love how the songs carry the emotional highs and lows of his life. It explores family rejection, loneliness, manipulation, and the struggle to love and accept yourself despite having all the fame and success in the world. I enjoyed the performances, music, and character development, but unlike Bohemian Rhapsody, I never felt emotionally invested enough for it to leave a lasting impression.
