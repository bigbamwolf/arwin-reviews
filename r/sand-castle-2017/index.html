Sand Castle 2017

★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-12-15
Sand Castle feels modern and clean even though the story takes place in 2003. The director leans heavily into long, continuous shots, which gives it a grounded and deliberate pace, but also makes the movie feel slow at times. The whole premise of fixing a water supply is honestly boring. Almost an hour in, there’s still barely any action, and even the characters seem to know it. Ocre straight up says the mission is a loser, and that line pretty much sums up the film’s energy. It reminds me of Breaking Bad in a way, slow, hot, dry, stuck in the desert, more about tension and waiting than movement. The third act looks great, especially the night scenes with the street lights, but everything feels a bit too perfect, almost staged, rather than raw or real. Still, the intensity ramps up quickly near the end, and that’s when the film finally grips you. What hits hardest is when things finally start to work, progress is made, and then a bomb explodes and wipes everything out. All that effort, wasted in an instant. By the time he’s sent home, the irony really sinks in. He doesn’t want to go home anymore. He wants to finish the job, but there’s no job left for him to finish.
