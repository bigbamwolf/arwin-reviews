Men (2022) review

★★
Rated 2/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-01
Drama, Fantasy, Horror · Directed by Alex Garland · 100 min
643 reviews · 1,569 films logged · 169,802 words
Alex Garland really likes making me question what the hell I just saw. Jessie Buckley is such a talented actress. But why is she here? Her grounded acting feels almost strange against how bizarre the movie becomes. She is giving a very real, emotionally readable performance inside a film that keeps becoming more surreal. At the same time, I also feel like the material itself does not fully deserve how good she is. The lighting and the overall vibe make everything feel weird and unsettling. The film looks beautiful at times, but there is always something slightly wrong with the atmosphere. Just like Alex Garland’s Annihilation (2018), the final sequence becomes incredibly surreal. Deeply symbolic and disturbing. Both films eventually reach a point where I know I am looking at something that is supposed to mean more than what is literally happening, but I do not fully connect with it either emotionally or intellectually. The ending worked as horror because it was disturbing and gross. That whole birthing sequence is the kind of imagery that makes me stare at the screen thinking, “What the fuck did I just watch?” My reading of it is basically: men birth men. One man gives birth to another, then another, like this endless cycle of men producing the same behavior over and over again. I can see the symbolism, but understanding the idea does not automatically make me feel connected to it. That is probably my biggest disconnect with the film. I was interested because it is A24 and Alex Garland, and I was curious about where all the weirdness was going, but once it got there, I admired the imagery more than I actually cared about what it was saying. I think the performances, cinematography, and disturbing body horror are much better than the storytelling itself. The payoff left me unsatisfied, and I never really connected with the characters enough to enjoy the film as a whole. Verdict: Weak
