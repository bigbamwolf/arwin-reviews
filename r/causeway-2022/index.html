Causeway 2022

★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-11-07
From the first scene pa lang, I instantly saw myself in Jennifer Lawrence’s character. Yung frustration ng katawan mo na hindi gumagana the way it used to. Yung simpleng paglalakad, pag akyat ng hagdan, at pagligo. Suddenly, they feel like battles. Same nung after ng surgery ko, when I couldn’t walk properly. You don’t just lose strength. You lose dignity. Jennifer is so different here. Vulnerable and fragile. No glamour, no hero shots, no Katniss, no Red Sparrow. Just a woman whose back is slightly curved, whose voice is always half broken, whose eyes are always somewhere else. She walks like someone carrying invisible weight: trauma, guilt, emptiness. She wears baggy shirts, boyish clothes, messy hair. And those blank stares into nowhere? That’s someone who’s alive, but not living. The way the film is shot makes you feel her isolation even more. Framing her with so much empty space makes her look small. Lost. The world is still moving, but she’s stuck. The slow push in when she starts recounting her trauma? The dialogue is quiet, pero the imagination does the rest. Walang flashback, walang dramatic music, but I saw everything. That’s how you know the writing is effective, you don’t need to see blood to feel the wound. You don't need IEDs to feel the shockwave. Then comes Brian Tyree Henry, someone who’s also broken, just hiding it better. He smiles, he jokes, he works, but every time the camera lingers on his eyes, you feel this silent kind of grief. They don’t heal each other but rather they just exist beside one another until the world feels less heavy. Walang solution, only survival. Causeway reasons that healing is sitting quietly beside someone who understands your silence. In the end, nothing spectacular happens. But that’s the point. Sometimes healing isn’t about becoming whole again, it’s about choosing to live even when you’re still in pieces.
