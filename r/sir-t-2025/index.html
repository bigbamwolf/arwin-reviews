Sirāt 2025

★★★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-03
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): The film starts like a search story but slowly turns into a meditation on grief, and by the end I felt less entertained and more emotionally drained, like it just sits beside me in silence instead of giving closure. The film moves like a trance through the desert, blending pulsing sound and vast landscapes into something beautiful yet quietly threatening. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The rave music keeps the film alive even without much dialogue. Sound design is top notch. Either rave or wind blowing, both absolutely and masterfully complement the beautiful imagery. I love the yellowish brownish desert aesthetic. The cinematography is impressive not just in daylight but also during dusk and dawn. It really feels like a beautiful journey about finding a lost daughter, and the characters are easy to like so I got attached fast. I could already feel danger when they took the treacherous canyon path. The moment Esteban woke up to a peaceful morning I knew that there's something wrong. Everything felt calm, the dog was healed, almost too good to be true. Then the tragedy hit suddenly. Ang sakit nung reaction ni Luis. Raw and real. I cried too. I really sympathize with fathers in films losing a child, especially as someone who lost two children. The minefield scene shocked me enough to sober their brain. Clever effects, the explosions looked real. From a search journey it suddenly became full survival mode and I found myself rooting for them harder. I was literally holding my breath. Anxiety inducing to the point I had to pause just to regain control of my senses. By the end, the film stops feeling like a rescue story and becomes something heavier. The desert was never just a place, it was grief itself, endless and indifferent. When the journey ends, nothing is truly resolved, like the film just sits beside you and lets you process instead of comforting you.
