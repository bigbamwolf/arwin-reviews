Highest 2 Lowest 2025

★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-12-29
At its core, the film quietly asks a difficult question: would you do the same sacrifice for someone who isn’t your family, the same way you would for your own blood? For a crime thriller, this film is surprisingly chill. The tone is lighter than expected, especially with how the score is used. Instead of going full tense or ominous, the director flirts with energetic Puerto Rican music even as the tension rises. That contrast keeps the film moving without feeling too heavy or oppressive. The director also plays with perspective through visuals. There’s a noticeable switch in color grading and LUTs depending on whose point of view you’re following. It adds to the sense of confusion and moral imbalance, making you feel how unstable the situation really is. Denzel is really good here. He convincingly portrays a conflicted founder businessman who is also a family man and a loyal friend. You can feel him constantly weighing responsibility, loyalty, and survival. Nothing feels exaggerated. He carries the quiet weight of someone trying to do right but trapped by consequences. This is very much a story about the consequences of a good deed. One decision, made with the right intention, slowly spirals into something messier and harder to control. Nice touch with the easter egg on the unit number A24. Simple, but satisfying to catch. And yeah, I really hated Yung Felon's character. Just a stupid piece of shit. Which I think means the film did its job there. I appreciated the ending resolution. In the end, it feels like he finds a way to have both worlds. Business and family don’t have to be mutually exclusive. By choosing to build a small family owned record label, he creates something grounded, personal, and honest. Not a perfect win, but a meaningful one. Quietly tense, morally uncomfortable, and more reflective than your usual crime thriller.
