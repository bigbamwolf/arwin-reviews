Send Help 2026

★★★
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-30
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): What starts with a rough and distracting CGI-heavy opening eventually settles into a tense survival thriller once the focus shifts to human behavior under pressure. The film becomes far more engaging when it strips away comfort, status, and authority, showing survival as an equalizer where power can flip instantly. Its strength lies in the psychological dynamic between the two leads, where trust, control, and desire quietly collide. While the visuals are uneven, with some scenes feeling artificial, moments of realism and danger do stand out. Overall, it’s less about survival mechanics and more about how power, dominance, and strategy emerge when systems collapse. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ The film opens with an unbelievable CGI plane crash. Hindi siya realistic, and it immediately took me out of the experience. Once the survival aspect kicks in, the film becomes more interesting. Survivalism is presented as the ultimate equalizer. When power and authority are stripped away, wag maging kupal, because the balance can shift very quickly. This is a story about how fragile dominance really is when resources are limited. There’s a strange comfort shown in the simplicity of survival. Being contented. Basic needs are provided. No overthinking about skin care, status, or appearances. Just food, shelter, and staying alive. That contrast works well agains the characters’ internal power struggles. The dynamic between the two leads is the real engine of the film. She delays the rescue partly to humble him, but it slowly becomes clear that she also wants him for herself. Power, desire, and control start to overlap, blurring moral lines. Visually, the film is inconsistent. Some shots clearly look like real locations, while others feel obviously shot on a studio set. However, the wave cinematography during the attempted raft escape stands out. That sequence looks very realistic and sells the danger effectively. The acting is solid. Both performances feel natural. Funny, scared, rageful. The emotional shifts feel earned, especially as desperation builds. By the third act, Linda becomes a full blown Wanda Maximoff, fully embracing control and manipulation. This film ultimately shows how intoxicating power can be. The story constantly pushes and pulls. Kala mo okay na silang dalawa, but it never really is. There’s always another layer of strategy underneath. In the end, it almost plays like a manifesto on how power is gained, maintained, and weaponized. Strategy and planning matter more than morality. Survival doesn’t just test the body, it exposes who people really are once there’s no system left to hide behind.
