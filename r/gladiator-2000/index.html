Gladiator (2000) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2025-12-19
607 reviews · 1,533 films logged · 157,261 words
The fire catapult hits feel real and dangerous. Ramdam mo yung weight and impact, not just spectacle. The opening war sequence is nicely done, parang Game of Thrones in scale and chaos. My only complaint is the use of long exposure and slow motion shots. It pulls me out of the moment instead of immersing me deeper. Ang ganda ng lighting and musical score during the scene where Commodus kills Caesar. The warmth and shadows make the moment feel intimate and disturbing at the same time. The escape from his supposed execution is excellent. The fight choreography, cinematography, color grading, and musical score all work together. From that moment, the film clearly shifts into another level. I like how controlled the gore is. Just enough to serve the story. The first gladiator fight is pure chaos. The camera movement is shaky and aggressive, but that feels intentional. Parang inilalagay tayo sa ulo ng mga gladiator, confused, overwhelmed, just trying to survive. Commodus gives off strong Joffrey Baratheon energy. A spoiled, insecure man child who inherited power and doesn’t deserve it. Joaquin Phoenix plays him as someone dangerous not because of strength, but because of ego and emotional instability. For a 2000 film, the set extensions and CGI still hold up. It works because there are real sets. The production design grounds everything, allowing the visual effects to blend naturally instead of feeling artificial. The costume design is superb. It quietly tells you who has power, who is enslaved. The dialogue and writing sometimes sound like poetry. Not in an overbearing way, but in short lines that feel memorable and weighty. The first Colosseum fight is a spectacle. The scale, the crowd, the choreography, everything works. I also like how color and lighting shift depending on the emotion. Warm tones are used during intimate moments, especially scenes involving family, love, or passion. It humanizes Maximus beyond revenge. Commodus’ death and Maximus finally joining his family in the afterlife feel satisfying. Not just because justice is served, but because the story closes emotionally. He finally finds peace.
