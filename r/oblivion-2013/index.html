Oblivion (2013) review

★★★★
Rated 4/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-08
Action, Adventure, Sci-Fi · Directed by Joseph Kosinski · 124 min
610 reviews · 1,536 films logged · 157,710 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): On rewatch, this clicks for me in a way it didn’t the first time. The opening mission briefing, visuals, and score immediately pull me in, and I start appreciating how tangible and location driven the world feels. The blend of sleek sci fi design and grounded cinematography makes the film feel like a showcase of Joseph Kosinski’s visual control. What once felt slow now reads as deliberate, and the unfolding mystery keeps me engaged. It plays like a polished sci-fi spectacle where craft and atmosphere carry as much weight as the story. Give Joseph Kosinski a Star Wars film!!! ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ This is my second time watching this. The first time I saw it, I got bored. But this time, the opening minutes with Tom Cruise explaining his mission hooked me immediately. I think before I was expecting a different kind of film or I simply did not understand Joseph Kosinski’s cinema language yet. Watching this on HBO MAX felt insane. The visuals and score instantly pulled me in. There are many shots that feel like they were captured on real locations because everything looks tangible and real! The terrain photography alone already shows an early glimpse of Kosinski’s capability, like a prototype of what he later perfected in Top Gun Maverick. The film has excellent CGI when it needs it, especially with the ship, drones, and background extensions. But what impressed me is how some drone shots look physically present, almost like practical builds were used. That blend gives a realistic touch to the world. The futuristic aesthetic reminds me of Tom Cruise’s work in Minority Report, but this feels cleaner, more refined, and visually disciplined. Even the explosions feel satisfying. I honestly get the sense that Kosinski studied Michael Bay haha! The scale and visual ambition make this feel like a director’s portfolio piece. I can easily imagine this as Kosinski’s pitch reel and director's treatment for a Star Wars film. The design language, barren landscapes, and sleek tech give me Rogue One and sequel trilogy vibes. It has that polished galaxy far far away identity that feels cinematic. Story wise, the reveal structure is what surprised me the most on this rewatch. Morgan Freeman’s appearance lands with strong presence, and seeing Nikolaj Coster Waldau adds another layer of intrigue. The second act entrance of the larger drone inside the apartment is genuinely menacing. The way the curtain drapes around it builds tension in such a simple visual way. I feel real fear in that moment. It communicates threat for real! Then the clone twist hits, and I genuinely react with shock. I do not remember that reveal from my first viewing, so it feels fresh and destabilizing. Suddenly the mission, identity, and purpose of the character shift. It reframes everything we know about the film. The third act explodes with momentum. It becomes nonstop energy, electric and destructive without feeling messy. The reason it works is the seamless integration of practical effects and digital work. Nothing feels artificial. The action carries physical impact, so even the explosions feel immersive. I guessed correctly that the authority controlling them was artificial intelligence rather than a real government or any space governing body, but that prediction does not weaken the experience. Instead, it reinforces the film’s theme about control, illusion, and manufactured purpose. Overall, Oblivion feels like a turning point piece in Kosinski’s career. On rewatch, I appreciate how controlled the visuals are, how deliberate the pacing is (though could be shorter in my opinion), and how the story unfolds through reveals. What bored me before now feels epic. This time, I see the craftsmanship clearly, and I am fully engaged.
