Dr. Strangelove or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bomb 1964

★★★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-03-28
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): What unsettles me most is how calmly it presents something so catastrophic, as if total destruction can be triggered by routine, ego, and blind protocol. The absurd logic and world ending stakes is where the dark comedy really works for me. Visually, I think the black and white cinematography is beautiful, and the script is sharp enough to carry both tension and satire at the same time. The tone is what makes it memorable for me, even if one major character choice does not fully land. I end up admiring the film more for its irony, staging, and terrifying logic than for every individual performance choice. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I can really feel the confusion and fear the moment they received the Wing Attack Plan R. That sudden shift from normal daily operations to irreversible action is terrifying. It shows how fragile the system is, especially when one low ranking official goes haywire. Add to that someone believing in conspiracy theories about the enemy. It becomes clear how dangerous it is to rely on rigid protocols in something as catastrophic as nuclear war. There is no room for error, yet the film shows how easy it is for things to go wrong. It feels like a House of Dynamite film but flipped. Instead of the US waiting for a nuclear strike, they are the ones who initiated it. It becomes a story about self destruction rather than defense. The dark comedy comes from the absurdity of logic. They are discussing something extremely serious, yet the way they talk about it makes it feel almost casual. There are also moments that feel like satire like the chewing gum bit. The way products and trivial things are inserted into such a serious situation adds another layer of absurd humor. Then you have characters like General Ripper whose obsession with preserving his “essence” reminded me of Lockjaw's "semon demon" logic in One Battlw After Another. Visually, the black and white cinematography is beautiful. The contrast enhances the mood and gives the film a timeless quality. The script is also sharp, carrying both tension and humor seamlessly. The aerial scenes are great. The way the planes are shot makes them feel powerful and real. The ground combat scenes are also well done. Kubrick really knows how to stage these sequences as I also love Full Metal Jacket. The score adds another interesting layer. It uses music in a way that contrasts with the events on screen. Instead of making everything feel heavier, it sometimes gives a strange sense of irony. The music almost softens the tension while the situation itself becomes more dangerous, which makes the overall tone even more unsettling. I would have loved to give this a 5 star, but Dr. Strangelove himself felt so out of place for me. Everything else worked, but his scenes did not land the same way. He is just too weird, and for me he does not add as much impact to the storytelling compared to the other characters. Honestly, I do not even feel like the film’s title should be named after him. It would have made more sense if it focused on General Ripper or even the President. His paraplegic condition, the uncontrollable twitching, the Nazi salute, and the accent all felt cringe to me. It really pulled me out of the experience.
