The Last Temptation of Christ (1988) review

★★★★★
Rated 5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-09
Drama · Directed by Martin Scorsese · 164 min
618 reviews · 1,544 films logged · 159,192 words
I don’t know why, but I felt really unmotivated to watch this. I had to push myself and approach it purely from a cinematic standpoint. Here, Jesus is portrayed by Willem Dafoe. This version of Jesus is not the usual all knowing, Superman type. He is vulnerable, doubtful, and unsure. It feels like he is slowly being shaped, not instantly destined to be the Messiah from birth. This is a very different kind of humanization. Too human, and too raw. After the desert scene, Willem almost looks like a more familiar European Jesus. Before that, I honestly had doubts if he would fit the role visually. His acting is strong though. I can feel the emotional disturbance churning inside him. Confusion, fear, anger, and guilt are all present. This is an early film, so the cinematography feels very different from modern biblical films. Lighting is mostly bright and high key, with minimal shadow contrast. A lot of static shots and clear frontal framing. It gives a sense of flatness, with minimal lens variation. Some desert scenes are backlit, which works well. During night scenes, there’s an old school technique that I don’t see much anymore. It reminds me of Filipino films like Pedro Penduko, Magic Temple, and Spirit Warriors. They use a cool backlight or edge light to simulate moonlight, giving characters a crisp blue outline. When red backlight is used, it reminds me of Indiana Jones. The film mainly explores the “what ifs.” What if Jesus had doubts? What if he was unsure of his role? What if he was surprised by the powers given to him by the Father? What if he wasn’t the Messiah? What if he had a family and children? Willem Dafoe brings a new way for me to read the Bible. Nakakatakot din siya at times, probably because I associate him with Norman Osborn. His rage toward the Pharisees feels intense and unfiltered. Comparing portrayals, Willem shows confusion, anger, and guilt, while Jonathan Roumie’s Jesus shows more compassion, warmth, and controlled rage. Here, other disciples are mostly sidelined, with Judas Iscariot getting the spotlight. It almost feels like multiple disciples were combined into Judas, the Zealot aspect, the doubting aspect, everything. The practical sets are strong, especially the blood effects. The production design is commendable. The prayer scene at the Garden of Gethsemane is more emotionally effective here than in The Passion of the Christ. I actually prefer this film over The Passion of the Christ. This one feels more complete as a film. Passion left me unsatisfied cinematically. Bitin yung acting, cinematography, and payoff. Parang “yun na yun?” What really works here is the humanization of Jesus in the first and second acts. Because of that, you develop more empathy going into the third act. I don’t think this film is blasphemous from start to finish. But my opinion shifted during the crucifixion sequence, when the angel tells Jesus he is not the sacrifice and not the Messiah. The wedding with Mary Magdalene made me laugh at the absurdity. The idea that the afterlife is still on Earth, the sex scene, and them having children felt shocking. I understand why this angered many people. I immediately thought that this might have inspired Dan Brown’s The Da Vinci Code. The third act almost feels like a parody at times. Natatawa na lang ako. Jesus cheating, suggesting mass hallucination, it felt absurd. The film touches on the conspiracy that the resurrection was not real and was created to give people hope. It becomes a matter of perception of who people need Jesus to be. Then comes the twist. The “guardian angel” is revealed to be the last temptation. Jesus was lied to. He truly was the Messiah, and by choosing the illusion, salvation was lost. That realization hits hard. When the film cuts back to Jesus and dolly zooms in on the cross and he begs to be crucified again, followed by the shout “It is accomplished,” ang galing! Parang Christopher Nolan level twist in terms of emotional reversal! I think this film is misunderstood. It played with my emotions. I was blindsided, just like Jesus was by Satan. I think that’s why it works. It reflects daily life. We get distracted from our purpose. We are tempted constantly. We think we know better than God. We resist His plans. We don’t fight temptation enough. And when we finally admit that and ask for forgiveness and finally trusts Him, that’s when we truly worship and glorify Him.
