Promising Young Woman (2020) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-10
598 reviews · 1,524 films logged · 155,326 words
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): I experience this as a revenge film driven more by psychology and social commentary rather than physical violence. Emerald Fennell directs with a sharp sense of control, letting tone, music, and performance carry Cassie’s inner rage. It feels unsettling, calculated, and personal, using revenge as a lens to examine accountability and the culture that protects harm. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ I watched this just to observe how Emerald Fennell direct a movie since I'll be watching Wuthering Heights tomorrow. Cassie is living a double life. By day she looks ordinary, but at night she becomes a vigilante targeting men who prey on women. I feel like she is savoring every moment of those confrontations. Every interaction feels calculated, controlled, and deeply personal. The score is great and feels like a direct reflection of what is going on inside her head. It swings between triumphant, revolted, and vengeful tones, almost narrating her emotional state. The music doesn’t just sit in the background. It pushes the tension and makes her actions feel both empowering and unsettling. I get I Spit on Your Grave vibes here, but this film approaches revenge in a more psychological and social way. It is less about physical brutality and more about exposing a culture that protects abusers and silences victims. Her death feels like a mirror to Nina’s fate. Both are casualties of a system where men dismiss their actions, erase evidence, and frame everything as an accident. It reinforces how easily truth gets buried when people choose to forget their past actions over accountability. The scene involving Ryan and the detective feels like a commentary on a world largely shaped by men. I read it as a reflection of how quickly blame gets shifted onto victims instead of confronting the real harm done. That moment made the film’s anger feel grounded in something familiar. The planned delivery of evidence is a smart narrative move and a strong way to close the story. Even after Cassie's death, she maintains control. Her plan ensures that what happened cannot be ignored or rewritten, giving the film a sense of justice. Overall, I experience this film as a revenge story layered with social critique.
