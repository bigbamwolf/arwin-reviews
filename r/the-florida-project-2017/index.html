The Florida Project (2017) review

★★★½
Rated 3.5/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-27
Drama · Directed by Sean Baker · 111 min
676 reviews · 1,600 films logged · 187,473 words
Childhood is basically a plotless movie you somehow remember forever. I watched this after Renoir (2025) because I wanted to experience that childlike sense of wonder again. It is kind of nostalgic watching this. It gives me the feeling of being a child again, when there is no real direction, nothing to worry about, no accountability, just having fun, playing around with friends, teasing adults, begging for money to buy ice cream, wandering around the motel and nearby places, and basically being rowdy. I just went along with it. There is something really familiar about having an entire day ahead of you with no schedule, no responsibilities, and no real idea where the day is going. As a kid, I do not really need a plan. I just find my friends and somehow the whole day happens. I love the mother and daughter's carefree relationship. They are not constantly telling each other how important their relationship is. They just hang out, argue, laugh, tease people, roam around. Moonee is loud, mischievous, fearless, and sometimes bratty, but she also feels like a product of the environment around her. A lot of what she sees as normal probably comes from simply growing up surrounded by it. That is also why the adult problems work for me. Poverty and instability are clearly there, but the film does not constantly stop and announce that I should feel sad. Sometimes it is just a conversation happening in the background while the kids are thinking about something completely different. From their perspective, this is simply life. I kind of feel like the film is plotless, but weirdly, that became part of why I enjoyed it. Childhood itself can feel plotless. You do not know where your life is going. You just wake up, find something to do, ride along with each day, and eventually one day you start getting your footing. The scene where they are teasing the nude lady by the pool is probably the moment that captured that feeling best for me. It is stupid, spontaneous, a little inappropriate, and exactly the kind of thing kids would find hilarious simply because an adult told them not to look. Visually, I love the cinematography, colors, set design, and especially the sunsets. The purple motel, pastel buildings, bright signs, blue skies, and warm Florida light make the whole place look almost like a child’s playground. That contrast is interesting because the environment looks so colorful even when the adults living inside it are struggling. The sunsets especially give the film this warm, almost memory like feeling. It looks like the kind of summer afternoon I remember years later even if nothing particularly important happened. And Bobby feels like the "lolo" (granfather in English) of the whole motel. He is constantly frustrated, correcting people, fixing things, and dealing with chaos, but underneath all that, I can still feel that he genuinely cares about the kids and the people living there. Verdict: Interesting TL;DR: Watching this felt like temporarily escaping adulthood and remembering what it was like when an entire day could be spent doing absolutely nothing important. I love how naturally the kids interact with each other and how their little adventures become entertaining despite the seemingly directionless storytelling. What makes it even more interesting is how the film presents poverty and difficult adult realities through the innocence of children who are simply enjoying life as they know it. The vibrant colors, beautiful sunsets, and warm cinematography make even the most ordinary moments feel like childhood memories. I also love Willem Dafoe's presence and the genuine warmth he brings to the community. I didn't need a compelling plot to enjoy this one. I was perfectly happy spending time in their little world.
