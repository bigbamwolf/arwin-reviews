Smile 2 (2024) review

★★★
Rated 3/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-10-31
Horror, Mystery, Thriller · Directed by Parker Finn · 127 min
649 reviews · 1,575 films logged · 173,102 words
Smile 2 feels like one long “Did that actually happen?” Cool continuous shot prologue. It is an effective hook that immediately throws me into the action. The cinematography and acting style take a completely different approach here. There are more continuous shots, more panning, and much more camera movement. The camera feels more aggressive compared with the first Smile (2022), which fits how much more chaotic Skye’s world becomes. This feels bigger than the first film in terms of production scale, locations, crowds, the whole pop star world, and the set pieces. But bigger does not necessarily mean better for me. I still prefer the first one when it comes to the level of intrigue and acting because that one feels much more grounded and believable. Naomi Scott has a different kind of intensity here. Her performance is more frantic, and more expressive, which fits the character, but I still prefer the more grounded acting style of the first film. One commonality is that they both have mommy issues. Here, Skye’s mother is also her manager, so there is this uncomfortable feeling that she is being treated both as a daughter and as a product that needs to keep performing. The psychological aspect is still there and works pretty well, mixed with body horror at times. The broken leg was nasty. The entity toys with Skye much more intensely here, but the fake outs eventually became repetitive for me. After a while, I started expecting that whatever I was watching might suddenly be revealed as another hallucination or false reality, which made some of the tricks less effective. It has that same mindfuck feeling I get from Limitless (2011), Vanilla Sky (2001), Fight Club (1999), and The Father (2020), where time disappears, memories become unreliable, and reality keeps slipping away. I am basically piecing the movie together alongside Skye while constantly questioning which moments actually happened. I admire how much more ambitious this sequel is technically, especially with the camera work and scale. But I still prefer the first film because its grounded approach made the horror and mystery feel more believable to me. Verdict: Watchable Watched on Netflix. TL;DR: Smile 2 feels like one long “Did that actually happen?” I love the continuous shots, aggressive camera movements, and bigger production scale, while Naomi Scott delivers a much more frantic performance. The psychological horror still works, especially when reality becomes so unreliable that I keep questioning what actually happened. But the constant fake outs eventually became repetitive, and I started expecting every scene to be another hallucination. I admire the technical ambition, but I still prefer the first Smile (2022) because its grounded acting and storytelling made the horror and mystery more believable.
