I Saw the TV Glow (2024) review

★
Rated 1/5
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-11-13
Drama, Horror, Mystery · Directed by Jane Schoenbrun · 100 min
655 reviews · 1,581 films logged · 175,968 words
So weird. I don't get it. At first, I thought it was about too much media consumption. But it seems more about gender identity, repression, and being true to oneself. I can appreciate that interpretation, but the surreal storytelling didn't really resonate with me. I like the lighting and color grading. Heavy A24 DNA. I love the pink and blue neon lighting, especially how the television glow illuminates the characters' faces. The contrast between the ordinary suburban setting and the strange world of The Pink Opaque is also visually interesting. It reminds me of the pacing of Moonlight (2016) and its intimacy with the characters. But Moonlight feels more grounded in reality, while this one relies heavily on surreal imagery and symbolism. Both have that slow, intimate approach, although I find the storytelling here harder to connect with. I don't like Maddy's monotonous delivery. I also don't find her character interesting. Her long conversations with Owen didn't really draw me in, even when she was explaining the mystery surrounding The Pink Opaque. But I like the acting of the sick, older Owen. Very convincing portrayal of someone who looks like he's dying. I especially like how his breathing becomes increasingly labored and how physically exhausted he looks. His breakdown during the birthday party is probably one of the most uncomfortable moments in the film for me. I love the lighting and color grading, especially the pink and blue neon lighting. I also appreciate older Owen's performance. But that's about it for me. I was genuinely curious about the mystery, yet the storytelling never resonated with me. I didn't feel emotionally connected to the characters, and the ending didn't give me the payoff I was hoping for. I can appreciate what the film is trying to say, but I just didn't enjoy how it was told. Veridict: Failed TL;DR: I can appreciate films that experiment with unconventional storytelling, but this one was too abstract for me to connect with. I was intrigued by the mystery and wanted to understand where everything was going, but the experience became more frustrating than engaging. The visuals are easily its strongest quality, and there is one performance that genuinely impressed me. Unfortunately, the characters, pacing, and lack of emotional payoff left me feeling detached from the whole thing. I understand why its message might resonate deeply with other people, but personally, I admired certain parts of it more than I enjoyed the film itself.
