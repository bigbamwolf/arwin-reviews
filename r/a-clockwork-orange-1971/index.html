A Clockwork Orange 1971

★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-02-02
Non spoiler thoughts (summary): The film throws you into a world of delinquent energy and never lets you settle. The elegant classical score clashing against violent, unruly behavior is what makes it so unsettling. Everything feels out of sync on purpose. The slang heavy dialogue, exaggerated accents, and out of time production design all add to the alienation. It looks futuristic and dated at the same time, like a past vision of tomorrow. Performances are manic and theatrical, especially the lead, whose presence carries the film even when it feels exhausting. This is not about growth or redemption, but about control, identity, and what happens when choice is taken away. ⚠️ Spoilers ahead. From here on, plot details are discussed. Read on if you’ve seen the film. If not, save this post and come back after. ⚠️ What a bunch of delinquents. Feels like watching Netflix’s Steve (2025) again. The group has those fiendish, clownish grins. Lunatic energy from start to finish. The musical score is beautiful, almost elegant, and that’s what makes it so disturbing. It feels completely mismatched with what’s happening on screen. The music evokes class, order, and sophistication, while the characters are chaotic, rowdy, and violent. That contrast makes it hard to watch, but ironically more engaging. The dialogue is loaded with words and slang from England that I’ve never heard before. It adds to the alienation. Ang OA ng accent ni Mr. Deltoid, nakakairita. The sets look retro but also strangely out of time. It’s futuristic, yet dated. Parang past imagining the future. The wardrobe gives Scooby-Doo era vibes, bright colors, costumes that feel playful and cartoonish, which again clashes with the brutality of the story. I get Shutter Island vibes in the middle portion, especially with the idea of “curing” a twisted mind. Alex goes from delighting in violence to being physically repulsed by it. After he’s cured and released, I also felt some Shawshank Redemption energy. He struggles to readjust to the outside world, but unlike Andy, there’s no real redemption here. He’s just displaced, stripped of his nature, and rejected by the same society that tried to fix him. Alex’s facial expressions and acting feel like a mashup of Evan Peters, Joaquin Phoenix, Mark Hamill, and Hayden Christensen. There’s manic charm, theatrical madness, vulnerability, and menace all mixed together. Overall, this film is weird. It's confusing, and disturbing. No character development as the government made sure of it.
