Challengers 2024

★★½
Reviewed by Arwin Edward Bagaslao · 2026-01-18
This review contains spoilers after the non spoiler section. Non spoiler thoughts (summary): Challengers is less a traditional sports film and more an intense psychological relationship drama that uses tennis as its battlefield. The film is driven by obsession, control, and identity, with competition bleeding into intimacy and vice versa. Zendaya delivers her most mature performance to date, projecting confidence and intimidation. The energy between the central trio is volatile and uncomfortable, turning ambition and desire into something combative. While the non linear structure and aggressive electronic score may feel intrusive at times, the film commits fully to emotional chaos over clean sports spectacle. What begins as competition gradually reveals itself as something far messier, where winning, love, and self worth become inseparable. Spoilers ahead. Read on if you’ve seen the film. From here on, plot details are discussed. Napapikit talaga ako dun sa injury. Grabe yung visual at sound effect, ramdam mo yung sakit in an instant. I feel like Tashi is genuinely passionate about tennis and can’t stop talking about it. To the point that she starts to feel invasive and bossy. Tennis isn’t just a sport for her, it’s her entire identity, and she projects that intensity onto everyone around her. This is the most mature version of Zendaya I’ve seen. Very controlled, confident, and intimidating without needing to overact. The non linear storytelling is quite distracting for me. Instead of adding tension, it sometimes pulls me out of the moment. The musical score also doesn’t always work. The electronic music keeps playing even during dialogue heavy or intimate scenes, and it often feels out of place, almost overpowering the emotions instead of supporting them. Art just wants to win Tashi’s approval every single time. Meanwhile, Tashi only really cares about winning tennis itself. Patrick, on the other hand, wants to win against Art both on the court and emotionally, by reclaiming Tashi’s affection. The love triangle is completely messed up. While Tashi and Patrick are out there cheating, Art is with their daughter. I feel sorry for Art. I really hate cheating movies, and this one doesn’t try to justify it either. The turning point for me was when Patrick signaled that he slept with Tashi again. That moment reignited something in Art. He needed that betrayal to feel alive in the game again, to want to win not just the match, but Tashi herself, against his own friend. From that point on, tennis and their relationship became the same battlefield. When Art and Tashi finally reconnect emotionally, they go somewhere beautiful together. And when Tashi screams again, it feels raw and unfiltered, like something that hasn’t been released in a long time. It’s not just about winning anymore, it’s about obsession, control, and the damage they willingly inflict on each other. I expected a sports drama with immersive camera shots, but instead I was given a deeply rooted love triangle centered on the sport, which pays off in the end.
